The fair way to split a Christmas party bill is to charge everyone the same for the set menu, let people pay for their own drinks (or the drinks package if they chose it), spread the service charge in proportion, and take each person's deposit off their share. Collect deposits before you pay the restaurant, and agree up front what happens if someone drops out.

Festive bookings come with more rules than a normal dinner: deposits, pre-order deadlines, minimum spends and set menus. The organiser often ends up holding hundreds of pounds of other people's money. Here's how to keep that simple.

## What makes Christmas bookings different

Most restaurants change how they take group bookings from late November. Ask about each of these when you book:

- **Deposit:** usually a set amount per head, taken when you book and knocked off the final bill. Usually non-refundable after a cut-off date.
- **Set or festive menu:** a fixed price per head, often two or three courses.
- **Pre-order:** everyone's choices sent a week or two ahead. Once sent, the restaurant may charge for those meals whether people turn up or not.
- **Drinks package:** a fixed price for, say, a glass of fizz on arrival and half a bottle of wine each.
- **Service charge:** commonly 12.5% for large groups. Check whether it's added to the drinks package too.
- **Minimum spend:** common for private rooms. If you fall short, someone pays the difference.

## Step 1: send the invite with the numbers

People can only say yes fairly if they know what they're agreeing to. Something like:

> 🎄 Christmas dinner, Fri 12 Dec, 7.30pm at The Green Man. Festive menu £42 a head, optional drinks package £30 (fizz on arrival and half a bottle of wine each), 12.5% service on top. They want a £10 deposit each by 21 Nov. Deposits are non-refundable after that date. Pre-orders due 1 Dec. Expect about £50 to £85 each depending on drinks. Send me £10 to confirm: [link]

## Step 2: collect deposits before you pay them

Only pay the restaurant once you've collected everyone's deposit. Count people as coming when they've paid, not when they've said "yes!!" in the chat. It's the single best way to stop the organiser being left out of pocket by no-shows.

Keep a simple list: name, deposit paid, menu choice, drinks package yes or no. That list is also your pre-order and, later, your split.

## Step 3: split the bill on the night

Here's a worked example. Fourteen friends, festive menu at £42 a head, eight take the £30 drinks package, the other six buy their own drinks from the bar (£84 between them). Everyone paid a £10 deposit, and service is 12.5%.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Festive menu, 14 × £42 | £588.00 |
| Drinks package, 8 × £30 | £240.00 |
| Other drinks (6 people) | £84.00 |
| Subtotal | £912.00 |
| Service 12.5% | £114.00 |
| **Total** | **£1,026.00** |
| Less deposits, 14 × £10 | £140.00 |
| **Still to pay** | **£886.00** |

Each person's share is their menu plus their drinks, times 1.125 for service, minus their £10 deposit:

- **With the drinks package:** (£42 + £30) × 1.125 = £81.00, minus £10 = **£71.00**
- **Sam, who had two soft drinks (£8):** (£42 + £8) × 1.125 = £56.25, minus £10 = **£46.25**
- **Lou, who had four glasses of wine (£28):** (£42 + £28) × 1.125 = £78.75, minus £10 = **£68.75**

Split it evenly instead and everyone pays £63.29 after deposits, so Sam pays £17.04 more than her own share, mostly for other people's wine. If your group has a few non-drinkers, see [how to split the bill when some people don't drink](/blog/split-bill-when-some-dont-drink/).

## Should the service charge go on the drinks package?

If the restaurant adds service to the whole bill, it's fair to spread it across everyone in proportion to what they had, which is what the example above does. Splitting the service charge equally would mean the non-drinkers tip on the wine. Our guide to [splitting service charge fairly](/blog/split-service-charge-fairly/) has the details.

## What happens when someone drops out?

Agree this in the invite, then stick to it. A fair default:

| When they drop out | What they pay |
|---|---|
| Before the deposit deadline | Nothing. Refund their deposit. |
| After the deposit, before pre-orders | Their deposit, unless someone takes their place |
| After pre-orders are sent | Whatever the restaurant charges for their meal |
| On the night, no word | Same as above. Ask for it kindly, the next day. |

If someone's ill on the day, plenty of groups quietly cover a deposit. That's a kind choice for the group to make together, not a cost the organiser should absorb alone. ## Work Christmas parties

Work dos have a few extra wrinkles.

**If the company is paying:** an employer can give staff an annual party tax-free if it's open to all employees and costs £150 or less per head, according to HMRC. That's the employer's concern, not yours, but it's why some companies set a per-head budget. If the budget covers the meal and a drink, anything extra at the bar is usually on you.

**If the company pays a set amount:** say the company gives £25 a head and the festive menu is £42. Everyone pays £17 towards the meal plus their own drinks. Take the contribution off each person's share, the same way as a deposit.

**If the boss puts a card behind the bar:** ask the bar how much is on it and tell people when it's gone. It's the gap between "the tab's run out" and "someone noticed" that causes the awkward bills.

**Pre-orders by spreadsheet:** a shared sheet with name, starter, main, dessert and dietary needs saves the organiser chasing 30 people on Slack.

## Friends' Christmas dinners and Secret Santa meals

With friends, the same method works. Two extra points:

- **Secret Santa:** set a gift limit (£10 to £20 is common) and keep it separate from the meal. Don't let gifts get mixed into the bill.
- **The host who cooked:** if it's a dinner at someone's house, the fair approach is to split the shopping receipt between everyone, or have each person bring a dish or a bottle so the host isn't left paying for everything.

## FAQ

### How do you split a Christmas party bill fairly?

Charge everyone the same for the set menu, have each person pay for their own drinks or the package, spread service in proportion, and take off each person's deposit.

### Do you get your Christmas party deposit back if you cancel?

It depends on the restaurant's terms, which are usually non-refundable after a cut-off date. Within a group, agree in the invite whether a dropout gets their deposit back. More in [group booking deposits and no-show fees](/blog/group-booking-deposit-split/).

### Is a drinks package worth it for a Christmas party?

Only if you'd drink at least that much at bar prices. For non-drinkers or light drinkers, paying as you go is almost always cheaper. Let people choose rather than putting the whole table on it.

### Who pays for the work Christmas party?

Often the employer pays some or all of it. HMRC treats an annual staff party open to everyone as tax-free for employees up to £150 a head. Anything beyond the company's budget is usually paid by each person.
