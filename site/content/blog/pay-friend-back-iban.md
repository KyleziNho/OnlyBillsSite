To pay a friend back by IBAN, start a new transfer in your banking app and enter their IBAN, the exact name on their account and the amount. Within the euro area, an instant transfer arrives within 10 seconds and can't cost more than a standard one, and since October 2025 your bank checks the name against the account before you confirm.

Here's how the name check works, how to share your own IBAN safely, and what to do if you're paying from a UK bank.

## How to pay a friend by IBAN, step by step

1. Ask your friend for three things: their IBAN, the name on the account exactly as the bank has it, and the amount.
2. In your banking app, start a new transfer (sometimes called a SEPA transfer or a transfer to a new payee).
3. Paste the IBAN rather than typing it. The check digits catch almost any single typo, but they can't tell you it's the wrong person's IBAN.
4. Enter the name. Your bank runs the name check here.
5. Enter the amount and a short reference ("Saturday dinner").
6. Choose instant if your bank offers it as a separate option.

## How fast is a SEPA transfer now?

Since 9 October 2025, banks in the euro area must be able to send SEPA Instant transfers, which put the money in your friend's account within 10 seconds, at any time, weekends included. They've had to be able to receive them since January 2025. An instant transfer can't cost more than the same bank's standard euro transfer.

A standard SEPA transfer still exists and usually arrives by the next working day. Banks outside the euro area, and some payment and e-money firms, have until 2027 to offer instant euro payments.

## What is Verification of Payee?

It's the name check. Since 9 October 2025, every bank in the euro area must offer it for free on both standard and instant euro transfers. When you enter your friend's IBAN and name, their bank compares the name with the account and your bank shows one of four results:

| Result | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Match | The name fits the account | Pay |
| Close match | Nearly right, for example a missing middle name. Your bank shows the actual name | Check it's your friend, then correct the name |
| No match | The name doesn't fit | Stop and ask your friend |
| Check not possible | The other bank couldn't answer | Pay only if you're sure of the details |

You can still pay after a warning. Your bank must tell you what that means for your protection if the money goes to the wrong account, so read it, and check with your friend before ignoring it.

**If you're the one being paid,** send your name exactly as your bank has it. "Sam Okafor" may come back as a close match if the account says "Samuel Okafor".

## Is it safe to share your IBAN?

Generally, yes. An IBAN identifies your account so people can pay into it. It doesn't let anyone log in or send money out. That needs your banking app, passwords and security codes, which you never share.

The one thing someone could try with your IBAN is a fraudulent SEPA Direct Debit. If that happens, you can claim a refund from your bank: within 8 weeks for any direct debit with no questions asked, and up to 13 months if you never authorised it. Check your statements now and then.

Sensible habits:

- Share it with people you know, in a private message.
- Don't post it in a big group chat that includes people you've only just met.
- If someone asks you to "verify" by paying a small amount to a new IBAN, treat it as a scam.

More on this in [is it safe to share your bank details](/blog/is-it-safe-to-share-bank-details/) (written for UK accounts, but the advice carries over).

## Can banking apps scan an IBAN from a QR code?

Some can. The European Payments Council's QR format, called GiroCode in Germany, holds the IBAN, name, amount and reference. Many banking apps in countries including Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Belgium and Finland can scan it and fill in a transfer. Support depends on your bank, so a typed or pasted IBAN is still the fallback that always works.

## Can you pay a euro IBAN from a UK bank?

Usually, yes. UK banks can send euros to a European IBAN, as an international payment or through SEPA, but fees and the exchange rate vary a lot, and some European banks charge to receive payments from UK accounts. For a dinner share, it's often cheaper to pay from a Revolut or Wise euro balance, or to agree to pay in pounds. See [splitting a bill abroad](/blog/split-bill-abroad-currency/) for which rate to use.

## FAQ

### Is it safe to give a friend my IBAN?
Generally, yes. An IBAN lets people pay you; it doesn't let them log in or send money out. The one risk is a fraudulent direct debit, and for those you can claim a refund from your bank. Don't post it in large group chats with people you don't know.

### How long does a SEPA transfer take?
A SEPA Instant transfer arrives within 10 seconds, any day and any time. A standard SEPA transfer usually arrives by the next working day.

### What happens if the name doesn't match?
Your bank shows a warning. For a close match it shows the actual name on the account. You can still pay, but if the money goes to the wrong person you may be less protected, so check with your friend first.

### Can I pay an IBAN from a UK bank?
Usually, yes, as an international or euro payment. Fees and exchange rates vary by bank and can be high, so a Revolut or Wise account is often cheaper for small amounts.
