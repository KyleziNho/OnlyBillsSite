A normal bank transfer between UK accounts goes by Faster Payments. Pay.UK says funds are "usually available almost immediately, although they can sometimes take up to two hours." The service runs day and night, 365 days a year, so you can send one at 11pm on a Sunday.

So if you're paying a friend back, the transfer itself is rarely the slow part. The slow part is everything before it: working out what you owe, getting their details and typing them in. Here's how long each way of paying takes, why some transfers get held, and which method to use when. Figures are as of November 2026.

## How long does a Faster Payment take?

Usually almost immediately, sometimes up to two hours (Pay.UK). Pay.UK also notes some payments take longer, especially outside normal working hours.

How much you can send depends on your bank. The Faster Payments system allows single payments up to £1 million, but Pay.UK says banks set their own limits, which can be lower and can vary by payment method and account type. Some banks also cap how much you can send in a day. For a £25 dinner share you'll never get near them.

## Why is my bank transfer taking so long?

The most common reasons:

- **It's a new payee.** Your bank runs a name check (Confirmation of Payee) the first time you pay someone. If the name doesn't match, it asks you to check before sending.
- **Your bank is checking it.** Under FCA guidance on rules introduced in October 2024, a bank with reasonable grounds to suspect fraud can hold a payment for up to four business days. That's rare for a small payment to a friend, but it happens.
- **It's late at night or a bank holiday.** Pay.UK says some payments take longer outside normal working hours.
- **A typo.** A wrong digit in the sort code or account number can fail the payment or send it to the wrong account, which is far slower to fix.

If it's been more than two hours and your app shows the payment as sent, ask your friend to check again, then contact your bank with the payment details. Worried it went to the wrong person? See [is it safe to share your bank details](/blog/is-it-safe-to-share-bank-details/) for how name checks and scam refunds work.

## What's the fastest way to pay a friend back?

It depends on what you've got in front of you.

| Situation | Fastest way | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Still at the table, small group | Split it at the till, one card each | Done before you leave. Works best with a few people |
| They've sent a Monzo, Revolut or Starling link | Pay the link by card or Apple Pay | No sort code to type, no new payee |
| They've sent a PayPal link and you use PayPal | Pay in PayPal | You're already logged in |
| You already have them saved as a payee | Bank transfer | A few taps, usually almost immediate |
| They bank with NatWest or Lloyds | Ask for their request link | You pay from your own bank on a web page |
| It's a big amount, like a holiday share | Bank transfer | Card payments into payment links are capped, often around £250 |

**On the link limits:** Starling says its users can receive up to £250 a day through Settle Up by UK debit card. Revolut says card payments into a payment link are typically capped at £250 a week. Bank transfers to their sort code and account number don't count towards those. Which links exist, and what each needs from the person paying, is in [how to pay a friend back without their bank details](/blog/pay-friend-without-bank-details-uk/).

### A worked example

Five friends, a £142.50 bill, Mo pays on his card.

- **Bank transfer:** four people ask Mo for his details, add him as a new payee, wait for the name check and send. Each transfer is quick, but someone always says "I'll do it when I'm home".
- **Payment link:** Mo drops his Monzo link in the group with everyone's amount. Each friend taps it and pays by card, Apple Pay or bank transfer, without a Monzo account. Nobody has to save Mo as a payee.

The money moves at about the same speed either way. The link wins because there's less to do, so people do it before they get home.

## Can you send money to a friend with Apple Pay in the UK?

Not directly. Apple's person-to-person payments (Apple Cash) are only available in the US: Apple's support page says the 50 United States, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico.

In the UK, Apple Pay works as a way to *pay* a link. When a friend sends a Revolut or Starling link, you can pay it by card or Apple Pay. A Monzo link takes card, Apple Pay or bank transfer. So Apple Pay often is the fastest way to pay a friend back, as long as they send a link first.

## Is splitting the card at the till faster?

For two or three people, often yes. You're done before the coats go on. For a big table it's slower: a 2021 comparethemarket.com survey of 2,000 diners found groups waited an average of 6 minutes 45 seconds to settle the bill. ## FAQ

### How long does a bank transfer take on a weekend?
Usually almost immediately, as on a weekday. Faster Payments runs day and night, 365 days a year, though Pay.UK says some payments take longer outside normal working hours.

### Why hasn't my Faster Payment arrived?
Most arrive almost immediately, but Pay.UK says it can take up to two hours. Longer delays usually mean a name check didn't match, a typo in the details, or your bank holding the payment for checks. Banks can hold a payment for up to four business days if they suspect fraud.

### What's the maximum I can send by Faster Payments?
The system allows up to £1 million per payment, but your bank sets its own limit, which is often lower. Check your banking app.

### Can I use Apple Pay to send money to friends in the UK?
Not person to person. Apple Cash is US only. You can use Apple Pay to pay a friend's Monzo, Revolut or Starling payment link.
