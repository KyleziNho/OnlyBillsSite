The simplest fair way to split a group holiday is to log every shared cost in one expense app as you go, then settle up once at the end with the fewest possible payments. Use a kitty for small daily spending if your group likes cash or a shared card, and pay for your own extras separately. Whoever has a card with no foreign transaction fees should pay for the big things.

Below are the three main methods, a worked example of settling up, the apps that do the maths, and the card fees that can quietly add about 3% to everything you spend abroad.

## The three ways to split holiday costs

| Method | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitty | Everyone puts in the same amount at the start (cash, or a shared card or Pot). Shared costs come out of it | Short trips, groups who spend about the same, daily stuff like coffees and taxis | The people who drink less subsidise the ones who drink more. Leftovers and top ups need tracking |
| Pay as you go | Whoever is at the till pays, and everyone pays them back that day | Small groups, trips with lots of separate meals | Lots of little transfers, and someone always "forgets" |
| One person pays, settle at the end | People pay as things come up, you log it all, and settle once at the end | Big costs (accommodation, car hire, activities), bigger groups | Needs an app or spreadsheet, and someone can end up hundreds of pounds out of pocket for a while |

Most groups end up mixing them: accommodation and car hire logged in an app, a kitty for groceries and coffees, and big dinners split by what each person had.

## Method 1: the kitty

A kitty is the oldest system there is: four of you put £100 each into an envelope or a shared Pot, and shared costs come out of it. When it runs low, everyone tops up the same amount. Whatever's left at the end gets split equally.

**Make it work:**

- Agree what the kitty covers. Groceries, fuel, taxis and coffees, yes. Someone's duty free, no.
- Keep receipts, or at least note each spend in the group chat.
- Don't use the kitty for big dinners if your group's spending varies a lot. That's where the non-drinkers end up paying for the cocktails.

Several bank apps have a version of this. Monzo's **Running Splits** let you add people and expenses for a trip and settle up when you're ready, and Monzo says friends can join as guests from a link even if they don't bank with Monzo. Revolut's **Group Bills** is a shared expense tracker where people without Revolut can join without signing up.

## Method 2: pay as you go

Whoever pays the bill gets paid back the same day. It works for a long weekend with three or four people. Past that, it becomes a stream of £6.40 transfers. If you go this way, settle every evening while everyone remembers who had what.

## Method 3: one person pays, settle at the end

This is what expense apps are built for. Everyone pays for things as they come up, logs them, and at the end the app works out who owes whom with the fewest payments.

### A worked example: a week in Spain for four

| Who paid | What for | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Ana | Villa | £1,200 |
| Ben | Car hire and fuel | £280 |
| Cara | Supermarket shops | £160 |
| Dan | Nothing yet | £0 |
| **Total** | | **£1,640** |

Split equally, everyone's share is £410.

| Person | Paid | Share | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ana | £1,200 | £410 | Owed £790 |
| Ben | £280 | £410 | Owes £130 |
| Cara | £160 | £410 | Owes £250 |
| Dan | £0 | £410 | Owes £410 |

Three payments settle the whole trip: Dan pays Ana £410, Cara pays Ana £250, Ben pays Ana £130. Not twelve.

## Splitting the big group dinners

An equal split works for groceries and the villa. It's less fair at the restaurant on the last night, when one person had the lobster and a bottle of wine and another had a salad and water. For those meals, split by what each person had (the [split the bill calculator](/tools/split-bill-calculator/) does this by item), then add each person's total to your trip app.

If your trip is in the UK (a cottage in Cornwall, a weekend in Edinburgh, a stag or hen do in Newcastle), OnlyBills can handle each restaurant bill: scan the receipt, friends tick what they had on their own phones, and service charge is spread fairly. It's built for UK restaurant and pub bills, so for meals abroad, log them in your trip app. If you're weighing up the two kinds of app, see [OnlyBills vs Splitwise](/vs/splitwise/). More on stag and hen weekends in [how to split costs on a stag or hen do](/blog/split-costs-stag-hen-do/).

## Foreign card fees: decide who pays for what

Here's the cost most groups forget. Many high street debit cards charge a fee every time you spend in another currency. Barclays, for example, charges a 2.99% non-sterling transaction fee on debit card payments, cash withdrawals and refunds abroad.

:::stat 2.99% | non-sterling transaction fee on a Barclays debit card abroad: about £30 on every £1,000 spent | Barclays, 2026

Some cards don't charge it:

- **Starling** says it charges no fees for card payments abroad and uses the Mastercard exchange rate with nothing added. Cash withdrawals abroad are free from Starling, though the ATM itself may charge.
- **Monzo** says you can pay in any currency fee free, at Mastercard's rate.
- **Revolut** charges extra on currency exchange above a fair usage limit on its Standard plan (£1,000 a month, then 1%), and its help pages describe an extra markup on exchanges made at the weekend.

**The practical move:** if one person in the group has a fee free card, let them pay for the villa, the car and the big dinners, and everyone pays them back in pounds. On £1,640 of shared costs, that saves about £49 compared with a 2.99% card.

### Always pay in the local currency

When a card machine or cash machine abroad asks whether you want to pay in pounds or the local currency, choose the local currency. Paying in pounds lets the shop or ATM set the exchange rate, which is usually worse. Starling and Monzo both give this advice.

## A holiday money checklist

1. Agree the method before you go: kitty, app, or both.
2. Agree what's shared and what isn't (drinks? souvenirs? the jet ski?).
3. Set up the trip in an app and add everyone before day one.
4. Let whoever has a fee free card pay for the big stuff.
5. Always pay in the local currency.
6. Split big dinners by what each person had.
7. Settle up within a few days of getting home. If it drags, see [what to do when someone won't pay their share](/blog/someone-wont-pay-their-share/).

## FAQ

### What's the fairest way to split holiday costs?
Split shared costs like accommodation and car hire equally, pay for your own extras, and split big meals by what each person had. Log everything in one app and settle up once at the end with the fewest payments.

### Is a kitty a good idea on holiday?
For small, similar daily costs like groceries and taxis, yes. For meals where people order very differently, a kitty means light eaters and non-drinkers subsidise everyone else.

### What's the best app for splitting holiday costs?
Splitwise, Tricount, Splid and Settle Up all keep a running balance across a trip and handle multiple currencies. Monzo Running Splits and Revolut Group Bills work inside those banks' apps, and friends at other banks can join.

### Should I pay in pounds or local currency abroad?
Local currency. If you choose pounds, the shop or ATM converts at its own rate, which usually costs you more.
