Across most of Latin America, friends pay each other back by bank transfer to a short identifier. In Argentina it's an **alias**, in Mexico a **CLABE**, in Colombia a **Bre-B key**, in Peru a phone number for **Yape or Plin**, and in Chile a block of **account details** with a RUT. You copy the identifier, paste it into your banking app and type the amount. Brazil is the exception: a Pix Copia e Cola code can carry the amount for you ([how that works](/blog/split-bill-pix/)).

If you live in the region, are spending a few months in Mexico City or Buenos Aires, or just got handed one of these at dinner, here's what each one is and what to do if you don't have a local account.

## At a glance

| Country | What locals share | How the friend pays | Works without a local account? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Argentina | Alias (or 22-digit CBU/CVU) | Paste alias in any bank or wallet, type amount | No |
| Mexico | 18-digit CLABE and name | Add as a payee, send by SPEI, type amount | No |
| Colombia | Bre-B key (phone, ID, email or @key) | Choose Bre-B, paste key, type amount | No |
| Peru | Mobile number | Send by Yape or Plin, type amount | No |
| Chile | Name, RUT, bank, account type, number, email | Add as a payee, transfer | No |

The last column is the catch for visitors, so it gets its own section below.

## Argentina: the alias

An alias is a memorable name linked to a bank account (CBU) or a digital wallet account (CVU). The Argentine government's guide says it's 6 to 20 characters of letters, numbers, full stops and hyphens, unique across the whole financial system, and can be changed once a day and up to 10 times a year. Any bank or wallet can send to any alias, and transfers between people arrive straight away.

So "pasame tu alias" (send me your alias) is how a group settles up. You'll often see aliases like three random words joined by dots, which is what banks assign by default.

## Mexico: the CLABE, and soon a phone number

A CLABE is an 18-digit interbank account number. Every Mexican bank can send to one through SPEI, the central bank's transfer system, which runs around the clock. Many apps also accept a debit card number, but the CLABE is the one that always works.

The first payment to a new CLABE means adding a payee, and some banks hold or cap the first transfer to a new payee. After that it's quick.

This is changing. Banxico has told banks to let customers receive transfers to a 10-digit mobile number linked to their account, with a deadline before 14 December 2026. The CLABE keeps working as before. DiMo, the phone-number scheme some banks already offered, had about 11 million linked phones in September 2026.

## Colombia: Bre-B keys

Bre-B is the central bank's instant payment system, live since late 2025. You receive money to a **llave** (key): your mobile number, your ID number, your email or an alphanumeric key starting with @. Any connected bank or wallet can pay any key and the money arrives straight away. The Banco de la República says that after one year, in October 2026, more than 36 million people had registered over 116 million keys. The cap per payment is 1,000 UVB, about 11 million pesos, and there's a proposal to raise it.

If you'd rather not share your phone or ID number with a group chat, an @ key is the private option.

## Peru: Yape or Plin

Peruvians pay friends by phone number. Yape belongs to BCP, and Plin sits inside the apps of BBVA, Interbank, Scotiabank and others. Since 2023 they've been able to send to each other by phone number at no extra cost, under interoperability rules pushed by the central bank (BCRP), and many bank apps can send to a phone number too. Each app sets its own daily caps, so check yours before paying for a whole group.

## Chile: account details

Chile has no alias or key system for friends. People send a block of details:

> Name: Sofía Rojas
> RUT: 12.345.678-9
> Bank: BancoEstado
> Account type: CuentaRUT
> Number: 12345678
> Email: sofia@example.cl

The RUT is the national ID number, which banks use to identify the account holder. Transfers between Chilean banks usually arrive straight away, but most banks limit how much you can send to a new payee for the first few hours.

## If you don't have a local account

All five systems need an account with a bank or wallet in that country. Visitors usually can't open one quickly, so:

- **Pay your share at the table.** Ask the restaurant to take your part on your card before your friend pays the rest. In most of the region this is a normal request.
- **Use cash.** Still fine between friends everywhere on this list.
- **An international transfer to their account.** Services such as Wise can pay out to a CLABE in Mexico and to bank accounts in several other countries in the region. Check the fee and the exchange rate first.
- **A payment app you both have**, such as PayPal, if your friend uses it.

If you're the one who paid and your local friends want to pay you, the same problem applies the other way round: they can't send to a UK or US account with an alias or a Bre-B key. Settle it in cash or let them pay their share at the table.

## A worked example with tip

Four friends have dinner in Mexico City. Food and drink come to $1,640 MXN and they leave a 15% tip ($246), so the total is $1,886. Sofi pays.

| Person | Ordered | 15% tip | Owes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sofi (paid) | $420.00 | $63.00 | $483.00 |
| Diego | $510.00 | $76.50 | $586.50 |
| Vale | $300.00 | $45.00 | $345.00 |
| Pablo | $410.00 | $61.50 | $471.50 |

An even split is $471.50 each, which would cost Vale $126.50 more than she had. Splitting the tip in proportion keeps it fair.

Tipping is voluntary across all five countries. In Chile and Colombia the bill usually shows a suggested 10% you can ask to remove; in Mexico, Argentina and Peru it's usually not included and you leave it on top.

## Send the identifier and the amount separately

On a phone, copying part of a message is fiddly. If you send "My CLABE is [18 digits], it's $586.50", your friend has to select just the number. Send the identifier on its own, then the amount, so each one copies cleanly.

## FAQ

### What is a CLABE?
A CLABE is Mexico's 18-digit interbank account number. Any Mexican bank can send money to it by SPEI, at any time of day. You give it to friends along with your name as it appears at the bank.

### What is an alias in Argentina?
An alias is a short, memorable name for a bank (CBU) or wallet (CVU) account, 6 to 20 characters of letters, numbers, full stops and hyphens. Any Argentine bank or wallet can transfer to it, and you can change it once a day.

### Can a tourist use Yape, Bre-B or a CLABE transfer?
Not without a local bank or wallet account in that country. Visitors usually pay their share at the table by card, pay in cash, or use an international transfer service that pays out to local accounts, after checking its fees and rate.

### Why not paste the amount together with the CLABE or alias?
Because your friend has to copy just part of the text, and it's easy to pick up an extra space or digit on a phone. Send the identifier and the amount separately so each one pastes cleanly.

### Do you tip on top of the bill in Latin America?
Tips are voluntary. In Chile and Colombia the bill usually suggests 10% and you can ask to remove it. In Mexico, Argentina and Peru, tips are usually left on top, typically around 10%, and up to 15% in Mexico.
