To split a bill equally on iPhone, open Calculator, type the total and divide by the number of people. To split it by what each person had, use Math Notes (in Calculator or Notes) to add up each person's items, or use an app that reads the receipt for you. Then send each person their amount with a payment link from your bank, because Apple's own way of sending money between iPhones isn't available in the UK.

Here's each method, with the exact steps and a real bill to try it on.

## The bill we'll use

Four friends at an Italian place. The receipt says:

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Margherita (Jo) | £12.50 |
| Carbonara (Kit) | £15.00 |
| Risotto (Lou) | £16.50 |
| Lasagne (Max) | £14.00 |
| Garlic bread (shared by all four) | £6.00 |
| Bottle of red (Jo, Kit, Lou) | £27.00 |
| **Subtotal** | **£91.00** |
| Service 12.5% | £11.38 |
| **Total** | **£102.38** |

An equal split is £25.60 each (£25.595, rounded up). But Max didn't drink. Split by what each person had, he owes £17.44. He'd be overpaying by £8.16.

## Method 1: Calculator (equal split)

Fine when everyone had roughly the same.

1. Open **Calculator**.
2. Type the total: 102.38.
3. Tap ÷, type 4, tap =.
4. You get 25.595. Round to £25.60 each.

**The catch:** rounding means the shares don't always add up. Four lots of £25.60 is £102.40, 2p over. Not a big deal, but it's why someone always ends up a few pence out. If you just want the number, the free [split the bill calculator](/tools/split-bill-calculator/) does equal and itemised splits in your browser.

## Method 2: Math Notes (split by what each person had)

Since iOS 18, Calculator has **Math Notes**, which also works in the Notes app. You write sums as text, and the answer appears as soon as you type an equals sign. The useful part: you can name values and reuse them, and if you change one, everything that uses it updates.

Open Calculator, switch to Math Notes, and type this:

```
service = 1.125
bread = 6 / 4
wine = 27 / 3
jo = (12.50 + bread + wine) × service =
kit = (15.00 + bread + wine) × service =
lou = (16.50 + bread + wine) × service =
max = (14.00 + bread) × service =
```

Math Notes fills in the answers:

| Person | Their items | Shared items | Before service | Math Notes shows | Rounded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jo | £12.50 | £1.50 bread + £9.00 wine | £23.00 | 25.875 | £25.88 |
| Kit | £15.00 | £1.50 bread + £9.00 wine | £25.50 | 28.6875 | £28.69 |
| Lou | £16.50 | £1.50 bread + £9.00 wine | £27.00 | 30.375 | £30.38 |
| Max | £14.00 | £1.50 bread | £15.50 | 17.4375 | £17.44 |
| **Total** | | | **£91.00** | **102.375** | **£102.39** |

Notice the total: £102.39, against a bill of £102.38. Each share rounded up by a fraction of a penny, and together they overshoot by 1p. Someone (usually the person who paid) knocks a penny off their own share. [How to split service charge and tips fairly](/blog/split-service-charge-fairly/) explains where that stray penny comes from. It works, but you need to check the total every time, and a single mistyped price throws the whole thing out.

:::note
Name variables with letters only (Math Notes needs Latin characters), and declare a value before you use it, or you'll get an error.
:::

## Method 3: Live Text (copy prices from the receipt)

Typing prices from a long receipt is where mistakes creep in. Live Text lets you copy them from a photo instead. It needs an iPhone XS or later.

1. Take a photo of the receipt.
2. Open it in **Photos** and tap the **Detect Text** button (or touch and hold a price).
3. Drag the handles to select the lines you want, then tap **Copy**.
4. Paste into Math Notes or Notes and build your sums around them.

Live Text copies the text; it doesn't know whose item is whose. You still do the assigning.

## Method 4: an app that splits the receipt

If you split bills often, a dedicated app saves the maths and the penny arguments. The main options on iPhone:

- **Splitwise:** great for running balances with housemates or on a trip. Since October 2026 its free Itemize feature lets one person photograph the receipt and friends claim their items from a QR code.
- **Tab:** an older app that scans the receipt and lets people tap what they had, but each person needs Tab to join.
- **OnlyBills:** made for restaurant bills. More on it below.

We compare them all, with UK prices, in [Splitwise alternatives: the best bill splitting apps](/blog/splitwise-alternatives/).

### Splitting the same bill in OnlyBills

The host [scans the receipt](/how-to/scan-a-receipt/) and OnlyBills reads every line. You check the total matches, then share a live table: friends scan a QR code or open a link and tick what they had on their own phone. They don't need to download anything; it opens on the web or as an App Clip.

The garlic bread splits between the four who shared it and the wine between the three who drank it. Service is spread in proportion. The four shares come to exactly £102.38: no penny over, no one doing sums. The rounding is handled so the shares always add up to the bill.

## Can you send money with Apple Pay in the UK?

No. Apple's support page says sending and receiving money with Apple Pay and the Apple Cash card are available only in the US, and you have to be a US resident aged 18 or over. You can pay in shops with Apple Pay in the UK, but you can't send your friend £25 through Messages the way people do in America.

So in the UK, people pay each other back by:

- **A payment link from your bank:** Monzo, Revolut and Starling all have one, and friends at other banks can usually pay it by card. See [how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling](/blog/split-bill-monzo-revolut-starling/).
- **Bank transfer:** send your sort code and account number and they pay from their banking app.
- **PayPal:** if everyone uses it.

On iPhone, the easiest thing is to drop the link straight into your group chat in Messages or WhatsApp with each person's amount. Here Jo paid, so Jo is the one who takes the penny off her own share.

> Dinner's sorted. Kit £28.69, Lou £30.38, Max £17.44 (Max, no wine for you). Pay me here: [link]

## Which method should you use?

| Situation | Best method |
|---|---|
| Everyone had about the same | Calculator, equal split |
| Two or three people, a few items | Math Notes |
| Long receipt, you want to avoid typos | Live Text plus Math Notes |
| Big group, shared dishes, service charge | An app that reads the receipt |
| Costs over a whole trip or a shared house | A balance app like Splitwise |

## FAQ

### Does iPhone have a built-in bill splitter?
Not a dedicated one. Calculator can divide a total, and Math Notes in Calculator or Notes can add up each person's items using named values. For itemised splits with service charge, an app does it with less room for error.

### Can I use Apple Cash in the UK?
No. Apple says sending and receiving money with Apple Pay and Apple Cash is available only in the US. In the UK, use a payment link from your bank, a bank transfer or PayPal.

### How do I split a bill with a tip on iPhone?
Multiply each person's items by 1 plus the tip rate. For 12.5% service that's × 1.125. In Math Notes, set service = 1.125 once and use it in each person's line.

### How do I copy prices from a receipt on iPhone?
Take a photo, open it in Photos, tap Detect Text or touch and hold the price, then tap Copy. Live Text needs an iPhone XS or later.
