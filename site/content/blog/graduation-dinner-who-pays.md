At a UK graduation meal, the graduate's parents or family usually pay, and the graduate doesn't. The meal is part of the celebration, so it's treated like a gift. When two families share the table (say, the graduate's parents and their partner's), the simplest fair options are for each family to pay for its own people, or to split the bill 50/50 between families, with the graduate's meal shared by both.

What goes wrong is rarely the money. It's that two sets of parents each assume the other is paying, or both reach for the card at once. Agree it before the day and the meal stays about the graduate.

:::stats
- £766 | average cost of a graduation per person, all items | Confused.com and OnePoll, 2026
- £81 | average spent on meals out for a graduation | Confused.com and OnePoll, 2026
:::

Those figures come from a Confused.com survey of 1,586 UK adults who had been to a graduation, carried out by OnePoll in June 2026. They're self-reported averages, but they show that the meal is one of the biggest single costs of the day.

## Who usually pays for a graduation dinner?

It depends on who organised it and who's at the table.

| Who's there | Who organised it | Who usually pays |
|---|---|---|
| Graduate and their parents | Parents | Parents |
| Graduate, parents, grandparents, siblings | Parents | Parents, sometimes with grandparents chipping in |
| Graduate, both their parents' households | One household | The household that invited, or each pays its own |
| Graduate and their partner's family too | Either side | Each family pays its own, or 50/50 |
| Graduate and course friends | Friends | Everyone pays their own |

If you're a grandparent, aunt or family friend who's been invited, it's kind to offer to pay for yourself. Many hosts will say no, but the offer is welcome.

## Should the graduate pay for their own graduation meal?

No, not at a family meal. The graduate is the guest of honour, and most are on a student budget or just starting work. If they want to say thank you, there are easier ways than paying for the table:

- buy a round of coffees or a dessert to share
- treat their parents to a meal later, from a first pay cheque
- write a card. It costs little and parents tend to keep them.

The exception is a dinner the graduate organises with friends. When everyone at the table has just graduated, everyone pays their own way.

## How do you split a graduation dinner between two families?

Pick one of these before you book, and say which one in the group chat.

1. **Each family pays for its own people.** Fairest when the families are different sizes. The graduate's meal is split between both families.
2. **Split the bill 50/50 between families.** Simplest when the families are about the same size and ordering is similar.
3. **One family hosts.** Generous and quick. The other family can offer to cover the drinks, or host next time.

Whichever you pick, spread the service charge in proportion to what each family had. Our guide to [splitting service charge fairly](/blog/split-service-charge-fairly/) shows how.

## Worked example: two families, one graduate

Maya is graduating. At lunch afterwards are her mum, her dad and her gran (one family, three people), and her partner Sam with Sam's mum and dad (the other family, three people). They share a £35 bottle of fizz for a toast, and the restaurant adds 12.5% service.

| | Food and drink | Share of fizz (£5 each) | Subtotal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maya's family: mum £38, dad £44, gran £26 | £108.00 | £15.00 | £123.00 |
| Sam's family: Sam £34, mum £36, dad £42 | £112.00 | £15.00 | £127.00 |
| Maya | £40.00 | £5.00 | £45.00 |
| **Subtotal** | | | **£295.00** |
| Service 12.5% | | | £36.88 |
| **Total** | | | **£331.88** |

**Option 1: each family pays for its own, and both cover Maya.** Add 12.5% to each subtotal and round at the end: Maya's family £138.375, Sam's family £142.875, Maya £50.625. Maya's share splits two ways, about £25.31 each, so:

- Maya's family pays **£163.69**
- Sam's family pays **£168.19**

**Option 2: 50/50.** Each family pays **£165.94**.

Here each family's share moves by only £2.25, because the families are the same size and ordered similarly, so 50/50 is fine. If Sam's family had brought two more people, option 1 would matter a lot more. You can test your own numbers in the [split the bill calculator](/tools/split-bill-calculator/).

## What if the graduate's parents are separated?

Ask the graduate first. It's their day, and they'll know what will feel comfortable. Common setups that work:

- **One table, each household pays its own.** Nobody is anyone's guest, so nobody owes anyone.
- **Two meals.** Lunch with one parent, dinner with the other. Common when ceremony day is long anyway.
- **One parent hosts the meal, the other hosts something else,** like drinks after or the hotel breakfast.

Whatever you choose, sort the bill privately beforehand. The table is no place to negotiate it.

## Booking tips for graduation day

- **Book as soon as you know the ceremony time.** Most UK universities hold summer ceremonies (usually June and July) and winter ones (roughly November to January). Restaurants near the ceremony venue fill up on those days.
- **Plan for the meal being bigger than the ceremony.** Many universities cap guest tickets. Heriot-Watt, for example, caps them at two per graduate, while Sussex starts at four for summer and ten for winter. The meal is often where the wider family joins in.
- **Lunch can beat dinner.** After a morning ceremony, a long lunch is often cheaper than dinner and fits round train times.
- **Ask about a set menu** for groups of eight or more. It makes the bill easier and the kitchen quicker.
- **Check for a deposit and service charge** when you book, so neither is a surprise. Here's [how group deposits work](/blog/group-booking-deposit-split/).
- **Decide who's paying on the card** before you sit down. One card, then settle up after, is fastest.

## Messages to copy

### Parents to the wider family

> We've booked lunch at the Italian place for 1pm after Maya's ceremony on 16 July. It's our treat, so please don't bring your wallets, just yourselves.

### One family to the other

> Really looking forward to the 16th. Shall we each pay for our own lot and split Maya's lunch between us? Happy to do 50/50 if that's easier for you.

### A grandparent or relative offering

> Thank you for including me. I'd like to pay for my own meal, please let me know my share.

### Settling up after

> Lovely day, thank you all. I put it on my card: £331.88 with service. Your family's share is £168.19, including half of Maya's. Bank details or a link to follow.

## FAQ

### Who pays for a graduation dinner in the UK?
Usually the graduate's parents or family, as a gift. If two families are at the table, each family often pays for its own people, or they split the bill 50/50.

### Should the graduate pay for their parents' meal?
Not at a family celebration. The graduate is the guest of honour. If they want to thank their parents, a small treat or a meal later on is a kind way to do it.

### How much does a graduation meal cost?
It varies with the restaurant and the size of the group. In a 2026 Confused.com survey, people said they spent about £81 on meals out for a graduation, as part of an average total of about £766.

### How do two families split a graduation meal?
Either each family pays for its own people and they share the graduate's meal, or they split the total 50/50. Each pays for its own is fairer when the families are different sizes.
