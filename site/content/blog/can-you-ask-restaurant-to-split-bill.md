Yes. In the UK it's completely normal to ask a restaurant to split the bill, and most will do it, either equally between cards or by what each person had. The polite way is to ask early, keep it simple ("split four ways, please"), and avoid it for big groups at busy times, where one card and settling up afterwards is much kinder to the staff.

Restaurants are used to it, and most card machines can divide a total between cards. The issue isn't whether you can ask. It's how much work the way you ask creates.

## How do restaurants split the bill?

There are three common ways, and they're very different amounts of work for the server.

| Method | What happens | Work for staff |
|---|---|---|
| **Split equally** | The total is divided by the number of cards, and each pays the same | Low. Most card machines can do it. |
| **Split by item** | The server moves each person's dishes onto a separate bill | Higher. Each item has to be assigned, often on the till. |
| **Custom amounts** | Each person says an amount to put on their card | Medium. Fine if someone's already done the maths. |

"Split equally four ways" takes a server a minute. "Can you do it by what everyone had?" for eight people can take ten, while their other tables wait.

## How do restaurant staff feel about splitting the bill?

Honestly, it depends on how it's done, when, and how big the table is.

Splitting a bill for two or four people is routine and most servers won't think twice. What frustrates staff is:

- **Being asked at the very end, by a large table, on a busy night.** Ten separate card payments with different amounts can tie up a server for a long time.
- **Arguments at the card machine.** Standing there while a table debates who had the garlic bread.
- **Lots of cards, each for a few pounds.** Each payment is a separate transaction.
- **[Cash and card mixed](/blog/split-bill-cash-and-card/) with no clear total.**

There's also tipping. In a 2016 OpenTable survey, 42% of restaurant owners said splitting the bill reduced the amount guests tipped (OpenTable, 2016). When a bill is split into lots of small payments, people often round down or skip the tip, and nobody owns the total.

:::stat 42% | of UK restaurant owners say splitting the bill reduces tips | OpenTable UK survey, 2016

That figure is from 2016, but anyone who's worked a Saturday shift will recognise them.

## How to make it easy for the staff

If you want the restaurant to split the bill, a few small things make a big difference.

### Say it early

Mention it when you order, or when you ask for the bill: "We'll be splitting this four ways, if that's OK." That gives the server time to plan, and some restaurants can set up separate bills from the start.

### Keep the instructions simple

- **Best:** "Split equally between four cards, please."
- **Fine:** "Could we have two bills? These three on one, these two on the other."
- **Harder:** "Can you split it by what everyone had?" Fine for a small table, but expect a wait.

### Do your own maths first if amounts differ

If everyone owes a different amount, work it out at the table before the server arrives, then give them the numbers: "£32.50 on this card, £41 on this one, the rest on this one." That's much faster than asking them to rebuild the bill item by item. [How to split a bill fairly](/blog/how-to-split-a-bill-fairly/) shows the sums, service charge included.

### Include the service charge

If a service charge is on the bill, make sure it's in each card payment, not forgotten when the total is chopped up. If there's no service charge and you want to tip, add it to each payment or leave it together. Since 1 October 2024, the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 means card tips and service charges must be passed on to workers in full, so they reach the staff whether they're on one card or six. If you're not sure how much to leave, see [how much to tip in the UK](/blog/how-much-to-tip-uk/).

### Be patient and say thank you

It's extra work. A thank you goes a long way.

## When one card and settling up is kinder

For bigger groups, one card is usually the better option, for everyone.

- **10 or more people:** one card, then settle up. Many restaurants limit the number of cards they'll split between for large tables, so ask at booking. Our guide to [splitting a bill for a large group](/blog/split-bill-large-group/) covers deposits, set menus and the night itself.
- **Busy service:** if the place is packed, don't ask for an item by item split.
- **Lots of shared dishes and bottles:** these are hard for a server to divide, and easy to get wrong.
- **People leaving at different times:** settling up afterwards means nobody has to wait.

The tradeoff is that someone has to pay the whole bill and get paid back. That's where it can turn awkward, so do it quickly: send everyone their share before you leave the table.

### Worked example: six friends, £214.88 bill

The bill comes to £214.88 including 12.5% service. Two people didn't drink, and two shared a bottle of wine.

**Option A: ask the restaurant to split by item.** The server moves 20-odd items onto six bills, adds service to each, and takes six card payments. On a busy Friday, that's a long wait.

**Option B: split equally six ways.** £35.81 each, with two people paying £35.82 so it adds up. Quick, but the two who didn't drink pay for wine they didn't have.

**Option C: one card, then settle up by item.** Sophie pays £214.88. Everyone works out what they had at the table and sends her their exact share before leaving. The server takes one payment, the split is fair, and nobody's waiting at the card machine.

Option C is the kindest to the staff and the fairest to the table. It just needs a quick way to do the maths.

## FAQ

### Is it rude to ask a restaurant to split the bill?
No. It's normal in the UK. Ask early, keep the request simple and be patient. For large groups or very busy nights, one card and settling up afterwards is more considerate.

### Will a restaurant split a bill between multiple cards?
Most will, usually equally or by amount. Some limit the number of cards for large groups, so if you're booking for 10 or more, ask when you book.

### Can you ask a restaurant to split the bill by what each person ordered?
Yes, and many will, but it takes the server much longer than an equal split. For more than four or five people, it's often quicker to work it out yourselves and give the server the amounts.

### Do restaurants charge for splitting the bill?
It's not common in the UK. If a restaurant has a policy on splitting for large groups, it's usually stated on the menu or at booking.
