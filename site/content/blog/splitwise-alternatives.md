The best alternative to Splitwise depends on what you're splitting. For one restaurant bill where everyone had different things, you want an app that reads the receipt line by line and lets each person claim their own items. For a fortnight of shared costs on a trip, or a house of flatmates, you want an app that keeps a running balance of who owes whom.

Most "best app" lists mix those two jobs up. This one doesn't. For a shorter, side by side view by situation, see [the best bill splitting apps in the UK](/blog/best-bill-splitting-apps-uk/). Below is what each app does today, what it costs in the UK, and who it suits. We checked every feature and price on the app's own website, its UK App Store listing or launch coverage in October 2026. This market moves quickly (Splitwise added free receipt splitting in the UK in mid October 2026), so we'll keep this page updated.

## Which app should you use?

- **You live with people or share costs over weeks:** Splitwise, Tricount or Settle Up.
- **You're on a group holiday:** Splid, Tricount or Splitwise for the trip as a whole, plus an itemised split for the big dinners.
- **You've just paid for dinner and everyone had different things:** Splitwise Itemize or OnlyBills. Both let friends claim from their own phone.
- **Everyone had roughly the same and you all bank with one app:** your bank's split button is free and quick.

## Splitwise

Splitwise is the app everyone else gets compared with, so here's the short version. You add expenses as they happen, split them equally, by exact amounts, percentages or shares, and it keeps a running balance per group, with debt simplification to cut the number of payments. It runs on iPhone, Android and the web.

**New in October 2026: Itemize.** According to The Paypers, Splitwise launched Itemize in the US and UK in mid October 2026. One person photographs the receipt, everyone else scans a QR code and claims their own items without the app or an account, and it's free.

**What it costs:** free, with a daily cap on how many expenses you can add. Splitwise Pro removes the cap and adds extras like currency conversion; the UK App Store lists Pro in-app purchases from £2.79 to £47.99.

**Best for:** the same group over time, and now one-off receipts too. For a feature by feature look at how it handles a restaurant bill, see [OnlyBills vs Splitwise](/vs/splitwise/).

## Tricount

Tricount has been owned by the Dutch bank bunq since 2022. Its pitch is simple: "Free. No ads. No limits." You create a group, add who paid for what, and it shows balances and who should pay whom. It handles multiple currencies, works offline, and lets you send payment requests with reminders. The App Store listing says you can share what friends outside Tricount owe you, and import Splitwise groups.

There's no itemised receipt splitting. You add each expense as a total and choose who it applies to.

**Best for:** anyone who wants a free group balance app with no subscription. If Tricount isn't quite right for your group, see [Tricount alternatives](/blog/tricount-alternatives/).

## Settle Up

Settle Up (by Step Up Labs) tracks group expenses for trips, flatmates and couples. It splits evenly or by weights, calculates the fewest transfers, works offline, and sends reminders. People can view the group's balances in a browser through a shared link without installing anything.

It's free with ads. Premium removes ads and adds receipt photos, recurring expenses, charts and Excel export. On the UK App Store, Premium is £3.99 a month or £18.99 a year, with group passes from £5.99 for one week up to £149.99 for lifetime group premium.

**Best for:** organised groups who like reminders and stats.

## Splid

Splid is a small, highly rated app (4.9 out of 5 on the UK App Store) built for holidays and housemates. No sign-up is needed, it works offline, syncs when you want it to, and converts between more than 150 currencies. It can also make PDF summaries.

Its website says "Splid is free", and the UK App Store lists optional in-app purchases, including Splid Plus at £3.99.

**Best for:** a trip abroad where you want something light and quick to set up.

## Tab

Tab (by bring10) was one of the first itemised receipt apps. You photograph the bill, it detects the items and prices, and each person taps what they had. Shared items and proportional tax and tip are supported, and it's free.

Two cautions. Its own listing says "Everyone that has Tab on their phone can join onto the bill", so friends without it can't claim their own items. And its old website no longer works, with only a handful of UK App Store ratings, so it's worth checking it's still maintained before relying on it.

## Your bank's split button

Monzo, Revolut and Starling all let you split a card payment from inside the app after you've paid. They're free and there's nothing to install, but they split a total rather than reading the receipt.

- **Monzo:** split a payment equally or by editing amounts. Friends not on Monzo can be sent a request link.
- **Revolut:** the Split bill button works with other Revolut customers. Group Bills and payment links cover friends who aren't.
- **Starling:** split a transaction by the number of people and send each one a Settle Up link they can pay on a web page.

Step by step guides for each are in [how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling](/blog/split-bill-monzo-revolut-starling/).

## OnlyBills

OnlyBills is a UK app built for one job: splitting a restaurant, pub or group bill by what each person actually had. The host scans the receipt and it reads every line. You check it adds up, then either share a live table or assign items yourself.

With a live table, friends scan a QR code or open a link and tick what they had on their own phone, with nothing to install (it opens on the web, or as an App Clip on iPhone). Splitwise Itemize now does something similar, so here's where OnlyBills is different:

- **It's made for UK bills.** It handles a 12.5% service charge, discounts and extras by spreading them in proportion (the method is in [how to split service charge and tips fairly](/blog/split-service-charge-fairly/)).
- **Each friend gets their exact amount as a payment link** for the host's bank: Monzo (card, Apple Pay or bank transfer, no Monzo account needed), Starling or Revolut (card or Apple Pay, no account needed), PayPal (a PayPal account may be needed) or bank details (from any banking app). Nobody needs to be in the same app or bank as the host.
- **The host can assign items for anyone who won't tap anything**, so the table still gets split when half your friends ignore the link.
- **Shares are exact to the penny** and always add up to the bill.

:::shot guest-01-join | Friends join from a link or QR code and pick their name. No app download.

**What it costs:** free, with no sign-up. Free includes 30 receipt scans in any 30 days. Typing a bill in is always unlimited. OnlyBills Pro, an optional subscription, raises the scan limit. The host needs an iPhone or iPad; friends join from their browser.

**Not for:** rent, household bills or tracking a whole trip's costs. Use Splitwise, Tricount or Splid for those.

## A worked example: when itemising matters

Four friends, a £126 bill: £112 of food and drink plus a 12.5% service charge (£14).

| Person | What they had | Equal split | Itemised share (with service) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amy | £40.00 | £31.50 | £45.00 |
| Ben | £30.00 | £31.50 | £33.75 |
| Chloe | £14.00 | £31.50 | £15.75 |
| Dev | £28.00 | £31.50 | £31.50 |

Splitting equally, Chloe pays £15.75 more than her food and service cost, and Amy pays £13.50 less. Any app above can do an equal split. The itemised ones (Splitwise Itemize, OnlyBills, Tab) get Chloe the fair number without someone working it out by hand. If you'd rather do it yourself, the free [split the bill calculator](/tools/split-bill-calculator/) does the same sum. For more on when an equal split is fine, see [is splitting the bill equally fair?](/blog/is-splitting-the-bill-equally-fair/).

## Can you use more than one?

Yes, and plenty of people do. A common setup on a trip: a balance app like Splitwise or Tricount for the villa, car hire and supermarket runs, and an itemised split for each big group dinner. That way the trip's running costs and each dinner's itemised shares stay separate, and nobody argues about the lobster at 1am.

## FAQ

### Is there a free alternative to Splitwise?
Yes. Tricount says it's free with no ads and no limits. Splid's website says it's free, and Settle Up has a free version with ads. Your bank's split feature (Monzo, Revolut or Starling) is also free. Splitwise itself is free to start, with a daily cap on expenses.

### Which bill splitting app scans receipts?
Splitwise (with its free Itemize feature), OnlyBills and Tab read an itemised receipt so people can be charged for what they had. Tricount, Settle Up and Splid record totals instead.

### Do my friends need to download an app to split a bill?
Not always. With Splitwise Itemize and OnlyBills, friends join from a QR code (or, with OnlyBills, a link) without installing anything. Tab needs everyone to have it.

### What's the best app for splitting costs on holiday?
For costs across a whole trip, a balance app like Splid, Tricount or Splitwise is the right tool, because it keeps a running total and handles currencies. For each restaurant bill, an itemised split saves arguments over who had the lobster.
