Split per head, not per couple, whenever there are singles at the table. A couple is two people eating two meals, so they pay two shares. Splitting per couple only works when everyone is in a couple and each couple spent about the same. When drinks differ a lot, split the food per head, let each couple or person pay for their own drinks, and spread the service charge in proportion.

Most arguments about couples and the bill come from one mistake: dividing by the number of parties instead of the number of people. Here's how to avoid it, a worked example, and what to say.

## Should couples pay per couple or per head?

Per head is the safe default. It treats everyone at the table the same, whether they came with a partner or not.

Per couple (one share per couple) is fine in one situation: a dinner where every unit is a couple, and the couples ordered roughly the same. Two couples on a double date with a £150 bill can each pay £75 and nobody loses out.

The trouble starts when singles are there too.

## Why splitting per couple short-changes singles

Two couples and two single friends have dinner. The bill is £240.

If someone says "let's split it four ways, one per couple and one each for the singles", here's what happens:

| | Per party (4 shares) | Per head (6 shares) |
|---|---|---|
| Couple 1 (2 people) | £60.00 | £80.00 |
| Couple 2 (2 people) | £60.00 | £80.00 |
| Single friend | £60.00 | £40.00 |
| Single friend | £60.00 | £40.00 |
| **Total** | **£240.00** | **£240.00** |

Per party, each single pays £60 for one dinner while each couple pays £30 a person. The singles are paying £20 each towards the couples' meals. Nobody means it that way. It just sounds tidy when you say "four ways".

## What if the couples drink and the singles don't?

This is the other common gap. Couples often share a bottle or two of wine, and a bottle split between two adds up fast. A single friend who had a lime soda ends up paying for wine they never touched.

They're not alone in minding. In a 2016 OpenTable survey of UK diners, 54% said fellow diners ordering a number of alcoholic drinks was a reason to avoid splitting the bill, and 34% said they go along with splitting equally because they don't want others to think they're tight (OpenTable, 2016). The survey is from 2016, so treat the numbers as a rough guide.

The fix is the hybrid split: food per head, drinks to whoever had them, and service in proportion. There's more on this in [how to split the bill when some people don't drink](/blog/split-bill-when-some-dont-drink/).

## A worked example: two couples and two singles

Six people at dinner. Sam and Jo are a couple. Priya and Tom are a couple. Leah and Max came on their own. Everyone had a similar main and the table shared starters, so the food is split evenly.

**What was on the bill**

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Food for six, shared evenly | £150.00 |
| Sam and Jo: 2 bottles of wine at £28 | £56.00 |
| Priya and Tom: 1 bottle of wine | £28.00 |
| Leah: 2 lime sodas at £3.50 | £7.00 |
| Max: 2 pints at £6.30 | £12.60 |
| **Subtotal** | **£253.60** |
| Service charge, 12.5% | £31.70 |
| **Total** | **£285.30** |

**Step 1: food per head.** £150 ÷ 6 = £25 each. So each couple has £50 of food.

**Step 2: add each party's own drinks.**

| | Food | Drinks | Before service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sam and Jo | £50.00 | £56.00 | £106.00 |
| Priya and Tom | £50.00 | £28.00 | £78.00 |
| Leah | £25.00 | £7.00 | £32.00 |
| Max | £25.00 | £12.60 | £37.60 |
| **Total** | **£150.00** | **£103.60** | **£253.60** |

**Step 3: add service in proportion.** The service charge is 12.5% of everything, so each party pays 12.5% on top of their own total (multiply by 1.125).

| | Before service | Service | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sam and Jo | £106.00 | £13.25 | £119.25 |
| Priya and Tom | £78.00 | £9.75 | £87.75 |
| Leah | £32.00 | £4.00 | £36.00 |
| Max | £37.60 | £4.70 | £42.30 |
| **Total** | **£253.60** | **£31.70** | **£285.30** |

That adds up exactly to the bill.

**How it compares with an equal split.** £285.30 ÷ 6 is £47.55 a head, so £95.10 per couple.

| | Equal per head | Hybrid | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sam and Jo | £95.10 | £119.25 | £24.15 more |
| Priya and Tom | £95.10 | £87.75 | £7.35 less |
| Leah | £47.55 | £36.00 | £11.55 less |
| Max | £47.55 | £42.30 | £5.25 less |

On an equal split, Leah would have paid £11.55 towards other people's drinks. Priya and Tom would have subsidised the other couple's second bottle too. The two-bottle couple would have saved £24.15, paid for by everyone else.

## Should a couple pay as one?

For paying, yes, if they like. One payment per couple means fewer card transactions at the till and fewer bank transfers afterwards. Six people can settle with four payments.

How the couple divides their share between themselves is their business. Whether one of them drank more, or they pay from a joint account, it doesn't change what the table is owed.

The one thing to avoid is treating a couple as a single diner when it comes to the amount. "We'll just pay one share, we shared a dessert" doesn't work if they each had a main and drinks.

## When does per couple make sense?

| Situation | Best split |
|---|---|
| Only couples, similar orders | Per couple, equally |
| Only couples, one couple drank much more | Food per couple, drinks by couple |
| Couples and singles, similar orders | Per head (a couple pays two shares) |
| Couples and singles, drinks differ | Food per head, drinks by person or couple, service in proportion |
| Someone's treating a couple (an anniversary, say) | Split their share between the rest of the table, per head |
| Couples who earn very different amounts | Itemised, so nobody is stretched by others' choices |

## How to say it without it being awkward

The easiest time is before the bill comes. Ideally before you order, or when the plan is made in the group chat. Saying it early sounds like organising. Saying it at the end can sound like an accusation.

**In the group chat beforehand:**

> Shall we split food per head and everyone pays for their own drinks? Makes it easy with a mix of couples and not.

**If you're single and someone suggests splitting per couple:**

> Could we do it per head instead? Otherwise Max and I are paying for two each.

**If you're in the couple that drank most:**

> We had most of the wine, so we'll cover the drinks we had. Let's split the food evenly.

That last one is the kindest of the lot. When the people who ordered more say it first, nobody else has to.

**When the bill arrives:**

> Food's £25 each. Drinks on top for whoever had them, then 12.5% service. I'll do the sums and send everyone their number.

## FAQ

### Do couples pay double when splitting the bill?

On a per head split, a couple pays for two people, because they ate two meals. That's not paying double, it's paying their share. Splitting per couple with singles present makes the singles pay double instead.

### How do you split a bill between two couples?

If you ordered about the same, split it in half. If one couple drank much more, split the food in half and let each couple pay for their own drinks, then add the service charge in proportion.

### Is it rude to ask to split per head instead of per couple?

No. It's the standard way when there are singles at the table. Suggest it early and frame it as keeping things simple.

### How do you split a bottle of wine between a couple and a friend?

Divide it by the number of people who drank it. A £27 bottle shared by three is £9 each, whether two of them are a couple or not.
