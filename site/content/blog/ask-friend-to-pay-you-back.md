The least awkward way to ask a friend to pay you back is to do it the same day, with the exact amount and a way to pay in the same message. "Hey, dinner came to £34.60 each, here's my link" feels normal. Waiting three weeks and then hinting is what makes it weird.

If you feel uneasy asking, you're not alone. In a 2023 Opinium poll of 2,000 UK adults for Starling Bank, about a third (33%) said they were currently owed money by friends, family, partners or colleagues, and nearly half (46%) said they felt too awkward or embarrassed to ask for it back (Starling Bank, 2023).

## Why asking feels so awkward

Most people aren't worried about the money. They're worried about looking tight, or about implying their friend is the kind of person who doesn't pay. So they wait for the friend to bring it up. The friend, meanwhile, has genuinely forgotten, or is waiting to be told how much.

That's the trap: nobody owns the next step. Asking clearly breaks it. You're not accusing anyone of anything. You're just doing the admin that someone has to do.

A few things make it far easier:

- **You covered it, so you're doing them a favour.** Telling them what they owe is part of that favour, not a demand.
- **The amount is a fact, not an opinion.** "£34.60" is easier to send than "so, about dinner..."
- **Most people want to pay.** They just need to know how much and where.

## When should you ask?

**Same day, ideally within a few hours.** The meal is fresh, everyone's still in the group chat, and the amount feels like part of the evening rather than a debt.

| When you ask | How it lands |
|---|---|
| At the table or on the way home | Normal. Everyone expects it. |
| That evening or next morning | Still normal. "Sorting out last night" |
| A week later | Fine, but people have to think about what it was for |
| A month later | Starts to feel like a grudge, even when it isn't |

Starling's survey found it takes more than two months on average for people to be paid back, and about a third (32%) had had to ask someone two or more times (Starling Bank, 2023). The longer the gap, the more likely it is you'll be chasing.

## What to put in the message

A good payback message has four things, and nothing else:

1. **What it was for.** "Dinner on Friday", "the train tickets".
2. **The exact amount.** To the penny. Rounding down to be nice is fine; vague is not.
3. **How to pay.** A payment link or your bank details. Don't make them ask.
4. **A light, friendly tone.** No apology. You've done nothing wrong.

Skip the long explanation of how you worked it out unless they ask. If you want to show your working, a photo of the receipt does it.

## Message templates you can copy

Swap in the names, amounts and link. These work on text, WhatsApp or iMessage.

### After a dinner you paid for

> Thanks for a great night! Dinner came to £34.60 each with service. Here's my link when you get a sec: [link]

### When people had different amounts

If you need to work the numbers out first, the [split the bill calculator](/tools/split-bill-calculator/) does it by item, service charge included.

> Worked out last night's bill by what everyone had. Sam £28.40, Priya £41.10, Jack £33.75. Pay here whenever: [link]

### When you covered someone's share of tickets or travel

> Hey, your gig ticket was £46.50 (including the booking fee). Bank details: [name, sort code, account number]. Cheers.

### For a group chat after a big night

> Right, sorting the bill from Saturday 🍕 It's £27.80 each. Link's here: [link]. I'll tick people off as it comes in.

### A friend who's skint right now

> No rush at all, but for when you're ready, your bit of Friday was £22.50. Happy for you to send it in a couple of goes if that's easier.

### For a partner's friend or someone you don't know well

> Hi, it's Alex from Friday's dinner. Your share came to £31.20. Here's how to pay: [link]. Lovely to meet you.

:::note
Put the amount in the first line. People skim messages, and "£31.20" is the bit they need.
:::

## Make paying back the easy part

The biggest reason people pay late is friction. They read your message on the bus, mean to sort it later, and forget. If the message contains a tap-to-pay link, a lot of them will pay right then.

Options that work in the UK:

- **A bank payment link.** Monzo, Starling and Revolut all let you send a link, and the person paying doesn't usually need an account with that bank. There's a step by step guide to [splitting a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling](/blog/split-bill-monzo-revolut-starling/).
- **Your sort code and account number.** Works from any banking app. Put your name exactly as the bank has it so the name check matches.
- **PayPal.** Fine if your friends use it, though they may need an account.

In Starling's survey, two in five (40%) people said they'd prefer to be paid what they're owed digitally (Starling Bank, 2023). Cash is fine too, but don't make someone find a cashpoint.

## What if they don't reply?

Give it a few days, then send one reminder. Keep it shorter and lighter than the first message, not longer.

### A reminder (3 to 5 days later)

> Hey, just bumping this in case it got lost: £34.60 for Friday. Link: [link] 🙏

If the link might be the problem, offer another way: "Would it help if I sent my bank details instead?"

### If you'd rather talk in person

> "Oh, before I forget, did you get my message about Friday? Fine to send it whenever."

Asking a question ("did you get my message?") gives them an easy way out of the awkwardness: they can say "oh god, sorry, doing it now". Most people do.

If one reminder hasn't worked, or it's turned into a pattern with one friend, the next steps are in [what to do when someone won't pay their share](/blog/someone-wont-pay-their-share/): a more direct second message, asking what's going on, and when it's worth letting go.

## How to stop it happening next time

- **Agree how you'll pay before the bill comes.** "Shall I get it and you all send me your bit?" sets the expectation.
- **Ask on the night.** Send the amounts before people get home.
- **Use one link for everyone.** One message in the group chat is easier than six separate ones.
- **Keep track.** A note on your phone of who's paid stops you chasing someone who already did, which is far more awkward than chasing someone who hasn't. In OnlyBills, [Owed](/how-to/track-who-owes-you/) keeps that list for you across bills.

## FAQ

### How do you politely ask a friend to pay you back?
Send a short message the same day with what it was for, the exact amount and a way to pay, such as "Dinner came to £34.60 each, here's my link." Friendly, specific and no apology.

### How long should you wait before asking for money back?
Don't wait. The same day or the next morning is normal and expected. If they haven't paid after 3 to 5 days, send one short reminder. If that doesn't work, see our guide to [when someone won't pay their share](/blog/someone-wont-pay-their-share/).

### Is it rude to ask for small amounts back?
No. If you agreed to split it, asking for £8 is just as reasonable as asking for £80. In Starling's 2023 survey, the minimum people said they'd need to be owed before asking for it back was £59, but there's no reason to sit on smaller amounts. If it's genuinely tiny and you'd rather treat them, that's your call.

### What do you say if a friend keeps forgetting to pay you back?
Ask a direct but warm question: "Did you get my message about Friday? Still need the £34.60 when you can." If it keeps happening, sort payment on the night next time, before anyone leaves.
