No. In most UK restaurants the service charge is discretionary, which means it's optional, even when it's printed on the bill automatically. You can ask for it to be removed, and you don't legally need a reason. The exception is a compulsory service charge that was clearly shown to you before you ordered, usually on the menu. That one is part of the price.

Since 1 October 2024, the law has also changed what happens to the money: employers in Great Britain must pass every penny of tips and service charges they control to their staff.

This isn't legal advice, but here's how it works in practice.

## Discretionary vs compulsory service charges

There are two kinds, and the wording tells you which you've got.

| | Discretionary (optional) | Compulsory |
|---|---|---|
| Usual wording | "A discretionary 12.5% service charge will be added", "optional service" | "A 12.5% service charge is included / applies to all bills" |
| Can you refuse it? | Yes, just ask for it to be taken off | Generally no, it's part of the agreed price |
| Is VAT charged on it? | No | Yes |
| Must it be in the menu prices? | No | Yes, since April 2025 (see below) |

Almost all UK restaurant service charges are discretionary. Restaurants often add them for groups ("a discretionary service charge of 12.5% is added for tables of six or more"), but adding it automatically doesn't make it compulsory.

HMRC's VAT guidance draws the same line. A service charge is only treated as optional "if customers have a genuine option" to pay it, and "it is the menu that sets the contract between the restaurant and the customer". In other words, what you were told before ordering is what counts. The bill "comes too late to change this contract" (HMRC VAT manual, VATSC06130).

## Can you refuse to pay a service charge?

**A discretionary service charge:** yes. Ask the server to remove it before you pay. You don't have to give a reason, though most people only do it when service was poor or they want to tip a different amount.

**A compulsory service charge:** usually not, because it was part of the price you agreed to when you ordered. But if the service fell well short, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 requires services to be performed "with reasonable care and skill" (section 49). Where they aren't, you may be entitled to a price reduction (section 56). Raise it calmly with the manager at the time.

**Hidden charges:** if a compulsory charge wasn't made clear before you ordered, you have good grounds to challenge it. Since 6 April 2025, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has banned "drip pricing", and the CMA's price transparency guidance (CMA209, November 2025) lists "mandatory cover or service charges at a restaurant" among the charges that must be included in the headline price. So a compulsory service charge should be built into the prices on the menu, not added at the end.

## How to ask for the service charge to be removed

You don't need to make a scene. Quietly, to your server or at the till:

> "Could you take the service charge off, please? We'd like to tip separately."

If service was poor:

> "Would you mind removing the service charge? The service wasn't great tonight, and I'd rather not pay it."

If you'd like to change the amount rather than remove it:

> "Could you take the 15% off and we'll leave 10% instead?"

It's a common request. A 2020 YouGov survey found 48% of Britons had asked for a service charge to be removed at least once, and 10% said they did it every time. RSM UK (2024) found 22% of consumers never pay the optional service charge.

## What the tips law changed in 2024

The Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 came into force on 1 October 2024 in England, Scotland and Wales. It's about what employers do with the money, not whether customers have to pay. The main points:

- **All of it goes to staff.** Employers must pass on all tips, gratuities and service charges they control, with no deductions (other than normal tax and National Insurance). That includes service charges added to the bill and tips paid by card.
- **No skimming card fees.** The amount that counts is what the customer paid, so a business can't take a cut to cover card processing.
- **Shared fairly.** Tips must be "allocated fairly" between workers, following a statutory code of practice.
- **Paid on time.** Workers must get their share by the end of the month after the month the tip was paid.
- **Written policy.** Places that regularly receive tips must have a written tipping policy that staff can see.
- **Records.** Employers must keep records of how tips were handled for three years, and workers can ask to see them.

Cash handed directly to a worker, which the business doesn't control, isn't caught by these rules in the same way. It's generally the worker's to keep.

**What's coming next.** The Employment Rights Act 2025 adds a duty for employers to consult their workers on their tipping policy and review it regularly. At the time of writing (October 2026), the government had withdrawn its revised code of practice so it could hold a new public consultation, and said employers should keep following the existing 2024 code.

## So should you still pay it?

The law means a service charge now reaches the staff who served you, which was a common worry before 2024. If service was good, paying the service charge is the simplest way to tip.

If you'd rather tip a different amount, or the service was poor, it's fine to ask for it to be removed. Being polite about it is all the etiquette you need. For a guide to how much is normal, see [how much to tip in the UK](/blog/how-much-to-tip-uk/).

## FAQ

### Is a 12.5% service charge compulsory in the UK?

Usually not. Most restaurant service charges are discretionary and you can ask for them to be removed. It's only compulsory if it was clearly part of the price before you ordered, and compulsory charges should now be included in the menu prices.

### Can a restaurant force you to pay a service charge?

Not a discretionary one. A compulsory charge that was clearly shown before you ordered is part of the contract, but if service was poor you may be entitled to a price reduction under the Consumer Rights Act 2015.

### Do restaurants have to give the service charge to staff?

Yes. Since 1 October 2024, employers in England, Scotland and Wales must pass all tips and service charges they control to workers, without deductions, and share them fairly.

### Do I need to tip if there's already a service charge?

No. The service charge is the tip. Only add more if you want to.

### Can a restaurant add service charge for large groups?

Yes, many add a discretionary charge for tables of six or more. It's still optional unless it was a clearly stated compulsory charge built into the price.
