In the UK there's no single rule, but splitting the bill is the most popular answer. In a 2023 Ipsos poll of 1,000 British adults, 34% said the bill on a first date should be split equally, 25% said the person who asked should pay, and 22% said the man should pay (Ipsos, 2023). So the polite default is: the person who invited offers to pay, the other offers to split, and you go with whatever feels comfortable to both of you.

## What UK surveys say

Ipsos asked 1,000 British adults aged 18 to 75 in February 2023 to imagine a man and a woman on a first date at a restaurant, eating and drinking the same. Who should be most responsible for paying?

| Answer | All | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|---|
| Split the bill equally | 34% | 28% | 39% |
| The person who invited the other | 25% | 27% | 23% |
| The man | 22% | 28% | 16% |
| Each pays for what they ordered | 15% | 11% | 18% |
| The woman | 1% | | |
| Don't know | 4% | 5% | 3% |

*Source: Ipsos UK Valentine's Day polling, 8 to 9 February 2023. Base: 1,000 online British adults aged 18 to 75.*

Two things stand out. Splitting (either equally or by what you ordered) adds up to about half of people, 49%. And men are more likely than women to say the man should pay: 28% of men against 16% of women.

A smaller 2022 survey of 739 UK adults who were single or dating, for Aqua credit cards, found the same pattern. Splitting the cost evenly was the most popular answer overall, chosen by 27%. Among men, the most common answer was paying because they wanted to treat their date (28%), and 19% of men said the man should always pay (Aqua, 2023).

So if you're wondering whether offering to split is rude, it isn't. It's the most common preference in UK polls, with plenty of exceptions.

## So who should pay?

A good working rule:

- **If you asked, offer to pay.** A quarter of people in the Ipsos poll said the person who invited should pay, and it's a simple rule both of you can follow.
- **If you were asked, offer to split.** A genuine offer, not a token reach for your purse.
- **Then go with what's said.** If they insist on paying, say thanks and offer to get the next one. If they're happy to split, split.

This works whatever your genders, and it avoids the guessing game. Nobody has to assume.

### What if one of you earns a lot more?

It's fine for the person with more money to treat, as long as it's offered, not assumed. If you're on a tight budget, pick the venue so you know you can cover your half: a pub, a coffee, a walk and a drink. There are more ideas in [the cost of going out in the UK, and how to spend less](/blog/cost-of-going-out-uk/).

### What if one of you ordered a lot more?

Splitting equally is easiest when you had similar things. If one of you had three cocktails and a steak and the other had a salad and tap water, splitting by what you each had is fairer. "Shall we just pay for our own?" is a perfectly normal thing to say.

## How to offer to split gracefully

The words matter less than the timing. Offer while the bill is on its way, not after they've already tapped their card.

### Phrases you can use

- "Shall we split it?"
- "Let me get half."
- "Can I get this one? You can get the next."
- "Happy to go halves, or we can just pay for our own."
- "I'd like to get this, if that's OK." (if you asked them out)

### If they say no and want to pay

- "That's really kind, thank you. I'll get the drinks after." Then actually do it.
- "Thank you. Next one's on me." Only say this if you want a next one.

### If they insist on splitting and you wanted to pay

Let it go. Pushing to pay after someone has said they'd like to split can feel like you're not listening.

:::note
Want to avoid the moment entirely? Excuse yourself near the end and settle with the staff at the bar. It's a classic move for a reason. Just be sure your date won't mind you paying.
:::

## What about the second date?

If one of you paid for the first date, the other usually offers to pay for the second, or at least the drinks afterwards. Taking it in turns is common and it evens out over time without anyone keeping score.

If you split the first date, carry on splitting until one of you suggests otherwise. Once you're seeing each other regularly, many couples settle into a pattern that suits their budgets: alternating, splitting, or the person who earns more paying a bigger share. The only rule is to talk about it rather than assume.

## How to split the bill on a date without it being awkward

Most UK restaurants are happy to split a bill for two between two cards (more on [asking a restaurant to split the bill](/blog/can-you-ask-restaurant-to-split-bill/)). Simply ask: "Could we split it, please?" The server can usually put half on each card, or each person's items on their own card.

If one of you pays and the other sends their half, do it right away, at the table or on the way home, with the exact amount. Don't leave a payment hanging over a first date. A short message works:

> Had a lovely time. Sent you £28.40 for my half of dinner. 😊

Paying the other person back the same night is polite, and it keeps money out of the second-date conversation.

## Tips for a first date that keeps money simple

- **Pick somewhere with prices you know.** No surprises when the bill arrives.
- **Check for a service charge.** Many UK restaurants add 12.5%. It's optional, but if you're splitting, split it too. See [is service charge mandatory](/blog/is-service-charge-mandatory-uk/), and if there's none, [how much to tip in the UK](/blog/how-much-to-tip-uk/).
- **Agree the plan early if it's a long evening.** Drinks, then food, then more drinks can add up. "Shall we take it in turns?" works well.
- **Coffee or a drink first.** A lower-cost first date takes pressure off whoever ends up paying.

In the Aqua survey, women's most popular ideal first date spend was £20 to £30, while men's was £50 to £60 (Aqua, 2023). You don't need to spend a lot to make a good impression.

## FAQ

### Should the man pay on a first date in the UK?
Some people still think so, but it's a minority view. In a 2023 Ipsos poll, 22% of British adults said the man should pay, while 34% said the bill should be split equally and 25% said the person who asked should pay.

### Is it rude to split the bill on a first date?
No. Splitting is the most popular answer in UK polls. Offer politely while the bill is on its way, and if your date insists on paying, accept graciously and offer to get the next one.

### Who should pay on a first date if you met online?
The same rule works: whoever suggested the date offers to pay, and the other offers to split. If it was mutual, splitting is the natural default.

### How do you offer to pay on a first date without making it awkward?
Offer as the bill is arriving, simply and once: "Shall we split it?" or "Let me get this one." Then go with their answer.
