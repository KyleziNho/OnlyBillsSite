For a group booking, each guest pays their own share of the deposit to the organiser before the organiser pays the restaurant. The deposit is then taken off the final bill, so on the night each person pays their share minus what they've already put in. If someone drops out, whether they get their money back depends on the restaurant's terms and on whether the group still gets the full deposit off the bill.

Deposits and no-show fees are legal in the UK, as long as the terms were clear when you booked and they're fair. An unfair term isn't binding on you, even if you agreed to it.

Those figures come from ResDiary, a restaurant booking platform, based on 208 UK venues and 827 consumers in January and February 2023. It's a small, industry-run survey, but it explains why groups get asked for deposits more and more. In the same report, venues with deposits that set a minimum party size put it at eight people on average.

## Who pays the deposit for a group booking?

Everyone pays their own part, and the organiser pays the restaurant. The organiser shouldn't put the whole deposit on their card and hope to collect it later. That's how organisers end up £60 down when four people drop out.

A simple order that works:

1. **Ask the restaurant** for the deposit amount, the deadline, and the cancellation terms (in writing).
2. **Tell the group** the per-head deposit, the deadline and what happens if they cancel.
3. **Collect** from everyone before the restaurant's deadline.
4. **Pay the restaurant** once you've collected. Count someone as coming only once they've paid.
5. **Keep a list:** name, deposit paid, date paid.

## How does a deposit come off the final bill?

The restaurant takes the total deposit off the bill, and each person takes their own deposit off their own share. Don't take the deposits off the total and then split the rest equally, unless you were splitting equally anyway.

### Worked example: ten people, £15 deposit each

Ten friends book a table. Each paid the organiser £15, so the restaurant holds £150. The bill comes to £612.00 including 12.5% service.

| | Their share (incl. service) | Less deposit | Pays on the night |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amy | £72.00 | £15.00 | £57.00 |
| Ben | £58.50 | £15.00 | £43.50 |
| Chloe | £45.00 | £15.00 | £30.00 |
| Dev | £81.00 | £15.00 | £66.00 |
| Ellie | £63.00 | £15.00 | £48.00 |
| Fin | £54.00 | £15.00 | £39.00 |
| Grace | £49.50 | £15.00 | £34.50 |
| Harry | £67.50 | £15.00 | £52.50 |
| Isla | £60.75 | £15.00 | £45.75 |
| Jack | £60.75 | £15.00 | £45.75 |
| **Total** | **£612.00** | **£150.00** | **£462.00** |

The restaurant's bill shows £462.00 still to pay. The organiser pays it, and everyone pays them their figure from the last column. Every share adds up, and nobody pays their deposit twice.

Two mistakes to watch for:

- **Forgetting the deposit altogether.** Check the bill shows it. If it doesn't, ask before anyone pays.
- **The organiser forgetting their own deposit.** They paid it too, so they take it off their share like everyone else.

## What happens to the deposit when someone drops out?

It depends on two things: what the restaurant does, and what the group agreed. Here are the common cases.

| What happens | What's fair |
|---|---|
| They cancel before the restaurant's deadline, and the restaurant refunds or reduces the deposit | Give them their deposit back |
| Someone takes their place | The new person pays the dropout directly, and the list is updated |
| The restaurant keeps their deposit as a cancellation charge | They lose it, if the group agreed that in the invite |
| The restaurant still takes the full deposit off the bill | Give it back. The table used their money, so it's not lost |

The last case catches a lot of groups out. Say Jack drops out the day before, and the restaurant still takes all £150 off the bill for nine people. If the other nine each take only their own £15 off, the organiser is left with £15 more than they paid. That's Jack's, so it goes back to him.

### What if someone just doesn't turn up?

If the restaurant charges a no-show fee for the missing seat, it usually goes on the card that made the booking. That's often the organiser's card. The fair thing is for the person who didn't come to pay it, if the group agreed that up front. Ask once, kindly, the next day. If they won't, our guide on [what to do when someone won't pay their share](/blog/someone-wont-pay-their-share/) has calm next steps.

## Are no-show fees legal in the UK?

Yes, in general. A restaurant can charge a deposit or a no-show fee if the term was made clear before you booked and it's fair. But "it's in the terms" isn't the end of it.

- **Unfair terms aren't binding.** Under section 62 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015, "An unfair term of a consumer contract is not binding on the consumer." A term is unfair if, contrary to good faith, it "causes a significant imbalance" in rights and obligations to the consumer's detriment.
- **Some terms are on the grey list.** Schedule 2 of the Act lists terms that may be unfair, including one requiring a consumer who cancels to pay "a disproportionately high sum in compensation or for services which have not been supplied".
- **Deposits should be small.** The Competition and Markets Authority's consumer guide says "Non-refundable deposits should only be a small percentage of the total price", and that a business is generally only entitled to keep enough to cover its actual losses.
- **Cancellation charges should reflect real loss.** The same guide says charges "must be a genuine estimate of the business' direct loss", and that businesses must take reasonable steps to reduce their losses. For a restaurant, that could mean filling the table again.

So a modest per-head deposit that comes off the bill, or a small no-show fee that was clearly shown when you booked, fits that guidance. Keeping a large prepayment when the restaurant filled the table anyway is harder to justify. Whether a particular charge is fair depends on the facts, and only a court can decide for certain.

### Do you get 14 days to cancel a restaurant booking?

No. The 14-day cancellation right for things bought online or by phone doesn't apply to catering or leisure services booked for a specific date. That's regulation 28(1)(h) of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013. Your rights come from the booking terms and the fairness rules above.

### What if you think a charge is unfair?

Write to the restaurant first, say which charge you're disputing and why, and ask for a refund. Keep it short and polite. If that doesn't work, Citizens Advice has a consumer service that can explain your options.

## Messages to copy

### Asking for the deposit

> Booked for Friday 7.30pm, 10 of us 🎉 The restaurant needs a £15 deposit each, which comes off the bill on the night. Please send it by Sunday: [link]. If you cancel after Tuesday the restaurant keeps it, so only pay if you're sure.

### Reminder before the deadline

> Final call for deposits, I'm confirming numbers tomorrow morning. If I haven't got yours I'll assume you can't make it, no worries either way.

### When someone drops out after the deadline

> Sorry you can't make it. The restaurant's keeping the deposit for late cancellations, so I can't refund the £15. If someone takes your place, they'll pay you back.

### When the deposit still came off the bill

> Good news, the restaurant took the full deposit off the bill even though you couldn't come. So your £15 went towards everyone's dinner, and I've just sent it back to you.

## FAQ

### Is a restaurant deposit taken off the final bill?
Usually, yes. Most group deposits are taken off the bill at the end. Check the bill shows it before anyone pays, and take each person's deposit off their own share.

### Can a restaurant keep my deposit if I cancel?
It can if the booking terms say so, the terms were clear when you booked, and the amount is fair. The CMA says non-refundable deposits should be a small percentage of the total price.

### Are no-show fees legal in the UK?
Yes, if they were made clear before booking and are fair. Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, an unfair term isn't binding, and a charge that's disproportionately high may be unfair.

### What happens to the deposit if the restaurant cancels?
You should get it back in full. The Consumer Rights Act lists, as possibly unfair, terms that let a business keep your money when you cancel without giving you equivalent compensation when the business cancels.

### Who pays the deposit if someone doesn't turn up?
The person who didn't turn up, if the group agreed that up front. If the restaurant still took the full deposit off the bill, their deposit wasn't lost and they should get it back.
