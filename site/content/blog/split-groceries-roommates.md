The fairest way to split groceries with roommates is to separate the receipt into two piles: **shared staples**, which you split evenly between the people who use them, and **personal items**, which each person pays for. Whoever paid at the register sends everyone their total, and you settle up weekly so the amounts stay small.

Splitting the whole receipt evenly is quicker, and it's fine if you all eat the same things. As soon as one person buys salmon and another buys ramen, it isn't. Below: three systems roommates actually use, a worked example and a message template for the group chat. (In the UK? It all works the same for housemates.)

## Three ways roommates split groceries

| System | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A shared kitty | Everyone puts in the same amount each week or month; shared food comes out of it | Groups who cook together | Personal food still needs its own budget; someone has to top it up |
| Take turns | Each person pays for the shared shop in rotation | Similar budgets and habits | Shops vary in size, so it drifts over time |
| Split each receipt by item | Shared items split, personal items to their owner | Different diets and budgets | Takes a couple of minutes per receipt |

Many homes mix them: a kitty or rotation for staples, and everyone buys their own personal food. If you shop together and put it all on one card, splitting each receipt by item is the one that stays fair.

## Shared staples vs personal items

Agree on the list once, and most arguments go away. A typical split:

- **Shared:** milk, eggs, bread, coffee, cooking oil, salt and spices, paper towels, dish soap, trash bags.
- **Personal:** anything only one person eats, snacks, alcohol, special diets (oat milk, protein powder), anything bought for a guest.

Two rules that help:

1. **Shared means used by everyone who pays for it.** If one roommate never drinks milk, the milk is split between the others.
2. **If in doubt, it's personal.** It's easier to share a jar of peanut butter later than to argue about who ate half of it.

## A worked example: one $96 receipt, three roommates

Riley pays $96.05 at the register for the week's shop. There's no sales tax on this receipt, since many states don't tax most groceries (if yours does, see below).

**Shared staples, split three ways: $44.23**

Milk $4.29, eggs $5.49, bread $3.99, coffee $8.99, olive oil $9.99, paper towels $7.99, dish soap $3.49.

$44.23 ÷ 3 = $14.743, so one person pays $14.75 and the other two pay $14.74. That way the shares add up to exactly $44.23.

**Personal items**

- Jordan: chicken thighs $9.49, Greek yogurt $5.99, granola $4.79 = $20.27
- Sam: oat milk $4.49, tofu $3.29, frozen pizza $6.99 = $14.77
- Riley: salmon $11.49, sparkling water $5.29 = $16.78

**Who owes what**

| Roommate | Personal | Share of staples | Total | Even split |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jordan | $20.27 | $14.75 | $35.02 | $32.02 |
| Sam | $14.77 | $14.74 | $29.51 | $32.02 |
| Riley (paid) | $16.78 | $14.74 | $31.52 | $32.01 |
| **Total** | **$51.82** | **$44.23** | **$96.05** | **$96.05** |

Jordan sends Riley $35.02 and Sam sends $29.51. On an even split, Sam would pay $2.51 more than their share this week. That's small once, but it's about $130 over a year of weekly shops.

### If the receipt has sales tax

Where groceries are taxed, the tax is often only on some lines, like paper towels, soap or soda. The simple fair approach: tax on a shared item is split with the item, and tax on a personal item goes to its owner. If that's too fiddly, spread the tax in proportion to what each person's items cost, the same way you'd [split tax and tip on a restaurant check](/blog/split-check-tax-and-tip/).

### Bulk buys and warehouse clubs

A Costco or Sam's Club receipt is the same exercise with bigger numbers. A few things to agree:

- **Big packs:** split between everyone who'll use them. If only two of you want the 48-pack of sparkling water, it's a two-way split.
- **Partial shares:** if someone wants a third of something, say so at the store, not when the receipt comes out.
- **The membership:** usually the member's own cost, unless you all agreed to share it.

## Settling up without nagging

Small, regular amounts get paid. Large, old ones cause friction. What works:

- **Settle after every shop or once a week.** Send the totals the same day the receipt comes home.
- **One person shops, everyone pays them.** Then the money only moves in one direction each time.
- **Keep a running tally** if you take turns paying, so it evens out across weeks instead of after every trip.
- **Use whatever the house already uses.** If two of you are on Venmo and one only has Zelle, that's fine. See [splitting when everyone uses a different payment app](/blog/split-bill-different-payment-apps/).

## FAQ

### What's the fairest way to split groceries with roommates?
Split shared staples (milk, coffee, cleaning supplies) evenly between the people who use them, and have each person pay for their own personal items. It takes a minute longer than splitting the whole receipt evenly, but nobody pays for someone else's steak.

### Should roommates split groceries evenly?
Only if you eat about the same and mostly share food. If one roommate buys much more, or someone has a very different diet, split by item. An even split quietly overcharges whoever buys least.

### How do you split a Costco receipt with roommates?
The same way, but watch the bulk items. Split a big pack between everyone who'll use it, and if someone only wants part of it, agree the share up front (for example, one of three rotisserie chickens). Membership fees usually stay with the member.

### How often should roommates settle up for groceries?
Weekly or after each shop works best. Small amounts are easy to pay and easy to remember. Letting it run for a month turns a few $20 debts into one awkward $150 conversation.
