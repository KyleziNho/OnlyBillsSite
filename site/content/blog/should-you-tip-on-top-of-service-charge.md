No. In the UK, a service charge is the tip, so you don't need to add anything on top. If the bill already includes 12.5% service, adding another 10% would mean tipping more than 20%, which is far above what's normal here. Only add more if the service was exceptional and you'd like to say thank you to someone in particular.

## Why the service charge counts as the tip

A service charge is a tip that the restaurant adds for you. Since 1 October 2024, employers in England, Scotland and Wales have had to pass every penny of tips and service charges they control to their workers, with no deductions apart from normal tax and National Insurance (Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023). So the service charge on your bill is meant to reach the staff. More on where the money goes in [who gets the tip?](/blog/who-gets-the-tip-uk-tipping-law/)

For comparison, YouGov found the average UK restaurant tip was 6.1% in 2023. A 12.5% service charge is already twice that.

## What tipping twice looks like

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Food and drink | £80.00 |
| 12.5% service charge | £10.00 |
| Bill | £90.00 |
| Extra 10% tip on the £90 | £9.00 |
| **You pay** | **£99.00** |

That's £19 on an £80 meal, or nearly 24%. Plenty of people do this by accident.

## Watch out for the card machine

The most common way people tip twice is the card terminal. Many machines ask "Add a tip?" with buttons for 10%, 12.5% or 15%, even when service is already on the bill. The prompt is generic. It doesn't know about the service charge.

Before you tap, check the bill for a line like "discretionary service 12.5%". If it's there, press "no tip" or "skip". This isn't rude. The staff are already being tipped through the service charge. Don't confuse it with a cover charge, a flat fee per person that doesn't have to go to staff ([cover charges in the UK](/blog/cover-charge-uk/)).

Also look out for "service not included" printed at the bottom of a bill. That means there's no service charge, and a tip is up to you.

## When an extra tip does make sense

- **Exceptional service.** Someone went out of their way: sorted a birthday cake, dealt brilliantly with a difficult table. A few pounds in cash to them is a nice thank you.
- **You want a specific person to get it.** A service charge is usually shared between the team. Cash handed to one server is generally theirs to keep, unless the restaurant pools cash tips.
- **The service charge was low.** If the bill added 5% and you'd normally tip 10%, topping up is reasonable.
- **You asked for the service charge to be removed** because you wanted to tip a different way. Then tip as you'd planned.

## When you shouldn't feel you have to

- **The service was poor.** You can ask for a discretionary service charge to be removed altogether. In a 2024 RSM UK survey of 2,000 consumers, 22% said they never pay the optional service charge. See [is service charge mandatory in the UK?](/blog/is-service-charge-mandatory-uk/)
- **You're splitting the bill.** Don't let one person add a tip on top for the whole table without asking. If the group wants to tip extra, agree it and [split it in proportion](/blog/split-service-charge-fairly/) like the service charge.

## A quick script

To the server, when the card machine asks:

> "Service is already on there, so no extra tip, thanks. It was lovely."

If you do want to add something:

> "Thanks so much, this is just for you."

## FAQ

### Do you tip on top of a 12.5% service charge?

No. The 12.5% is the tip. Only add more if you want to thank someone for exceptional service.

### Should I tip if service is included?

No, "service included" means a tip has already been added. Check the bill for the amount.

### Does the service charge go to the waiter?

It has to go to the staff. Since October 2024, employers in Great Britain must pass all tips and service charges they control to workers and share them fairly. It's usually shared across the team rather than given only to your server.

### Is it better to tip in cash?

If you want a particular person to get it, cash handed to them is the most direct way. Card tips and service charges are shared according to the restaurant's tipping policy.
