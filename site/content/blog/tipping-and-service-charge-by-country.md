In most of Europe, tipping is a small, optional thank you: round up or leave a few euros. The US and Canada are the exceptions, where around 15 to 20% is expected, and some countries put a charge on the bill instead, like Italy's coperto or Brazil's 10% service. Always read the bill before adding anything.

Below is what's on the bill and what's expected in each country, then how to split those extras fairly in a group. These are customs, not laws, except where we say otherwise.

## Quick table

| Country | What's often on the bill | What's expected on top |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Sales tax | 18 to 20% at sit-down restaurants |
| Canada | Sales tax (GST/HST/PST) | Around 15 to 20% at sit-down restaurants |
| United Kingdom | Often 12.5% service charge | Nothing if service is added. See [how much to tip in the UK](/blog/how-much-to-tip-uk/) |
| Spain | Rarely anything | Optional: small change, or a few euros |
| Portugal | Couvert, only if you ask for it or eat it | Optional: small change, up to about 10% |
| Italy | Coperto per person, sometimes servizio | Optional: round up |
| France | Service included in prices by law | Optional: a few euros |
| Germany, Austria | Nothing extra | Round up, or 5 to 10% |
| Nordics | Service built into prices | Optional: round up |
| Brazil | 10% service, optional | Nothing more |
| Mexico | Usually nothing | 10 to 15%, voluntary |

## United States

Tipping is part of how servers are paid, so it's expected. Toast, which processes payments for US restaurants, says the average tip at full service restaurants was 19.1% in the second quarter of 2026, and 15.8% at quick service places. That's card and digital tips only. Most people work the tip out on the amount before tax. Check for an automatic gratuity on large parties before adding your own.

## Canada

Tips at sit-down restaurants are expected, and norms are close to the US. Restaurants Canada has suggested working the tip out on the pre-tax subtotal. Card machines often suggest higher percentages, so you can type your own.

## Spain

Tipping isn't expected. Many people leave small change or round up at a bar or café. At a sit-down meal, a few euros, or about 5 to 10% if the service was good, is generous. Some tourist-area restaurants now print suggested tips on the bill. They're optional.

## Portugal

Tipping is optional and modest: small change, or up to about 10% at a smart restaurant. The thing to know is the couvert, the bread, olives or butter put on the table when you sit down. Under Portuguese law (Decree-Law 10/2015, article 135), nothing can be charged if you didn't ask for it or didn't eat it. If you don't want it, say so when it arrives. Consumer group DECO says that if you eat it, you can be charged.

## Italy

Two charges can appear:

- **Coperto:** a per person cover charge, often €1.50 to €4. It goes to the restaurant, not the staff, and should be shown on the menu.
- **Servizio:** a service charge, usually 10 to 15%, more common in tourist areas. If it's there, you don't need to tip.

Without servizio, rounding up or leaving a couple of euros is plenty.

## France

By law, prices in French restaurants and bars include service ("service compris"). The DGCCRF, France's consumer authority, says a tip on top is voluntary and no business can demand one. Leaving a few euros for good service is common but not expected.

## Germany and Austria

Tipping is expected but small: round up, or 5 to 10%. The custom is to tell the server the total you want to pay when they bring the card machine or take your cash. On a €43 bill you'd say "48, please" rather than leaving coins on the table.

## The Nordics

In Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Finland, service is built into prices and wages. Visit Sweden says it's common to round up, for example from 280 SEK to 300 SEK, and that any service charge will be clearly shown on the bill.

## Brazil

Most restaurants add 10% "taxa de serviço" to the bill. Under the 2017 tips law (Lei 13.419) that money goes to the staff, but paying it is up to you, and consumer bodies say the restaurant must tell you it's optional. Most people pay it and add nothing more. Some places now charge 12 to 15%; they should tell you before you order.

## Mexico

Profeco, Mexico's consumer agency, says tips are voluntary and can't be added to the bill without your agreement. By custom many people leave 10 to 15%.

## How to split extras fairly in any country

Extras come in two kinds, and they split differently.

- **Per person charges** (coperto, couvert you all ate, a cover charge): split equally among the people they apply to.
- **Percentage charges** (service, tips, tax): split in proportion to what each person ordered. If the service charge is 10% of the food, each person's 10% comes from their own food.

### Worked example: three friends in Rome

| | Ana | Ben | Cara | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food and drink | €22.00 | €30.00 | €18.00 | €70.00 |
| Coperto (€2.50 each) | €2.50 | €2.50 | €2.50 | €7.50 |
| Servizio 10% of food | €2.20 | €3.00 | €1.80 | €7.00 |
| **Pays** | **€26.70** | **€35.50** | **€22.30** | **€84.50** |

Split equally, everyone would pay €28.17: Cara would pay €5.87 more than her share, and Ben €7.33 less.

A tip on top is a group choice. If everyone agrees to round €84.50 up to €90, split the €5.50 in proportion too, or equally if the difference is only cents. More on this in [how to split service charge and tips fairly](/blog/split-service-charge-fairly/).

## FAQ

### Do you tip in Spain?
It isn't expected. Locals often leave small change or round up, and a few euros or about 5 to 10% is a generous thank you at a nicer restaurant. Check the bill first in case service has already been added.

### Is service included in Brazil?
Most restaurants add a 10% taxa de serviço to the bill. It goes to the staff, but it's optional, and the restaurant should tell you so. Most people pay it.

### What is coperto in Italy?
A per person cover charge for the table, bread and so on. It goes to the restaurant, not the staff, and it isn't a tip. If you're splitting, it belongs to each person who sat down.

### Should you tip in Portugal if service is on the bill?
No. If serviço is already on the bill, you don't need to add more. Otherwise small change or rounding up is normal, and up to about 10% at a smart restaurant.
