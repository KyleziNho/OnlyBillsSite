In Brazil, friends split a bill with Pix. One person pays, then sends each friend a **Pix Copia e Cola** code with that friend's amount inside. The friend copies it, opens their banking app, chooses Pix Copia e Cola, pastes and confirms. Nobody types an amount, so nobody gets it wrong.

Pix is the central bank's instant payment system, and almost everyone has it: press reports of central bank data put it at 187.8 million users at the end of October 2026. If you're living in Brazil, visiting friends there or splitting a bill with Brazilian friends, here's how it works.

## What is Pix Copia e Cola?

Every Pix QR code is a line of text underneath. "Copia e Cola" (copy and paste) is that text: a long string starting "000201" that holds the recipient's Pix key, their name and, optionally, the amount. The central bank's rules say the copied text must be identical to what's in the QR code, so the two work the same way. Copia e Cola exists for the obvious problem: you can't scan a QR code that's on your own screen.

You could just send your Pix key instead, and plenty of people do. But then each friend types their amount, and with four friends owing four different sums, someone will send the wrong one.

There's a safety check built in. Before the payer confirms, their bank shows the real name of the key's owner, taken from the central Pix directory. If the name looks wrong, they don't pay.

## How to send a Pix request from your bank

Most Brazilian banking apps have a **Cobrar** (charge) or **Receber** (receive) option in the Pix area. In Nubank, for example, you go to Pix, then Cobrar, type the amount and create a QR code you can show or share as a link or code. Other banks have something similar under a different name.

Each request holds one amount, so four friends means four requests. It works, it just takes a few minutes.

## Which Pix key should you share?

A Pix key can be your CPF (tax number), phone number, email or a **random key** (chave aleatória): a long string of letters and numbers your bank generates. For paying friends back, use the random key. It doesn't reveal your CPF or phone number to a group chat, and you can delete it and make a new one whenever you like. You create it in the Pix keys section of your banking app.

## The 10% service charge

Most Brazilian bars and restaurants add a 10% service charge (serviço, or "os 10% do garçom"). It's customary but optional: you can ask for it to be taken off. If the table pays it, the fair way is for each person to pay 10% of what they had, not an equal slice.

## A worked example

Four friends at a bar. The bill is R$ 252.01: R$ 229.10 of food and drink plus R$ 22.91 service. Júlia pays it all by card. The two shared snack plates (R$ 72.00) are split four ways, R$ 18.00 each.

| Person | What they had | Subtotal | 10% | Owes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Júlia (paid) | 3 draught beers, water, share of plates | R$ 61.70 | R$ 6.17 | R$ 67.87 |
| Lucas | 4 draught beers, share of plates | R$ 69.60 | R$ 6.96 | R$ 76.56 |
| Bia | 2 beers, a caipirinha, share of plates | R$ 65.80 | R$ 6.58 | R$ 72.38 |
| Rafa | 2 soft drinks, share of plates | R$ 32.00 | R$ 3.20 | R$ 35.20 |

An even split would be R$ 63.00 each, so Rafa, who drank soft drinks, would pay R$ 27.80 more than his share. Júlia sends three Copia e Cola codes: R$ 76.56, R$ 72.38 and R$ 35.20.

## If you don't have a Brazilian bank account

Pix needs an account with a Brazilian bank or payment institution, so most visitors can't pay or receive it directly. If you're visiting, pay your share in cash, ask the friend who paid whether a Wise, Revolut or PayPal transfer suits them, or pay the restaurant your part by card before the bill is settled. Some apps aimed at visitors let you pay Pix codes from a foreign card; check the fee and exchange rate before you use one.

## Two limits worth knowing

Bar bills tend to get settled late. Between 8pm and 6am, the default Pix limit for individuals is R$ 1,000, which you can ask your bank to change. And on a new phone the bank doesn't yet recognise, Pix payments are capped at R$ 200 each and R$ 1,000 a day until the device is approved. One person's share of a dinner rarely hits either.

Pix by tapping your phone (Pix por aproximação) works on Android phones with NFC. On iPhone it still wasn't available when we checked in November 2026; Brazil's competition authority, CADE, is investigating Apple's control of the iPhone's NFC chip. On iPhone, pay by QR code or Copia e Cola.

## FAQ

### What is Pix Copia e Cola?
It's the text behind a Pix QR code. You copy it, open your banking app, choose Pix Copia e Cola and paste. The recipient's details, and the amount if the code includes one, fill in automatically. It's how you pay a Pix code on the same phone it arrived on.

### Which Pix key should I share with friends?
A random key (chave aleatória). It works like any other Pix key but doesn't reveal your CPF or phone number, and you can delete it and create a new one in your banking app at any time.

### Do you have to pay the 10% service charge in Brazil?
No. The 10% is customary but optional, even when it's printed on the bill. If your group pays it, split it in proportion: each person pays 10% of what they had.

### Can a foreigner use Pix?
Only with an account at a Brazilian bank or payment institution. Visitors usually pay friends back in cash or by an international transfer or payment app. Some services let you pay Pix codes from a foreign card, so compare their fees and exchange rates first.

### Can you pay Pix by tapping an iPhone?
Not as of November 2026. Pix by tapping (Pix por aproximação) works on Android phones with NFC. On iPhone, use a QR code or Pix Copia e Cola.
