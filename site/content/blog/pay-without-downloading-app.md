Your friends shouldn't need a new app to pay you £23.40 for dinner. In the UK they can pay from a link in their browser (by card, Apple Pay or bank transfer, depending on the link), or by a bank transfer from the banking app they already have. Splitting the bill itself can work the same way: friends open a web page or an iPhone App Clip, tick what they had, and see what they owe.

Here's what each of those is, how they work, and what you're sharing when you use them.

## Why "download this app" doesn't work at a table

Ask six people to download an app, make an account and confirm an email at 10.45pm, and you'll be lucky if half of them bother. The rest will say "just tell me what I owe". The split falls back on one person doing it all, which is the thing the app was meant to avoid.

It's also a lot to ask for a one-off. The person who paid uses the app. Everyone else just wants to see a number and pay it.

## Three ways to pay without a new app

| Method | What your friend does | Do they need an account? | What they see about you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank payment link (Monzo, Revolut, Starling) | Taps the link, pays on a web page | No account at your bank needed | Your name and the amount |
| Bank transfer | Types your sort code and account number into their own banking app | Their usual bank only | Your name, sort code and account number |
| PayPal link | Pays on PayPal's page | A PayPal account may be needed | Your PayPal name |

### Bank payment links

Most UK challenger banks give you a personal link that anyone can pay.

- **Monzo:** when you split a bill with someone not on Monzo, you can share a request link with them.
- **Revolut:** a Revolut.me link can be paid by card or Revolut transfer. Revolut's help pages say card payments via links are typically capped at £250 a week, and a request expires after 10 days.
- **Starling:** a Settle Up link opens a secure web page where people pay with a UK debit card. Starling says you can receive up to £250 a day this way. Its help pages also mention Apple Pay and Google Pay.

The big privacy plus: Starling points out that with a link there's no need to share account details or set up a new payee. Full steps for each bank are in [how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling](/blog/split-bill-monzo-revolut-starling/).

### Bank transfer

Still the universal option. Your friend pays from any UK banking app, by Faster Payments. The trade off is that you're handing out your sort code and account number. That's normal between friends, but you might not want it in a 15 person group chat. Here's [how long a bank transfer takes](/blog/how-long-does-bank-transfer-take-uk/) and why some get held.

## What is a web link split?

Instead of an app, the split lives on a web page. The person who paid shares a link or QR code, and everyone opens it in their phone's browser. They pick their name, tick their items, and see their share. Nothing gets installed. It works on any phone with a browser, iPhone or Android.

Several apps now do this. Splitwise launched Itemize in the US and UK in October 2026, where friends scan a QR code and claim their items without the app or an account. OnlyBills works the same way: the host scans the receipt and shares a live table, and friends join from a link or QR code. There's a side by side in [OnlyBills vs Splitwise](/vs/splitwise/), and the guest's view is walked through in [claim your items without the app](/how-to/claim-your-items-without-the-app/).

## What is an App Clip?

An App Clip is a small part of an iPhone app that opens for one task, without downloading the whole app. Apple describes App Clips as "small parts of an app that allow you to complete a task quickly and without downloading the entire app".

You'll have met them without noticing: paying for parking from a QR code on a sign, or ordering at a café table. They open from:

- a QR code or Apple's App Clip Code
- an NFC tag
- a link in Messages
- a banner in Safari
- suggestions from Siri or Apple Maps

An App Clip feels like a native iPhone screen rather than a web page. Then it gets out of the way. Apple's support page says data associated with an App Clip is deleted from your device after 10 days of non-use (30 days if you signed in with Sign in with Apple), and App Clips are removed automatically after 30 days of non-use.

**To remove one yourself:** open the App Library, scroll to the bottom of the list in search, touch and hold the App Clip, and tap Remove App Clip. You can turn App Clips off entirely in Screen Time's content restrictions. There's more on how they work, what they can see and how to turn them off in [what is an App Clip on iPhone?](/blog/what-is-an-app-clip/)

## Is it safe to pay from a link?

Generally yes, if you check three things before you pay:

1. **You know who sent it.** A link from a friend in your group chat is very different from a text from an unknown number.
2. **The name on the payment page is the person you owe.** Bank link pages show the payee's name.
3. **The amount matches what you agreed.** If you split by item, you should be able to see where your number came from.

If you get a payment link out of the blue claiming you owe money, don't pay it. Ask the person directly, using a number or chat you already have.

For what someone can and can't do with your sort code, and how scam refunds work, see [is it safe to share your bank details](/blog/is-it-safe-to-share-bank-details/).

## What do you share when you join a split?

This depends on the app, so check its privacy policy. For OnlyBills, which we make, the privacy policy says a live table stores the receipt's items, the display names people type in and who claimed what, for the life of the shared session, so everyone at the table can see it update. A receipt image shared to the table may be stored too. Guests don't create an account. The host can end the table, which removes it. Payment happens with the host's bank or PayPal, not inside OnlyBills.

## FAQ

### Can I pay someone back without their banking app?
Yes. Monzo, Revolut and Starling all have payment links that people at other banks can pay, usually by card on a web page. Or send a bank transfer from your own banking app.

### What is an App Clip on iPhone?
A small part of an app that opens for one task, like paying or ordering, without downloading the full app. It opens from a QR code, NFC tag, link in Messages or Safari banner, and is removed automatically after 30 days of non-use.

### Is it safe to pay from a payment link?
It's as safe as the person who sent it. Check it came from someone you know, the name on the page is theirs and the amount is what you agreed. Don't pay unexpected links from unknown numbers.

### Do my friends need an account to split a bill with me?
Not with apps that use web links or App Clips. Splitwise Itemize and OnlyBills both let friends claim their items from a QR code without the app or an account.
