Since 1 October 2024, the staff get it. In England, Scotland and Wales, any tip, gratuity or service charge that a business controls must be passed to its workers in full, shared fairly, and paid by the end of the following month. That covers service charges on the bill and tips added by card. A per person cover charge is different: see [cover charges in the UK](/blog/cover-charge-uk/). Cash handed straight to a server, which the business never touches, is generally theirs to keep.

This guide is about where your money goes. Whether you have to pay a service charge in the first place is a separate question, covered in [is service charge mandatory in the UK?](/blog/is-service-charge-mandatory-uk/)

## What changed on 1 October 2024?

The Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 came into force, along with a statutory code of practice on distributing tips fairly. It added a new part to the Employment Rights Act 1996 (sections 27C onwards). Before it, there was no legal duty for employers to pass on tips paid by card or added as a service charge, and some kept a share.

The main rules for employers:

- **Pass on all of it.** The full amount of qualifying tips must go to workers. The only deductions allowed are normal tax and National Insurance (Acas).
- **No card fees.** The law counts what the customer paid, ignoring any deductions, so a business can't keep a slice to cover card processing (section 27C(6)).
- **Share it fairly.** Tips must be allocated fairly between workers, taking the code of practice into account (section 27D).
- **Pay it on time.** By the end of the month after the month the customer paid. A tip left in July must be paid out by the end of August (section 27G).
- **Not instead of wages.** Tips can't be used to top up pay to the minimum wage (Acas).

The government estimated that about £200 million a year would go to workers instead of being kept by employers.

## Cash, card or service charge: where does each tip go?

| How you tip | Covered by the law? | Who gets it |
|---|---|---|
| Service charge on the bill | Yes | Shared between staff under the tipping policy |
| Tip added on the card machine | Yes | Shared between staff under the tipping policy |
| Cash left on the table and pooled by the business | Yes | Shared between staff under the tipping policy |
| Cash handed to a server, kept by them, business not involved | No | That server |
| Tip paid through an app straight to a worker | No | That worker |
| Tip to a self-employed person (some taxi drivers, freelancers) | No | That person |

The test is whether the employer has "control or significant influence" over how the money is shared (section 27C). If it does, the rules apply, even if the tip was cash.

## Who shares in the tips?

The law says "fairly", not "equally" and not "only your server". Many restaurants share tips between front of house and kitchen staff, sometimes by hours worked, role or a points system. The code of practice gives employers factors to weigh and leaves the details to them.

Some businesses use a **tronc**: a pooled fund shared out by a "troncmaster", sometimes independent of the employer. That's allowed, but the employer still has to make sure it's set up and run fairly, and step in if it isn't (Acas).

So if you tip by card, expect it to be split between the team who looked after you, including people you didn't see.

## Policies, records and what staff can check

These rights belong to workers rather than customers, but they explain how the rules are enforced:

- **Written policy.** Any business that receives tips more than occasionally must have a written tipping policy, available to all workers (section 27I).
- **Records.** Employers must keep records of tips and how they were shared for three years (section 27J).
- **Right to ask.** A worker can request those records, once every three months, and the employer has four weeks to respond (section 27J).
- **Tribunal.** If tips aren't shared fairly or paid on time, a worker can complain to an employment tribunal, normally within 12 months (section 27K).

Customers have no right to see the policy, but nothing stops you asking.

## What's the status of the code of practice?

As of October 2026, the original **2024 statutory code of practice** is still the one employers must follow.

Changes are on the way. The Employment Rights Act 2025 will add a duty for employers to consult their workers when they write or review their tipping policy, review it at least every three years, and share an anonymised summary of what workers said. A revised code to go with it was laid before Parliament in June 2026, then **withdrawn on 29 July 2026** so a new public consultation could be held. That consultation ran from 19 August to 29 September 2026, and the government is now analysing the responses. GOV.UK says employers should keep following the existing code in the meantime.

None of this changes the core rule: tips and service charges still go to staff in full.

## Does it apply everywhere in the UK?

No. It covers England, Scotland and Wales. Employment law is devolved in Northern Ireland, and this Act doesn't apply there.

## How to make sure the staff get your tip

- **Paying the service charge is fine.** Since 2024 it's meant to go to the team, so you don't need to remove it and tip separately to be sure.
- **Want one person to get it?** Give them cash directly. If the business doesn't pool cash, it's theirs.
- **Not sure?** Ask: "Does the service charge go to the staff here?" Staff will usually tell you straight. The revised draft code encourages, but doesn't require, businesses to share their tipping policy with customers.
- **Don't tip twice by accident.** If service is already on the bill, you can skip the card machine's tip prompt. See [should you tip on top of a service charge?](/blog/should-you-tip-on-top-of-service-charge/)

If you work in hospitality and think your tips aren't being passed on, Acas has free, confidential advice.

## Splitting the tip in a group

When a group shares a bill, the service charge or tip should be split in proportion to what each person had, not equally. Someone with a £15 order shouldn't tip the same as someone with a £45 one. ## FAQ

### Do waiters get the service charge in the UK?

Yes. Since 1 October 2024, employers in England, Scotland and Wales must pass all service charges they control to workers, in full and fairly shared. It's usually split across the team, not only your server.

### Can a restaurant keep part of the tips for card fees?

No. The law counts the full amount the customer paid, so card processing fees can't be deducted. Only normal tax and National Insurance can come off.

### Are cash tips covered by the tipping law?

Only if the business collects or controls them, for example by pooling them. Cash handed directly to a worker and kept by them isn't covered, and is theirs.

### When do staff get their tips?

By the end of the month after the month you paid. A tip from 20 October must be paid out by 30 November.

### Does the tipping law apply in Northern Ireland?

No. The Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 applies in England, Scotland and Wales.
