An App Clip is a small part of an iPhone app that opens when you need it, without installing the full app. You scan a QR code, tap an NFC tag or tap a link, a card slides up from the bottom of the screen, and you tap Open. Apple's examples are renting a bike, paying for parking and ordering food. When you're done, the App Clip doesn't land on your Home Screen, and Apple says your iPhone removes it after 30 days if you don't use it again.

Here's how they work, what they can and can't see, and how to find or delete the ones you've used. Everything below comes from Apple's own iPhone User Guide, its App Clips privacy page and its developer documentation, as of November 2026.

## How does an App Clip work?

A business that has an app on the App Store can offer one small task from it as an App Clip. Apple's developer documentation uses a doughnut shop: the full app lets you order, save favourites and collect rewards, while the App Clip only lets you order. It's kept small so it loads quickly, even on weak signal.

When you open one, your iPhone downloads just that small piece and runs it. Some App Clips let you sign in with Apple and pay with Apple Pay, so you don't type a card number or make an account. If you like it, some App Clips show a banner at the top of the screen that takes you to the full app.

Developers have to keep App Clips small. Apple's developer documentation caps an App Clip at 15 MB on iOS 16, and allows up to 100 MB on iOS 17 and later in certain cases.

## How do you open an App Clip?

According to Apple's iPhone User Guide, you can get an App Clip from:

| Where | What you do |
|---|---|
| A QR code or App Clip Code | Scan it with the Camera app, or Code Scanner in Control Center |
| An NFC tag or NFC App Clip Code | Hold your iPhone near it |
| Maps | Tap the App Clip link on a place's information card (where supported) |
| Safari or Messages | Tap the App Clip link |

When the card appears, tap Open (or Play, for a game demo). An App Clip Code is Apple's own round code, a bit like a QR code, made for App Clips. Some can be scanned with the camera, and some also have an NFC tag inside so you can just hold your phone near them.

## Is an App Clip safe? What can it see?

App Clips have tighter limits than full apps. Apple's developer documentation says App Clips:

- **can't track you** across other apps and websites, and can't ask for permission to
- **can't read your Contacts, Calendar, Photos, Files, Messages, Reminders or Health data**, or your motion and fitness data
- **can't ask for location all the time.** If one asks for your location and you agree, Apple's privacy page says the access lasts one day, and only while you're using it
- **can't share data with any other app** except their own full app
- **can't run in the background**
- **can only send notifications for up to 8 hours** after each time you open one. You can turn this off on the App Clip card, or for all App Clips in Settings > Notifications > App Clips

Apple also tells developers to keep in-app purchases for the full app. Paying for the thing itself, like the parking, with Apple Pay is allowed.

## What do Apple and the business learn when you use one?

Apple's App Clips privacy page explains both sides:

- **Apple:** your iPhone tells Apple which App Clip to download, but not which link, tag or code you scanned to get it.
- **The business:** the developer may collect information through the App Clip itself, such as the name you type in. Their privacy policy and the full app's App Store page say what they do with it. If you later install the full app, your App Clip data moves across to it.
- **Location check:** an App Clip opened from a QR code or NFC tag may ask to confirm your location. That only tells it "yes" or "no" for whether you're near the place the code was meant to be, not where you are. It's there to spot a code that's been moved.

Data from an App Clip is deleted from your iPhone after 10 days of not using it, or 30 days if you signed in with Sign in with Apple.

:::note
A QR code can be stuck over the real one, so look at the card before you tap Open. It shows the name of the app. If it isn't the one you expected, close it.
:::

## How do you find App Clips you've used?

App Clips don't appear on your Home Screen. To see the ones you've opened:

1. Swipe left past your last Home Screen page to open App Library.
2. Tap the search field at the top.
3. Scroll to the end of the alphabetical list. Your App Clips are listed at the bottom.

Tap one to open it again.

## How do you delete an App Clip?

1. Open App Library and tap the search field at the top.
2. Type the App Clip's name, or scroll down to App Clips at the bottom.
3. Touch and hold the App Clip, then tap Remove App Clip.

If you don't, Apple says App Clips are removed automatically after 30 days of not being used.

## Can you turn App Clips off?

Yes. Go to Settings > Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions > Content Restrictions, tap App Clips and choose Don't Allow. Apple says this also deletes any App Clips already on your iPhone.

## What's the difference between an App Clip and an app?

| | App Clip | Full app |
|---|---|---|
| Install from the App Store | No | Yes |
| On your Home Screen | No, only in App Library | Yes |
| Size | Small, with a strict limit | Can be much larger |
| Tracking across apps | Not allowed | Only if you allow it |
| Access to Contacts, Photos, Health | No | If you allow it |
| Notifications | Up to 8 hours after each use | If you allow them |
| Removed when unused | Yes, after 30 days | No |
| In-app purchases | Apple says keep them for the full app | Yes |

## What are App Clips used for?

The tasks Apple describes are short and done on the spot: rent a bike, pay for parking, order food. Apple's documentation also mentions demo App Clips, where a game lets you play the first level, or a fitness app gives you one free workout, before asking you to install the full app.

They suit anything where you'd never bother to download an app for a two minute job, such as a scooter you'll use once or a car park in a town you're visiting.

## FAQ

### Do App Clips cost anything?
Opening an App Clip is free. Apple tells developers to keep in-app purchases for the full app, but some App Clips let you pay for the thing itself, like parking or food, with Apple Pay.

### Do App Clips work on Android?
No, App Clips are an Apple feature for iPhone. Many businesses that use them also offer a web page for other phones. OnlyBills, for example, opens in the browser on Android.

### Is an App Clip the same as downloading the app?
No. Only a small part of the app runs, it doesn't go on your Home Screen, and your iPhone removes it after 30 days if you don't use it. You can still get the full app from the App Store if you want it.

### Can an App Clip see my photos or contacts?
No. Apple's developer documentation says App Clips can't access Contacts, Calendar, Photos, Files, Messages, Reminders or Health data, and can't track you across apps.

### Why did an App Clip open when I scanned a QR code?
The business linked that QR code to its App Clip. If you'd rather not use it, close the card and remove it from App Library.
