Usually, yes. Your sort code and account number are what people need to pay you, and Lloyds puts it plainly: "It's usually fine to share your account number and sort code. But never share them with anyone who gets in touch out of the blue." The things to keep to yourself are your passwords, PIN and security codes.

Here's what someone can and can't do with your details, the checks UK banks now run, what happens if a payment goes wrong, and when a payment link is the tidier choice. Rules and figures are as of November 2026.

## What can someone do with your sort code and account number?

Mostly, pay you. Those two numbers identify your account, so anyone sending you money needs them. HSBC says it's "generally safe to share these details to receive a payment", and to share them only with people and organisations you trust.

What they can't do is log in to your banking or send money out of your account. That needs your app, your passwords or your security codes, which is why HSBC tells you to "never share confidential information such as your online banking passwords, your PIN, or other security codes".

The one kind of payment that's collected from your account rather than sent by you is a Direct Debit. If one is ever taken in error, the Direct Debit Guarantee says you're entitled to "a full and immediate refund" from your bank, and you can cancel a Direct Debit at any time by contacting your bank. Check your statement now and then, and report anything you don't recognise.

### So who should you share them with?

| Share freely | Think twice | Never share |
|---|---|---|
| Friends and family paying you back | A group chat with people you don't know well | Your PIN |
| Your employer, for wages | Anyone who contacts you out of the blue asking for them | Online banking passwords |
| Organisations you trust, for refunds or Direct Debits | | One-time security codes |

## What is Confirmation of Payee?

It's the name check your bank runs when someone sets up a new payee. Pay.UK describes it as "an account name-checking service designed to help reduce misdirected payments." Pay.UK says more than 300 organisations use it, with over 2 million checks a day.

When your friend types in your name, sort code and account number, their bank checks the name against your account and shows one of these:

- **Match:** the details are right, so they can pay.
- **Close match:** they're shown the actual name on the account, so they can fix it or check with you.
- **No match:** the name is wrong, and they should check the details with you.
- **Unable to check:** the check couldn't be done, for example if the other bank doesn't offer it.

**What this means for you:** when you send your details, give the name exactly as it appears on your account. "Sam Okafor" might come back as a close match if the account says "Samuel Okafor".

Copy and paste this:

> Name: Samuel Okafor
>
> Sort code: 12-34-56
>
> Account: 12345678
>
> That's my name as the bank has it, so the name check should come up as a match.

## What if you're scammed into sending money?

Since 7 October 2024, UK banks have had to refund most victims of authorised push payment (APP) scams. That's when you're tricked into sending money to a fraudster. As of November 2026, the main rules are:

- **Covered:** UK payments by Faster Payments or CHAPS.
- **Limit:** up to £85,000 per claim, set by the Payment Systems Regulator.
- **Excess:** banks may take off up to £100, but some waive it. Nationwide, for example, says it waives the excess.
- **Deadline:** report it within 13 months of the last payment.
- **Speed:** valid claims are usually refunded within five business days, or up to 35 if the bank needs more information.

**Not covered:** card payments, cash, international payments, and disputes with a genuine seller, according to Nationwide's summary of the rules. A bank can also refuse if it can show you were grossly negligent, for example by ignoring a clear scam warning.

Banks also have more time to stop suspicious payments. Under FCA guidance on rules introduced in October 2024, a bank with reasonable grounds to suspect fraud can hold a payment for up to four business days. Normal transfer times are in [how long a bank transfer takes](/blog/how-long-does-bank-transfer-take-uk/).

If you think you've been scammed, Take Five, the UK Finance campaign, says to contact your bank straight away. You can also report it at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040.

## How do you check a payment link is genuine?

A payment link is only as trustworthy as the person who sent it. Before you pay, check:

1. **Who sent it.** It should come from your friend, in a chat you already have with them. Be wary of a "new number" message asking for money.
2. **The web address.** The real ones are monzo.me, revolut.me, settleup.starlingbank.com and paypal.me. A lookalike address is a warning sign.
3. **The name on the page.** Monzo, Revolut, Starling and PayPal pages show who you're paying. It should be your friend.
4. **The amount.** It should match what you agreed. If you split by item, you should be able to see how they got the number.

If anything doesn't fit, stop and message or call them on a number you already have. Take Five's advice is to stop and ask yourself whether it could be fake. It's always fine to say no to a request or ignore it.

## Why is a payment link tidier than bank details in a group chat?

Bank details posted in a group chat stay in the chat history for everyone in it, including people you've only met once. A request link does the same job without that. Starling says people paying a Settle Up link "won't need a Starling account to make a payment, or even your account details."

Links also suit a split. Each friend pays their own amount by card or Apple Pay on a web page, and nobody has to add you as a new payee. Every way to pay without bank details is in [how to pay a friend back without their bank details](/blog/pay-friend-without-bank-details-uk/).

One exception: a link that *sends* money (like Monzo's "Pay anyone using a link") can be claimed by whoever opens it first. Monzo warns not to add those to group messages. Request links, which people pay into, are the ones to share with a group.

## FAQ

### Can someone take money from my account with my sort code and account number?
Not by transfer. Sending money out needs your banking login, passwords or security codes. Direct Debits are collected from your account, but they're covered by the Direct Debit Guarantee, which gives a full and immediate refund if one is taken in error.

### Is it safe to send bank details over WhatsApp?
Generally, to people you know. Lloyds even lets you share your details from its app to messaging apps, and says to only send them to people you know. In a large group, a payment link keeps your details out of the chat history.

### What does "close match" mean when paying someone?
It's a Confirmation of Payee result. The name you typed is similar to the name on the account, and your bank shows you the actual name so you can check it before you pay.

### Will my bank refund me if I pay a scammer?
Often, yes, if you paid by Faster Payments or CHAPS. Since 7 October 2024 banks must refund most APP scam victims up to £85,000 per claim, and may take off an excess of up to £100. Report it within 13 months.
