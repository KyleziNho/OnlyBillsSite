OnlyBills splits a bill in two stages. First, each item goes to whoever had it, and a shared item is split equally between the people who shared it. Then service charge, tips, discounts and other extras are spread across everyone in proportion to what their items came to. The shares always add up to the bill total exactly, to the penny.

## How is a shared dish split?

Equally between the people who shared it. If three people tap the garlic naan on a live table, or you assign it to three people yourself, each pays a third. A £6.00 naan becomes £2.00 each.

When a split doesn't divide into whole pennies, OnlyBills hands out the odd penny so the parts still add up to the item's price. A £10.00 bottle of wine shared three ways comes out as £3.34, £3.33 and £3.33.

## What about items ordered more than once?

A line like "Kingfisher ×2" or "Garlic Naan ×2" is one line with a quantity.

- **Assigning yourself:** tapping the line opens "Split this item". Choose **Who ordered what** to say how many each person had (one beer for Sam, one for Priya), or **Share equally** to split the whole line between the people you pick.
- **On a live table:** everyone who taps the line shares it. In the OnlyBills app and the App Clip, the person who tapped it can also say "How many did you have?".

## How is service charge split?

In proportion to what each person's items came to, not equally. Someone whose food was £20 pays twice as much service as someone whose food was £10. That's how the service was earned, so it's the fair way to share it.

A percentage service charge (12.5% is common in UK restaurants) is worked out on the items after any bill-wide discount, and recalculated if you edit the items. Lines that are already inside the prices, such as "VAT included", are shown for information and don't add anything.

## How are tips split?

The same way as service: in proportion to what each person ordered. If a tip is printed on the receipt, OnlyBills reads it as a tip line. To add one yourself, tap **Add charge or tip** on the check page, choose Tip, and enter an amount or a percentage.

## How are discounts split?

It depends what the discount is for:

- **A discount on one item** (like "2 for 1" printed under a dish) only comes off that item, so only the people who had it get the saving.
- **A discount on the whole bill** (like 10% off) is shared in proportion, just like service.

If the scan finds a discount but can't tell what it was for, the check page asks "Which item was this for?" with each item and **Whole bill** to choose from.

## A worked example

Amy, Ben and Cal have dinner. The receipt:

| Item | Price | Who had it |
|---|---|---|
| Lamb curry | £14.00 | Amy |
| Chicken curry | £12.00 | Ben |
| Dal | £8.00 | Cal |
| Garlic naan | £6.00 | All three |
| Service charge 12.5% | £5.00 | |
| **Total** | **£45.00** | |

**Step 1: items.** The naan is £2.00 each. So Amy's items come to £16.00, Ben's to £14.00 and Cal's to £10.00. That's £40.00 of food.

**Step 2: extras in proportion.** Amy had 40% of the food (£16 of £40), Ben 35% and Cal 25%. The £5.00 service splits the same way: £2.00, £1.75 and £1.25.

| Person | Items | Service | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amy | £16.00 | £2.00 | **£18.00** |
| Ben | £14.00 | £1.75 | **£15.75** |
| Cal | £10.00 | £1.25 | **£11.25** |
| **Total** | £40.00 | £5.00 | **£45.00** |

Split equally, each would pay £15.00. Cal would be £3.75 out of pocket for food Amy ate.

### With a 10% discount

Say the restaurant takes 10% off the bill. The food drops from £40.00 to £36.00, and 12.5% service on £36.00 is £4.50, so the total is £40.50. Shared in proportion, Amy pays £16.20, Ben £14.18 and Cal £10.12. Ben and Cal's exact shares were £14.175 and £10.125, so one of them takes the extra penny and the three shares add up to £40.50 exactly.

## FAQ

### Why not just split the service charge equally?
Because then light eaters subsidise big orders. Sharing service in proportion means everyone pays service on what they actually had.

### What if the shares don't add up to the bill?
They always do. OnlyBills works in whole pennies and hands out any odd penny so that every person's share added together equals the bill total.

### Can I see how my share was worked out?
Yes. On a live table, tap **Show breakdown** under your share to see each item and your part of the extras. On the summary, tap a person to see their items.

### What happens to items nobody claimed?
On a live table, the host can tap **Split what's left evenly** to share them. Anything still unclaimed when the host finishes counts as the host's, since they paid the restaurant.
