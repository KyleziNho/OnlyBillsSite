How much should you tip in the UK?

In UK restaurants, 10% to 12.5% is normal if there's no service charge, and nothing extra if there is. Here's what's normal in pubs, cafés, taxis and more.

In a UK restaurant, a tip of 10% to 12.5% is normal for good table service, but only if a service charge hasn't already been added. If the bill already includes a service charge, you don't need to tip on top. In pubs, cafés and taxis, tipping is optional and usually small: round up, or leave nothing.

Tipping in the UK is never compulsory. It's a thank you, not part of the price, and plenty of people don't tip at all.

These are common practice, not rules. Tip more if someone went out of their way, and less or nothing if the service was poor.

Restaurants

If there's no service charge, 10% is the usual baseline and 12.5% is common in cities, especially London. On a £64 dinner for two, that's £6.40 to £8. Most people round to something tidy, like £7 or £8.

If there's a service charge, that is the tip. Check the bottom of the bill before you add anything, because it's easy to tip twice. Look for "service charge", "discretionary service" or "optional gratuity".

In London, 12.5% has long been the standard service charge, but Time Out reported in 2025 that 15% is now "not uncommon" on London bills. If you see 15% and think it's too much, you can ask for it to be changed or removed (more on that in is service charge mandatory in the UK?).

How much do people actually leave? A 2023 YouGov survey put the average restaurant tip in Britain at 6.1%. About half (46.9%) leave 5% to 10%, 15.8% leave more than 10%, and 20.6% don't tip at all. So if you leave 10%, you're more generous than most.

What about poor service? You don't have to tip, and you can ask for a discretionary service charge to come off. Which? (2025) found 26% of people had tipped in the last year despite bad service, mostly because it felt expected or they'd have been embarrassed not to.

Pubs and bars

At the bar, tipping isn't expected. You order, you pay, that's it. Some people offer the bar staff a drink ("and one for yourself"), and the staff may take the price of a drink as a tip instead. It's a nice gesture, not an obligation.

In a gastropub or bar with table service, it's closer to a restaurant. If a service charge hasn't been added, 10% is a fair tip for good service.

Cafés and coffee shops

Tipping in cafés is optional. Many people leave nothing, some leave loose change or round up. Card machines in cafés increasingly suggest a tip before you pay. You can press "no tip" or "skip" without feeling bad about it.

If it's a sit down café with table service and you've had a proper meal, some people tip as they would in a restaurant.

Food delivery

Tipping delivery drivers is optional and many people don't. Which? (2025) found nearly half (46%) of people hadn't tipped a delivery driver or courier in the past year, and only 11% tipped every time.

If you do, a pound or two is typical, more in bad weather or for a big order. Tipping through the app is usually the most reliable way for it to reach the driver; check the app's own explanation of where tips go.

Taxis and ride apps

For a black cab or local taxi, most people round up to the nearest pound or few pounds. Some add about 10% for a longer trip or help with heavy bags.

For Uber and similar apps, tipping is optional and done in the app afterwards. Which? (2025) found a third (34%) of people never tip a taxi or Uber driver, and only 19% tip on every journey.

Hairdressers and barbers

Around 10% is common, or a few pounds for a simple cut. If a junior washed your hair, some people give them a pound or two separately.

Plenty of people don't tip at the salon. Which? (2025) found 28% tipped their hairdresser every time and 26% tipped their barber on every visit, while 38% never tipped either. Of those who tipped a hairdresser, 80% used cash.

Does the tip actually go to the staff?

Since 1 October 2024, the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 has required employers in Great Britain to pass all tips, gratuities and service charges they control to their workers, without deductions, and to share them out fairly. That includes tips paid by card and discretionary service charges. It doesn't make a service charge compulsory for you, though: our guide to whether service charge is mandatory covers your rights and how to ask for it to come off.

Cash you hand directly to a person, which the business has no say over, is generally theirs to keep, though some places pool cash tips by agreement. If you want to know how a restaurant shares its tips, you can ask.

Which? (2025) found 31% of people who'd tipped in a restaurant weren't confident about who would receive it, so if it matters to you, asking is fair.

Tipping in a group

When a group tips, split the tip in proportion to what each person spent, not equally. If you had a £15 main and your friend had a £45 three course dinner, a 10% tip is £1.50 for you and £4.50 for them. The same goes for a service charge. Questions people ask

Is it rude not to tip in the UK?

No. Tipping is optional everywhere in the UK. In restaurants it's polite to tip for good service if no service charge has been added, but nobody is obliged to, and around a fifth of people never tip after a meal out.

Should I tip if there's a service charge?

No, you don't need to. The service charge is the tip. Only add more if the service was exceptional and you want to.

How much do you tip on a £100 bill in the UK?

£10 to £12.50 if no service charge has been added. If a 12.5% service charge is already on the bill, nothing more is needed.

Do you tip in pubs in the UK?

Not when you order at the bar. In pubs with table service and food, a tip of about 10% is normal if no service charge has been added.

Is it better to tip in cash or by card?

Since October 2024, card tips and service charges the business controls must legally be passed to staff in full. Cash handed directly to a person usually stays with them. Either is fine; if it matters to you, ask how the venue shares tips.
