How to avoid an awkward bill split

Most awkward bill splits are decided before anyone orders. What to say before the meal, at the table and afterwards, with messages you can copy and a £ example.

The way to avoid an awkward bill split is to decide how you're paying before anyone orders, say out loud who's paying the restaurant, and send everyone their share before you leave. The awkward bit is almost never the money itself. It's the silence when the bill lands and nobody wants to be the one who speaks first.

You're not imagining it. In a 2021 survey of 2,000 diners for comparethemarket.com, 43% said they feel awkward about how to pay when they eat out, and 13% said they'd fallen out with someone over a bill (comparethemarket, 2021).

This guide takes the evening in three parts: before the meal, at the table and afterwards. Each has a message you can copy.

Why do bill splits get awkward?

Three things cause nearly all of it:

  • Nobody agreed the method. Half the table assumes you're splitting equally, the other half assumes you're paying for what you had.
  • Orders were very different. Someone had a starter and tap water, someone else had steak and half a bottle of wine.
  • Somebody has to ask for money. Whoever paid ends up chasing friends, and nobody likes doing that.

The fixes are boring, and that's why they work. You make each decision early, when it costs nothing to say.

What should you say in the group chat before the meal?

The group chat a day or two before is the best place to settle the split. Nobody is hungry, the server isn't waiting, and a message is easier than a conversation.

Keep it light and make it about convenience, not money:

Looking forward to Saturday! Shall we each pay for what we have? Easier than working out who had the wine.

If the group usually splits equally and you'd like to keep doing that:

Shall we just split it equally on Saturday? If anyone's planning a light one, shout and we'll sort it by what you had.

The second version is kinder than it looks. It gives the person on a budget, or the non-drinker, a way out without making them ask for one. If that person is you, there's a full guide on how to say you only want to pay for what you had.

For a big or expensive booking, add the practical bits too:

Booked for 8 at 7.30. They add 12.5% service and want a £10 deposit each. I'll pay the bill on the night and send everyone their share.

That one message answers who's paying, how you're splitting and what extras there are. Deposits have their own guide: group booking deposits.

What should you do at the table?

Decide who pays the restaurant

The smoothest option for most groups is one person paying and everyone paying them back that night. Say it before the bill comes:

I'll get this one on my card, then I'll send round what everyone owes.

Handing the server six cards works too, but ask first, and have the amounts ready. The step by step version is in how to split the bill at a restaurant.

Say it if you only had a starter

If you ordered much less than everyone else and nobody has agreed a method, say so when the bill arrives, once, and lightly:

I just had the soup and a lemonade, so I'll pay for mine if that's OK?

Most people say "of course". It's not rude. In a 2016 OpenTable survey of UK diners, 34% said they go along with an equal split so others don't think they're tight, which suggests plenty of people at your table are quietly thinking the same thing (OpenTable UK, 2016).

Here's how much it matters

Four friends, a bill of £153.00 including 12.5% service. Maya had a starter and water. The others had mains, sides and drinks.

Person Ordered With 12.5% service Equal split
Sam £44.00 £49.50 £38.25
Priya £40.00 £45.00 £38.25
Jack £38.00 £42.75 £38.25
Maya £14.00 £15.75 £38.25
Total £136.00 £153.00 £153.00

Splitting equally, Maya pays £22.50 more than she ate. That's the moment that goes quiet. Notice the service charge is split in proportion too, so Maya's starter doesn't pay the same service as Sam's steak. More on that in splitting service charge fairly.

Don't argue about pennies

If the itemised shares come to £42.75 and someone sends £43, that's fine. Rounding up a little is normal. Going through the receipt line by line at the table is what makes people cringe, so do it on your phone, not out loud.

How do you ask for the money afterwards?

Send it the same day, ideally before anyone gets home. Put the amount and a way to pay in the same message, so nobody has to reply asking "how much?".

Thanks for tonight! It came to £153 with service. Sam £49.50, Priya £45, Jack £42.75, Maya £15.75. My Monzo link: [link]

If someone hasn't paid after a few days, one short nudge is enough:

Just bumping this in case it got buried: £42.75 for Saturday. Link's above 🙏

How do you stop it happening next time?

  • Make one person the default organiser who sends the "how are we paying?" message for every booking.
  • Pick a default method for your group. If your orders are usually similar, equal is fine. If they aren't, by item is fairer. The £5 rule helps you decide.
  • Pay back on the night. The longer a debt sits, the stranger it feels to mention. In Starling Bank's 2023 survey, people waited more than two months on average to be paid back (Starling Bank, 2023).
  • Remember money is tight for lots of people. In a 2026 Confused.com survey, half of Brits said they'd turned down an invitation because of the cost (Confused.com, 2026). A group that splits fairly makes it easier for those friends to say yes.

Questions people ask

How do you split the bill without it being awkward?

Agree the method in the group chat before the meal, say who's paying the restaurant before the bill arrives, and send everyone their exact share with a payment link the same day. Most awkwardness comes from leaving those decisions to the end.

Is it rude to ask to pay only for what you ordered?

No. Asking once, politely, is normal, especially if your order was much cheaper than everyone else's. It's easiest before you order, but it's fine when the bill comes too.

Who should bring up how to split the bill?

Whoever organised the booking, ideally a day or two before. If nobody does, anyone can, with something like "Shall we each pay for what we had?" in the group chat.

How soon should you ask friends to pay you back?

The same day. Send the amounts and a way to pay before people get home, then one short reminder after 3 to 5 days if anyone hasn't paid.
