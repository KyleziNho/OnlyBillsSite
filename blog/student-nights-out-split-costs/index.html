How to split costs on student nights out

Pres, rounds, Ubers home and cheap dinners. Fair ways for students to split the costs of a night out on a tight budget, with £ examples and messages to copy.

Kyle, founder of OnlyBills19 October 2026 · 5 min read

On a student budget, the fairest way to split a night out is to pay for what you use: bring your own drinks to pres or chip into a kitty only for shared bottles, skip big rounds, split taxis by the people in them, and split dinners by what each person ordered. Then pay each other back the same night, before the loan runs out and everyone forgets.

Money is the reason a lot of students stay in. In NatWest's 2026 survey of 5,019 UK university students, 92% said they had turned down a social event because of money. The aim here is a system where the friend on the tightest budget can still come.

Pres: who pays for the drinks?

The cheapest part of the night, and the easiest to keep fair.

  • Bring your own. Everyone brings what they'll drink. No maths, no subsidising.
  • Kitty for shared stuff only. If someone buys mixers, ice and a bottle for the table, split that between the people drinking it. £18 of shared drinks between six is £3 each.
  • Don't charge the host for hosting. But do chip in if they've bought snacks for everyone.

A message that works:

Pres at mine from 9 🎉 BYO drinks. I'm getting mixers and crisps, £2 each to me if you want in.

Rounds: usually a bad idea for students

Rounds feel generous but they're expensive and uneven. In a group of six, a round of pints or doubles can easily be £30 or more, and the person who buys first often pays for drinks that the last person never gets round to buying back.

Better options:

  • Get your own. Completely normal on a night out.
  • Rounds of two. Pair up with one friend and alternate.
  • Say you're out early. "I'm doing my own tonight, skint till Friday." Nobody will think twice.

Ubers and taxis home

Split the fare by the people in the car. For a single drop-off, divide evenly: a £16 Uber between four is £4 each.

With several stops, people going further should pay a bit more. A simple fair method: split the shared part of the journey evenly, and the people going further split the rest between them.

Say a £21 ride for four, where two get out at the first stop, which is about £8 into the fare:

  • Shared part to the first stop: £8, split four ways = £2 each.
  • Remaining £13 split by the two going on = £6.50 each.
  • First two pay £2. Last two pay £8.50. Total £21.

Uber's own Split Fare option divides the fare equally between riders who have Uber accounts and accept the request. It has to be set up before the trip ends, so for uneven splits it's easier for one person to pay and the others to send their share.

Cheap dinners

Curry houses, pizza places and BYOB restaurants are student favourites because they're cheap, but group bills still go wrong.

  • BYOB corkage: split the corkage between the people who brought bottles, not the whole table.
  • Sharing dishes: rice, naan and sides for the table split evenly. Mains are paid by whoever ordered them.
  • Service charge: many places add 10% or 12.5% for groups. Spread it in proportion to what each person had.

Takeaways in halls

Ordering together on one app saves on delivery fees, but someone has to pay. Split the food by who ordered what, and the delivery and service fees evenly or in proportion. Our guide to splitting a takeaway order has the details.

Keeping it fair when budgets are different

Not everyone has the same loan, job or help from home. A few habits that make a group easier to be in:

  • Suggest the cheap option sometimes. A film night or a walk is no worse a plan than a £40 night out. In the NatWest survey, movie nights (47%) and park walks (37%) were among the ways students were socialising for less.
  • Never make someone justify paying for only what they had. "Just pay for yours" should be the default, not a favour.
  • Put the cost in the plan. "Bottomless brunch, £35 each" lets people decide before they're at the table.

Pay each other back the same night

Small debts pile up fast when it's £4 here and £6.50 there. Settle up before bed, while everyone remembers. Send exact amounts and a way to pay:

Uber was £21: Sam and Lia £2 each, Jake and me £8.50. Sort code 00-00-00, acc 12345678 🙏

Questions people ask

How do you split pres fairly?

Everyone brings their own drinks, and anything bought for the group (mixers, a shared bottle, snacks) is split between the people who had it.

Should students do rounds?

Only in small groups that drink at the same pace. In a big group, buying your own or doing rounds of two is fairer and much cheaper.

How do you split an Uber with multiple stops?

Split the part everyone shared evenly, then the people going further split the rest. Or split evenly if the stops are close together.

How do you split a restaurant bill when some people are on a tight budget?

Split by what each person ordered rather than evenly, and spread any service charge in proportion. Agree it before ordering so nobody feels awkward.
