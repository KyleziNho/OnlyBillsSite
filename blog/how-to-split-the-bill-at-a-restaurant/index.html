How to split the bill at a restaurant

What to do at the table, from agreeing the split before you order to asking for the bill, one card or several, and getting everyone to pay back that night.

The smoothest way to split the bill at a restaurant is to agree how you're splitting before anyone orders, ask for one bill at the end, check it, then either pay on a few cards with clear amounts or have one person pay and everyone send their share before you leave. The awkward part is almost always deciding things at the end, with the server waiting.

It's a common worry. In a 2021 comparethemarket.com survey of 2,000 diners, 43% said they feel awkward about how to pay when eating out, and people had waited an average of 6 minutes 45 seconds to settle the bill.

This guide is about the logistics. For which method is fairest (even, itemised or a mix), see how to split a bill fairly.

Step 1: decide how you're splitting before you order

Thirty seconds at the start saves ten minutes at the end. Once menus are down, say it plainly:

"Are we splitting evenly or paying for our own? I'm easy either way."

If you know you'll be ordering less (not drinking, skipping starters), say so now rather than at the bill:

"I'm not drinking tonight, so I'll just pay for mine if that's OK."

And if there are dishes for the table:

"Shall we get two sharers for everyone and split those?"

Step 2: order in a way you can untangle later

  • Name the sharers. "Two bottles of red for us four" is easy to split later. "Another bottle" at 10pm is not.
  • Keep a rough count of rounds if drinks are coming one at a time. Most receipts list every drink, but not who had it.
  • Ask about service charge if there's no mention on the menu. Groups of six or more often get a discretionary 12.5% added. You'll want to know before you work out shares. (It's optional: see is service charge mandatory in the UK?)

Step 3: ask for the bill

Catch your server's eye, or ask when they next pass:

"Could we get the bill when you have a sec, please?"

If you already know you'll pay on several cards, say it now, not when the card machine arrives:

"We'll be paying on three cards, is that all right?"

Most UK restaurants will split a bill, but some limit how many cards they'll take for big tables. More on that in can you ask a restaurant to split the bill?

Step 4: check the bill

Read it before anyone pays. Look for:

  • Dishes or drinks you didn't order
  • A service charge, and whether it's the percentage you expected
  • Any deposit or voucher that should have been taken off
  • A second service line or "optional gratuity" on top of a service charge

If something's wrong, say so calmly: "I think we only had two bottles, could you check?" It's usually a mistake, and staff would rather fix it now.

Step 5: one card or many?

Even split on several cards: easy. "Could you split it four ways, please?"

Uneven split on several cards: do the maths first and give the server a list:

"Could we do £42.10 on this card, £38.50 on this one, and the rest on mine?"

Putting "the rest" on the last card means nobody has to make the pennies add up.

One card: the most common choice now, because cash has nearly gone. UK Finance figures show cash was just 8% of UK payments in 2025, and it says Faster Payments growth has been driven partly by people splitting bills and sending money to friends. One person pays, and the table settles up by bank transfer.

Step 6: settle up before you leave

If one person paid, the aim is that they're paid back before everyone goes home. Memory and goodwill fade fast. In a 2023 Starling Bank survey of 2,000 UK adults, a third said they were owed money by friends, family, partners or colleagues, and nearly half (46%) admitted feeling too awkward and embarrassed to ask for what they're owed (Starling Bank and Opinium, 2023).

What makes it easy:

  1. Give each person an exact number. "£34.62", not "about thirty-five".
  2. Send a way to pay. Your sort code and account number, or a payment link from your bank (Monzo, Starling and Revolut all do these). Friends at other banks can usually pay by card or bank transfer. See how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling.
  3. Do it at the table, while everyone's phones are out.

A message to copy into the group chat:

Thanks all, lovely night! I paid £186.40 including service. Shares below, send to me when you can 🙏 Priya £41.20 · Tom £33.75 · Jas £38.10 · Mel £35.25 · Me £38.10 Sort code 00-00-00, account 12345678

(Use your own details, of course.)

If someone still hasn't paid by the next day, a light nudge is fine. How to ask a friend to pay you back has messages for that.

What if someone has to leave early?

Work out their share before they go, using the menu prices plus the service percentage. Someone who had a £15 main and a £5 glass of wine with 12.5% service owes £22.50. They can pay you then and there, or pay the restaurant for their items if the staff are happy to do a separate bill.

Questions people ask

How do you ask for the bill to be split at a restaurant?

Tell the server when you ask for the bill: "We're paying on three cards, could you split it three ways?" For different amounts, give them a list and put the remainder on the last card.

Is it better for one person to pay?

For groups of more than about four, or whenever you're splitting by item, yes. It's quicker for everyone. The payer should send exact amounts and a way to pay before the group leaves.

How do you split a bill at a restaurant without cash?

One person pays by card, then everyone sends their share by bank transfer or a payment link. Most UK banking apps can send money to any other UK account.

Who should pay when you split the bill?

Whoever's happiest to wait to be paid back, ideally someone the group knows well. It's often the person who organised the meal.
