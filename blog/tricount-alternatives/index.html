Tricount alternatives: which app fits your group?

When Tricount is the right choice, and when Splitwise, Settle Up, Splid, Monzo Split or OnlyBills fits better, with UK prices checked in November 2026.

For most groups, Tricount is hard to beat on price: it says it's free, with no ads and no limits. You'd switch for a specific reason. If you want a web app and reminders, try Settle Up. If your friends are already on Splitwise, use that. If you want something tiny for one trip, Splid is a good fit. If the problem is one restaurant bill where everyone had different things, use an app that reads the receipt, such as Splitwise's free Itemize or OnlyBills, and keep Tricount for the rest.

Here's what Tricount does well, where it's not the best fit, and what each alternative offers. Facts are from each app's own website or UK App Store listing, as of November 2026. For a wider view, see the best bill splitting apps in the UK.

Who is Tricount for?

Tricount is a group expense tracker. You create a "tricount" for a trip, a flat or a couple, add each expense with who paid and who it was for, and it works out the balances and who should pay whom to settle up. It's been owned by the Dutch bank bunq since 2022, which is why bunq features appear in it.

It suits groups who share lots of costs over days or months and want one free place to log them: a week in Lisbon, a house of four, a stag do.

What does Tricount do well?

Quite a lot, for nothing. As of November 2026:

  • Price. Its website says "Free. No ads. No limits." and "No ads or daily expense cap."
  • Currencies and offline. Its UK App Store listing says it supports over 100 currencies and works offline.
  • Getting paid. You can send payment requests, see who's paid and who needs a reminder, and share what friends owe you "without needing them to be on the app".
  • Easy start. The website says you can try it without signing up, and it's on iPhone and Android.
  • Switching in. The listing says you can move your Splitwise groups to Tricount.
  • Ratings. 4.8 out of 5 from about 6,400 ratings on the UK App Store.

So if Tricount is working for your group, there may be no reason to move.

Settle Up

Settle Up does the same core job as Tricount: a group, a list of expenses and the fewest transfers to settle. The differences are a web app (alongside iPhone and Android) and a link that lets group members see balances in a browser without installing anything. Its App Store listing says it supports all currencies with live exchange rates and works offline.

Cost: free with ads. Premium removes ads and adds receipt photos, recurring expenses, statistics and Excel export. As of November 2026 the UK App Store lists Premium at £3.99 a month or £18.99 a year, or from £5.99 to upgrade a whole group for a week.

Pick it if: someone in your group wants to do the admin on a laptop, or you need recurring expenses like a monthly bill.

Splitwise

Splitwise is the one most people already know, which counts for a lot when you're trying to get eight people into the same app. It splits equally, by amounts, percentages or shares, and runs on iPhone, Android and the web.

Cost: free, with a daily cap on new expenses. Splitwise Pro lists unlimited expenses, no ads, receipt scanning and currency conversion. The UK App Store lists Pro options from £2.79 to £47.99. Unlike Tricount, you'll be adding expenses to an account.

Also new: Splitwise launched Itemize in the UK in October 2026. One person photographs the receipt and the others scan a QR code to claim their own items, with no app or account, free (The Paypers, 16 October 2026).

Pick it if: your friends are already on it. There's a closer look at it in Splitwise alternatives.

Splid

Splid is a small app made for trips. Its website says it's free with no sign-up, it works offline or synced, and it converts between more than 150 currencies. It can also produce a PDF summary. On the UK App Store it's rated 4.9 out of 5 from about 2,700 ratings, with an optional Splid Plus purchase at £3.99.

Pick it if: you want the lightest possible setup for one holiday.

Monzo Split

If your group banks with Monzo, Monzo Split may be all you need. You can split one bill or keep a Running Split going for ongoing costs. Monzo says friends can join as guests from a link "even if they don't live in the UK or bank with us", and that Split is "completely free, for you and all your friends." You'll need a Monzo account yourself.

Revolut and Starling have split features too, with different rules on who can pay. They're compared in how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling.

OnlyBills (for restaurant and pub bills)

OnlyBills isn't a Tricount replacement for a whole trip. It does one part of it: the meal out where nobody had the same thing.

The host scans the receipt and it reads every line. You check it adds up and share a live table: friends scan a QR code or open a link, then tick the items they had on their own phone, in their browser or as an App Clip on iPhone. Shared dishes split between the people who shared them, service charge is spread in proportion, and every share is exact to the penny. Friends then pay the host with a Monzo, Starling, Revolut or PayPal link, or by bank transfer.

Cost: free, with no sign-up for anyone. Free includes 30 receipt scans in any 30 days. Typing a bill in is always unlimited. An optional subscription, OnlyBills Pro, raises the scan limit.

Bear in mind: the host needs an iPhone or iPad (friends can join from any phone's browser), and it isn't built for rent or a trip's running costs.

Can you use Tricount and another app together?

Yes, and on a trip it often works best. Say four of you spend a week in Cornwall. The cottage, car hire and supermarket runs go in Tricount. Then one night the bill is £168: £150 of food and wine plus an £18 tip.

Person Ordered Share with tip
Ella £48.00 £53.76
Finn £42.00 £47.04
Gus £36.00 £40.32
Hana £24.00 £26.88
Total £150.00 £168.00

Split equally, that's £42 each, so Hana would pay £15.12 more than her share. Split the dinner by item and have everyone pay back whoever paid the bill that night. Leave the dinner out of Tricount, so it isn't counted twice. Holiday tips are in how to split costs on holiday.

How do you move away from Tricount?

There's no need to delete anything. The simplest switch is to settle up in Tricount first, so every balance is at zero, and start fresh in the new app. That way nobody argues about which app has the right numbers.

Questions people ask

Is Tricount really free?

Its website says "Free. No ads. No limits." as of November 2026. It's owned by the bank bunq, which offers optional bunq accounts and cards that can connect to it.

Does Tricount scan receipts?

Its listing says you can share expense photos. Neither its website nor its App Store listing mentions reading a receipt line by line, so you add each expense as an amount.

What's the best Tricount alternative for a restaurant bill?

One that reads the receipt and lets each person claim their items, such as OnlyBills or Splitwise's free Itemize. Keep Tricount for the trip's other costs if you like it.

Is Tricount or Splitwise better?

For a free balance app with no daily cap, Tricount. If your friends already use Splitwise, or you want its free Itemize for receipts, Splitwise. Both run on iPhone and Android.
