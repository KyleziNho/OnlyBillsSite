How to split a takeaway order fairly

Who pays the delivery fee, the service fee and the tip on a group takeaway? A fair method, a worked example and what Deliveroo, Uber Eats and Just Eat offer.

Kyle, founder of OnlyBills13 October 2026 · 5 min read

The fair way to split a group takeaway is: everyone pays for their own food, the delivery fee and any rider tip are split equally, and any fee that grows with the size of the order (like a percentage service fee) is split in proportion to what each person ordered. One person usually pays the app, then everyone pays them back.

Below is how to do that with real numbers, plus what Deliveroo, Uber Eats and Just Eat actually offer for group orders in the UK (we checked their own help pages in October 2026).

Do any delivery apps split the payment for you?

App Group ordering for friends Each person pays separately?
Uber Eats Yes. Start a group order and share a link; guests tap "Join order" and add their own items Yes. Uber's help page says the organiser can "pay for everyone, or have each guest pay for their own share"
Deliveroo Its FAQ describes group orders "where everyone can add their own dish" under Deliveroo for Work Its FAQ doesn't describe splitting the payment between people
Just Eat Group ordering is offered through Just Eat for Business, with a budget per person and top-ups paid on your own card We couldn't find a consumer split payment feature

So for most friend groups, the reality is still: one person orders, one person pays, and the rest owe them. Uber Eats is the exception if you set the order to split before checkout.

Which costs are on a takeaway bill?

Cost What drives it Fairest way to split
Your food and drink What you ordered You pay for your own
Shared sides (rice, naan, chips for the table) Everyone who ate them Equally between the people who shared
Delivery fee Distance from the restaurant, per order Equally: it's one fee whatever each person ordered
Service fee Set by the app, varies by order In proportion to what each person ordered
Small order fee Only if the basket is under the restaurant's minimum Rarely applies to group orders. If it does, split it equally
Rider tip Optional Equally, or between whoever wants to tip

Deliveroo's FAQ explains each of these: the delivery fee "is based on your location and will vary depending on your distance from the restaurant", there may be a service fee, and a small order fee "will only be applied to your basket if the total is less than the restaurant's minimum spend". Group orders are one of the easiest ways to avoid that last one.

A worked example: curry for four

Four friends order on one account. The order comes to £66.31.

  • Ana: £14.50
  • Ben: £11.90
  • Cara: £18.00
  • Dan: £14.00
  • Food total: £58.40
  • Delivery fee: £2.99
  • Service fee: £2.92 (in this example, 5% of the food)
  • Rider tip: £2.00
Person Food Delivery (equal) Service fee (in proportion) Tip (equal) Pays
Ana £14.50 75p 73p 50p £16.47
Ben £11.90 75p 60p 50p £13.74
Cara £18.00 75p 90p 50p £20.15
Dan £14.00 75p 70p 50p £15.95
Total £58.40 £2.99 £2.92 £2.00 £66.31

(The fee columns are rounded to the nearest penny for display; the "Pays" column is worked out exactly and rounded once, so the four shares add up to the £66.31 the app charged.)

An equal split would be £16.58 each. Ben would overpay by £2.84 and Cara would pay £3.57 less than her share. Once, that's a shrug. Every Friday for a year, it's about £150 for Ben, and the kind of thing that quietly annoys the person who always orders the cheapest thing. We've looked at how equal splits add up in how much splitting the bill equally costs you.

Shared dishes

Shared sides go to the people who had them, not to everyone. If Ana, Ben and Cara share a £4.50 naan and Dan doesn't touch it, that's £1.50 each for three people, and nothing for Dan. Same logic as a bottle of wine when someone isn't drinking.

How to do it with each app

Uber Eats: let the app split it

  1. Pick the restaurant and tap Start group order (or select the group order option).
  2. In the settings, choose to split the bill so each guest pays for their own share. You can also set a deadline or a spending limit.
  3. Share the link in your group chat. Guests open it and tap Join order.
  4. Everyone adds their own items. Then lock the order so nobody can add more, and check out.

Uber's help page doesn't spell out how delivery and service fees are divided in a split order, so look at each person's total before you lock it.

Deliveroo and Just Eat: one person pays, everyone pays back

  1. One person builds the basket. Ask everyone to send their order in the group chat first, with prices.
  2. Add shared sides once, and note who's sharing them.
  3. Check out. Screenshot the order summary showing the fees.
  4. Work out each share with the method above, and send each person their number and a payment link.

Getting paid back

The person who paid should send the numbers while the food is still warm. Something like:

Food's here! Ana £16.47, Ben £13.74, Cara £20.15, Dan £15.95 (your food plus a share of delivery, service and the tip). Pay me here: [link]

A payment link from your bank (Monzo, Revolut or Starling) lets friends pay by card from any bank, or they can send a bank transfer. There's more on each bank's option in how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling, and wording for the follow up in how to ask a friend to pay you back.

If you have a printed receipt for the order, OnlyBills can read it: the person who paid scans it, shares a link, and each friend ticks their own items and sees their share with the fees spread in proportion. The shares add up to the penny, and each friend gets a link to pay the host back.

Common takeaway arguments, settled

"I only had a starter, why am I paying the same delivery fee?" Because the rider made one trip for the whole order. The delivery fee is a cost of getting the food there, not of the food itself, so equal is fair.

"I wasn't going to tip." Fair enough: tips are optional. Split it between whoever wants to tip, or leave it off.

"It's only a few quid, just split it equally." Also fine, if everyone agrees and the orders are close. Ask once, at the start: "Equal split, or everyone pays for their own?"

"The person who ordered gets the loyalty points." Usually true, and usually not worth arguing about. If it's a big regular order, take turns.

Questions people ask

Can you split payment on Deliveroo?

Deliveroo's FAQ mentions group orders where everyone adds their own dish (under Deliveroo for Work), but doesn't describe splitting the payment between people. Most groups have one person pay and everyone else pay them back by bank transfer or payment link.

Does Uber Eats let you split the bill?

Yes. When you start a group order, Uber's help page says you can pay for everyone or have each guest pay for their own share. Set it before you check out.

Who should pay the delivery fee on a group order?

Split it equally. It's one charge for one trip, whatever each person ordered. Split percentage-based fees, like a service fee, in proportion to each person's food.

Is it fair to split a takeaway equally?

It's fair when everyone ordered about the same and agrees to it. When orders vary a lot, paying for your own food plus an equal share of delivery is fairer and only takes a minute to work out.

Split it fairly in a minute

Scan the receipt. Friends tap what they had on their own phone. Your first split is free.
