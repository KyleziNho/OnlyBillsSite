Tikkie, Wero and iDEAL explained: paying friends in the Netherlands and nearby

A Tikkie is a payment request you pay through iDEAL, which is becoming Wero. How each works, where Wero is live, and how to pay without a Dutch bank.

A Tikkie is a payment request link: a Dutch friend sends it, you tap it, and you pay in your own banking app through iDEAL. iDEAL is now being folded into Wero, the European Payments Initiative's app, which friends in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg already use to pay each other by phone number.

Here's what each one is, what's changing, and how to pay if you don't have a Dutch bank.

What is a Tikkie?

Tikkie is ABN AMRO's payment request app, used across the Netherlands to split bills. About 170 million Tikkies were sent in 2025, according to ABN AMRO's figures as reported by IamExpat. To send one you need the Tikkie app and a Dutch bank account. To pay one you don't need the app: you tap the link, pick your bank, and confirm the payment in your banking app.

A few things worth knowing:

  • The creator sets the amount, or can leave it for the payer to fill in. Leaving it open is handy when everyone owes something different.
  • Requests expire. Pay soon after you get it.
  • Groepie is Tikkie's feature for tracking shared costs in a group, like Splitwise inside Tikkie, ending in payment requests to settle up.

How to tell a Tikkie is genuine

Fake Tikkies are a common scam in the Netherlands. Rabobank says a real Tikkie link always starts with https://tikkie.me/. Watch for lookalikes such as "tlkkie" with an L. Be wary of strangers, for example on Marktplaats, asking you to pay one cent "to check your account". When in doubt, call the person who sent it.

What is iDEAL?

iDEAL is the Dutch system for paying online straight from your bank account. It's what you use to pay a Tikkie, and most Dutch online shops.

What is Wero, and what's happening to iDEAL?

Wero is a payment app from the European Payments Initiative (EPI), a group of European banks. You can use it in your banking app or the Wero app to send money to a phone number or email, and it's moving into online and in-store payments.

  • Belgium, France and Germany: Wero has worked for payments between people since 2024.
  • Luxembourg: Wero replaced Payconiq in September 2026.
  • Netherlands: iDEAL is moving to Wero in stages. Since early 2026 the checkout button has read "iDEAL | Wero", and by October 2026 all Dutch banks were due to be connected to Wero. The Dutch banks aim to finish the switch by 31 December 2027 at the latest. For consumers, paying online stays the same: you still confirm in your own bank's app.
  • Austria: listed by EPI as coming soon.

EPI says Wero has about 58 million users. In Germany, PayPal and plain bank transfers are still the most common way friends pay each other, so don't assume a German friend has Wero switched on.

Is Wero replacing Tikkie? No. Wero replaces the iDEAL plumbing underneath. Tikkie stays as the request app, and ABN AMRO is adding Wero to it, including sending Tikkies to friends elsewhere in Europe.

How to pay a Tikkie without a Dutch bank

  1. Open the link and look at the bank list. If your bank is there, you can pay normally. N26, for example, lets customers with a German IBAN pay Tikkies from the N26 app.
  2. If it isn't, ask for an IBAN. Every Tikkie sender has a Dutch bank account, so they have an IBAN. A euro instant transfer arrives within 10 seconds. See how to pay a friend back by IBAN.
  3. Or use Revolut or PayPal, if your friend has either.

If you're paying from a UK bank, a euro transfer may carry fees. A Revolut or Wise euro balance is usually cheaper.

Paying friends in Belgium, France and Germany

If your friend uses Wero and you bank in a Wero country, send to their phone number from your banking app or the Wero app. If you don't, the IBAN route works everywhere in the euro area. In Germany, ask whether they'd prefer PayPal. More country by country in how friends pay each other back in Europe.

Where OnlyBills fits

OnlyBills doesn't create iDEAL or Wero payment links: there's no public way for an app to do that. What it does instead depends on where you are.

Netherlands: you paste your own Tikkie link (bunq.me request links work too). Friends at the table see their exact share and a button that opens tikkie.me, with the note "Enter €11.00 if it asks" and an Amount line they can copy. Because each friend owes a different amount, a Tikkie with the amount left open fits best.

Belgium and France: Wero is one of the first suggestions. Friends see your number and their amount, each with a Copy button, and the hint "Copy the number, then send with Wero in your bank app." Germany: PayPal, Revolut and IBAN come first, with Wero under More options.

Visitors without a Dutch bank can pick Revolut or your IBAN from the same screen. OnlyBills never touches the money; friends tap "I've paid" when they've sent it. Setup is in how to get paid back outside the UK.

Questions people ask

What is a Tikkie?

A payment request link from Tikkie, an app run by ABN AMRO. You tap the link and pay in your own banking app through iDEAL. You don't need the Tikkie app to pay one.

Can I pay a Tikkie without a Dutch bank account?

Only if your bank appears in the list on the Tikkie payment page. Some non-Dutch banks do, for example N26 for customers with a German IBAN. Otherwise ask your friend for their IBAN and send a euro transfer.

Is Wero replacing Tikkie?

No. Wero is replacing iDEAL, the system Tikkie payments run on. Tikkie stays, and ABN AMRO is adding Wero to it. Dutch banks aim to finish moving iDEAL to Wero by the end of 2027.

Which countries use Wero?

Wero works for payments between people in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg. In the Netherlands it is arriving through the iDEAL migration, and EPI lists Austria as coming soon.
