How to split a bottomless brunch bill fairly

How to split a bottomless brunch when some take the drinks package and some don't, plus time limits, service charge and deposits. With a worked £ example.

The fair way to split a bottomless brunch is simple: everyone on the package pays the package price, anyone not on it pays for what they ordered, shared plates are split between whoever ate them, and service charge is spread in proportion. Then take off any deposit each person has already paid.

The catch is that many venues don't let a table mix and match. Check that before you book, because it decides whether your non-drinking friend pays £20 or £50.

Does everyone at the table have to do bottomless?

Often, yes. Plenty of venues sell bottomless brunch per table, not per person. QHotels' terms, for example, say "All guests seated at the table must participate in the bottomless brunch package unless agreed otherwise by management." Albert's Schloss, which runs bottomless brunches, says in its guide that the whole table must take part "most of the time".

So before you book, ask the venue three questions:

  • Does the whole table have to take the package?
  • Is there a non-alcoholic or soft-drinks version, and how much is it?
  • Can anyone order from the normal menu instead?

If the answer is "whole table, no exceptions", tell the group the full price per head in the invite. Someone who doesn't drink can then decide whether it's worth it, rather than finding out at the table.

How do you split it when some people don't take the package?

Treat the package as one item per person, and everything else as normal.

  1. Each package is one item for the person who had it.
  2. À la carte food and drinks go to whoever ordered them.
  3. Shared plates (fries, a sharing board) split between the people who ate them.
  4. Service charge is spread in proportion to what each person had. If the venue adds 12.5%, that's 12.5% of each person's own share.
  5. Deposits come off each person's share.

This is fairer than an equal split, because a bottomless package often costs two or three times what a non-drinker would spend on brunch and a coffee.

Worked example: eight friends, six on the package

Eight friends book brunch. Six take the £48 bottomless package (a brunch dish and 90 minutes of drinks). Priya doesn't drink and orders from the menu: £20 of food and coffee. Tom is driving: £24 of food and soft drinks. Priya, Tom, Jess and Kai share two portions of fries (£8). The venue adds 12.5% service.

Item Cost
Bottomless package, 6 × £48 £288.00
Priya's order £20.00
Tom's order £24.00
Fries, shared by 4 £8.00
Subtotal £340.00
Service 12.5% £42.50
Total £382.50

What each person pays, with 12.5% added to their own share:

Who Their items Pays
Four on the package, no fries £48.00 £54.00 each
Jess and Kai, package plus fries £48.00 + £2.00 £56.25 each
Priya £20.00 + £2.00 £24.75
Tom £24.00 + £2.00 £29.25
Total £382.50

Split it equally and everyone pays about £47.81. Priya would pay about £23 more than her own share, mostly for other people's prosecco. For more on this, see how to split the bill when some people don't drink.

Can two people share one bottomless package?

Usually not. Packages are priced per person, and venues often serve alcoholic drinks one at a time to the person on the package. QHotels' terms, for example, say "Alcoholic drinks will be served one at a time per person." Sharing a package with a friend who isn't on it is likely to break the venue's rules, so ask staff rather than trying it.

What happens if someone arrives late?

They normally pay the full package price and get less time. The clock usually starts at your booking time, not when each person sits down. QHotels, for instance, says bottomless brunch "is available for 90 minutes from your reservation time".

Ninety minutes is the common length, with some venues offering two hours. If someone in your group is always late, tell them the real start time is 15 minutes earlier, or suggest they skip the package and order from the menu.

Should non-drinkers pay service charge on the packages?

No. If service is added to the whole bill, it's fair for each person to pay it on their own share only, which is what the example above does. Splitting the service charge equally would mean Priya tips on bottomless cocktails she never had.

Remember that a service charge is usually discretionary, even when it's printed on the bill. Our guide to whether service charge is mandatory explains when you can ask for it to be removed. And if you've bought the package as a prepaid ticket, check whether service is included in the ticket price or added on the day, so you don't pay it twice.

How do brunch deposits work?

Many venues take a deposit to hold a group table, and it's usually taken off the final bill. Albert's Schloss notes you'll "often need to pay a deposit to secure your table", usually non-refundable if you cancel at the last minute.

If one person pays the deposit for everyone, collect each person's part before you pay. Then, on the day, take each person's deposit off their own share. In the example above, if everyone paid a £10 deposit, Priya pays £14.75 on the day and the four package people pay £44.00. For dropouts and no-show fees, see group booking deposits.

Is bottomless brunch allowed in Scotland?

Not in the "unlimited" sense. Under the Licensing (Scotland) Act 2005, a drinks promotion that "involves the supply of unlimited amounts of alcohol for a fixed charge" counts as an irresponsible drinks promotion, which isn't allowed on licensed premises. So Scottish brunches usually come with a set number of drinks or a drink with each course, and the food is what's bottomless.

In England and Wales the rule is less strict. The mandatory licensing conditions ban providing "unlimited or unspecified quantities of alcohol free or for a fixed or discounted fee" only where it's done "in a manner which carries a significant risk of undermining a licensing objective". That's one reason venues set rules such as time limits and one drink at a time.

For splitting, none of this changes the method. A brunch with three drinks included is still one package per person.

Questions people ask

Do non-drinkers have to pay for bottomless brunch?

Only if the venue makes the whole table take the package. Many do. Ask when you book whether there's a soft-drinks version or whether non-drinkers can order from the menu.

How do you split a bottomless brunch bill?

Each person on the package pays the package price, everyone else pays for what they ordered, shared plates split between whoever ate them, and service charge goes on each person's own share.

How long does bottomless brunch last?

Usually 90 minutes, sometimes two hours. The time normally runs from your booking time, so latecomers get less time for the same price.

Is service charge added to bottomless brunch?

Often, yes, at the venue's usual rate. Check whether a prepaid package already includes it. Service charge is usually discretionary in the UK.
