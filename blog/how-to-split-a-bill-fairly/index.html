How to split a bill fairly

Three fair ways to split a restaurant bill (even, itemised and hybrid), when to use each, and one £159.19 dinner for four worked out all three ways.

The fairest way to split a bill is for everyone to pay for what they had, with shared dishes split between the people who shared them and the service charge spread in proportion. If everyone ordered about the same, splitting evenly is fair too, and quicker. When food was similar but drinks weren't, a hybrid works best: split the food evenly and let people pay for their own drinks.

The right method depends on how different the orders were. Below are the three methods, a table for picking one, and the same dinner worked out each way so you can see what changes.

The three ways to split a bill

1. Split evenly

Add up the bill, divide by the number of people. It's fast and nobody has to read the receipt. It's fair when everyone had roughly the same, or when the table genuinely shared everything.

It stops being fair when one person had a salad and tap water and another had a steak and three cocktails. In a 2016 OpenTable survey, 38% of UK diners said they feel they lose money when the bill is split, and 58% said they agree to split equally because it's easier to pay, not because it's fair.

2. Split by item (itemised)

Everyone pays for exactly what they ordered. Shared things (a bottle of wine, nachos for the table) are divided between the people who had them. Service charge and any discount are spread in proportion to each person's spend, so someone with a £20 order pays less service than someone with a £50 one.

This is the fairest method, and it's what most people say they prefer when asked. In a small 2004 restaurant experiment, 19 of the 24 diners asked (about 80%) said they'd rather pay individually than split the bill (Gneezy, Haruvy and Yafe, 2004). The downside is the maths, especially with a long receipt.

3. The hybrid: food even, drinks separate, service proportional

This is the one most groups end up using without naming it. Food is usually similar from person to person, so split it evenly. Drinks are where the big gaps are, so each person pays for their own. Then spread the service charge in proportion to what each person's total came to.

It's quicker than a full itemised split and much fairer than an even one when some people didn't drink.

Which method should you use?

Situation Best method
Everyone had a main and a drink or two, similar prices Even
Sharing plates, tapas or a set menu, similar drinks Even
Food similar, but some drank a lot and some didn't drink Hybrid
Someone had a much pricier main or extra courses Itemised
Mixed group, not close friends, or anyone on a tight budget Itemised
Large group of 10 or more with a set menu Even for food, drinks by person
Someone's birthday and the group is covering them Any method, then share their part between everyone else

A simple rule of thumb: if anyone's order differs from the average by more than about £5, an even split stops being fair. There's more on that in is splitting the bill equally fair?

One dinner, split three ways

Four friends go out. Here's what they had:

Food Drinks
Amy Burger £16 2 pints, £13
Ben Salad £12 Lime soda, £3.50
Cara Steak £26 Half a £32 bottle of wine
Dan Pasta £18 Half the wine, plus an £11 cocktail
Shared Nachos £10 (all four)

Food comes to £82 and drinks to £59.50, so the subtotal is £141.50. A 12.5% service charge adds £17.69, making the bill £159.19.

Even split

£159.19 ÷ 4 = about £39.80 each (one person pays £39.79 so it adds up).

Itemised split

Each person's items, plus a quarter of the nachos (£2.50), then multiplied by 1.125 to add their share of the 12.5% service:

  • Amy: £16 + £2.50 + £13 = £31.50, so £35.44
  • Ben: £12 + £2.50 + £3.50 = £18.00, so £20.25
  • Cara: £26 + £2.50 + £16 = £44.50, so £50.06
  • Dan: £18 + £2.50 + £16 + £11 = £47.50, so £53.44

Total: £159.19.

Hybrid split

Food split evenly (£82 ÷ 4 = £20.50 each), drinks paid by whoever had them, service in proportion:

  • Amy: £20.50 + £13 = £33.50, so £37.69
  • Ben: £20.50 + £3.50 = £24.00, so £27.00
  • Cara: £20.50 + £16 = £36.50, so £41.06
  • Dan: £20.50 + £27 = £47.50, so £53.44

Total: £159.19.

What changed

Even Hybrid Itemised

Cara £39.80 £41.06 £50.06

On an even split, Ben pays almost double what he'd pay for his own order. The hybrid fixes most of that with one easy step. The itemised split is exact, and in this case it's the only one where Cara pays for her pricier steak.

How to split the service charge fairly

Service charge is a percentage of the bill, so the fair way to share it is as the same percentage of each person's part. Multiply each share by 1.125 for 12.5%, or 1.1 for 10%. Splitting it equally means the person with the smallest order tips the most as a proportion of what they spent.

Rounding each share to the penny can leave the total a penny or two off. Give the leftover pennies to whoever had the biggest share. Our guide to splitting service charge and tips fairly has the full method.

Shared dishes, bottles and the birthday person

  • Shared dishes: divide by the people who ate them, not the whole table. Three people sharing £9 of garlic bread pay £3 each, and the fourth pays nothing towards it.
  • Bottles of wine: split between the people who drank from it. A £32 bottle between two is £16 each. Same for any corkage.
  • Someone you're treating: work out their share first, then divide it between everyone else. See birthday dinner: who pays?

Why even splits cost more than you think

An even split doesn't just move money around. It can make the bill bigger. In the same 2004 experiment, diners ordered about 37 shekels' worth each when paying for themselves and about 51 when the bill was split evenly, roughly 36% more. The study was small (groups of six students in Israel), but the logic holds: if your extra dessert costs you only a sixth of its price, you're more likely to order it. How to suggest a fair split without it being awkward

Say it early, before anyone orders, and make it about ease rather than money:

"Shall we just pay for what we each have? Saves anyone subsidising the wine."

"Food split evenly and drinks separately? I'm not drinking tonight."

If the bill's already here:

"I'll do the maths. Everyone just tell me your drinks and I'll send the numbers round."

Questions people ask

What is the fairest way to split a bill?

Pay by item: everyone covers what they ordered, shared dishes are split between the people who shared them, and the service charge is spread in proportion. If orders were all similar, an even split is just as fair and faster.

Should you split the bill evenly or by what you ordered?

Evenly if everyone's order is within about £5 of each other. By what you ordered if there's a big gap, especially with drinks.

How do you split a bill when one person had a lot more?

Either split by item, or use the hybrid: split the food evenly and have each person pay for their own drinks and any extras, like a starter or dessert only they had.

How do you split service charge between friends?

In proportion to each person's share of the bill. For a 12.5% service charge, multiply each person's total by 1.125.
