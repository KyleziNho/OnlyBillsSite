How to split a bill with a discount or voucher

Who gets the saving when a group bill has a voucher, 2 for 1 deal or dining card discount, and how to share it fairly, with worked £ examples to the penny.

If a discount applies to the whole table's bill, share it in proportion to what each person ordered: 20% off the bill means 20% off everyone's share. If the voucher or card belongs to one person, the saving is theirs, and it's their choice whether to keep it or pass it round the table. Agree which it is before you order, not when the bill arrives.

The maths is the easy part once you know which kind of saving you've got. Below is a quick way to tell, then worked examples for percentage discounts, fixed vouchers, 2 for 1 deals and service charge.

Who gets the saving from a voucher or discount?

It depends on whose saving it is. There are two kinds.

The table's saving. The restaurant is running a deal for everyone, or the group booked because of an offer. Everyone ate under it, so everyone shares it.

One person's saving. Someone has a gift card, a birthday voucher, a staff or student discount, or a dining card they pay a membership for. That's their money or their perk. They might happily share it, but nobody else should assume they will.

Type of saving Whose is it? Fair way to split
Restaurant's own deal, such as 20% off the bill The table In proportion to each person's order
£ off voucher from a promotion the group booked with The table In proportion, or equally if you agree
2 for 1 on mains The table, unless it came from one person's card Spread across everyone who had a main
Gift card or gift voucher The person who owns it Treat it as them paying part of the bill
Student, staff or other personal discount The person it belongs to Off their own order only, unless they offer
Dining card or membership deal The cardholder, who may choose to share Ask. If shared, in proportion

A gift card is the one people most often get wrong. It isn't a discount, it's money. If Priya pays £30 of a £120 bill with her gift card, she has paid £30 towards her share, exactly as if she'd tapped her own card. The other diners don't get a cut of it.

How do you split a percentage discount?

Each person takes the same percentage off their own order. That's it.

Four friends use a restaurant's 20% off the bill offer:

Person Ordered 20% off Pays
Ana £22.40 minus £4.48 £17.92
Ben £31.20 minus £6.24 £24.96
Cal £18.40 minus £3.68 £14.72
Dev £28.00 minus £5.60 £22.40
Total £100.00 minus £20.00 £80.00

A quicker way: multiply each person's order by 0.8. The shares add up to the bill because everyone got the same rate as the restaurant gave the table.

What you shouldn't do is take £5 off each person. Cal ordered the least, so a flat £5 would give him 27% off while Ben got 16% off. With a percentage discount, a percentage split is the fair one.

How do you split a fixed £ voucher?

A voucher for a set amount, such as £12 off, can be split two ways, and both are defensible.

In proportion (fairest). Work out what percentage of the bill the voucher is, then take that off everyone. Three friends at a pub, £12 off a £96 food bill. £12 is 12.5% of £96, so each person gets 12.5% off.

Equally (simplest). Everyone came, everyone gets £4 off.

Person Ordered Proportional (12.5% off) Equal (£4 off)
Ella £18.00 £15.75 £14.00
Finn £36.00 £31.50 £32.00
Grace £42.00 £36.75 £38.00
Total £96.00 £84.00 £84.00

The equal way gives Ella, who ordered least, a bigger share of the saving than her share of the bill. That isn't wrong if the group is happy with it, and some groups prefer it because it's easy to say out loud. Just pick one before anyone does the sums, so it doesn't look like you chose the method that suited you.

How do you split a 2 for 1 deal?

With most 2 for 1 offers, the cheaper dish in each pair comes off. So the saving lands on whoever happened to order the cheaper main, which is luck rather than fairness. The fair fix is to spread the whole saving across everyone who had a main.

Four friends, 2 for 1 on mains. The mains were £20, £16, £13 and £11, so £60 in total. The receipt shows the £16 and £11 mains taken off, a £27 saving.

£27 is 45% of £60, so everyone takes 45% off their main (or multiplies it by 0.55):

Person Main Pays, saving where it landed Pays, saving shared in proportion
Hari £20.00 £20.00 £11.00
Isla £16.00 £0.00 £8.80
Jack £13.00 £13.00 £7.15
Kemi £11.00 £0.00 £6.05
Total £60.00 £33.00 £33.00

Drinks, starters and anything else outside the deal are paid on top as normal.

How the dishes are paired changes the saving. Here, pairing the two dearest mains (£20 with £16) gave £27 off. Pairing £20 with £11 would only have given £24. If the restaurant lets you choose, put the most expensive dishes together.

If the 2 for 1 came from one person's dining card, the same table works if they're happy to share. Dining cards such as tastecard advertise deals like 2 for 1 meals at some chains and 25% off the total bill at others, and its terms say the membership can only be used by the named member, and that offers may not be combined with other offers (tastecard, 2026). The person who pays for the card may reasonably want something back for it. A common compromise is that they share the saving and someone else buys their drink.

Is service charged on the price before or after the discount?

It varies. UK card machine and till systems let the restaurant choose: Square's guidance for UK restaurants, for example, says venues can set each service charge to be calculated before or after discounts (Square UK help centre). Check the receipt to see which total the percentage was applied to.

Here's the same 20% off dinner for four, with a 12.5% service charge done both ways.

Person Ordered After 20% off Service on full price Pays Service on discounted price Pays
Ana £22.40 £17.92 £2.80 £20.72 £2.24 £20.16
Ben £31.20 £24.96 £3.90 £28.86 £3.12 £28.08
Cal £18.40 £14.72 £2.30 £17.02 £1.84 £16.56
Dev £28.00 £22.40 £3.50 £25.90 £2.80 £25.20
Total £100.00 £80.00 £12.50 £92.50 £10.00 £90.00

Whichever way the restaurant did it, split the service in proportion. If service was on the full price, each person pays 12.5% of their own full price order. If it was on the discounted price, each pays 12.5% of their discounted share.

A discretionary service charge is optional in the UK, so if you think it was worked out on the wrong total, you can ask about it politely. More on that in is service charge mandatory in the UK?

What if the discount only covers food?

Then only the food gets it. If a deal is 50% off food, someone who mostly drank gets a smaller share of the saving, and that's correct. Split the food discount in proportion to each person's food, then add their drinks at full price.

The same goes for any deal with exclusions: set menus, mains only, weekdays only. Spread the saving only across the items it actually came off.

What to say at the table

Most of the awkwardness comes from nobody saying which kind of saving it is. These help.

When it's your voucher and you want to share it:

I've got a £20 voucher, let's put it on the whole bill and split it by what we each had.

When it's your voucher and you'd like to keep it:

I'm using my gift card for my bit. Everyone else just pays for what they had.

When the group booked because of a deal:

The 2 for 1 is for the whole table, so let's share the saving rather than whoever got the cheaper main eating for free.

When someone has a personal discount:

Your student discount is yours, don't worry about sharing it.

Can an app work it out?

Yes, and this is where hand-worked splits most often go wrong. Discounts and service charges spread in proportion produce lots of fractions of a penny, and if each person rounds their own share, the total rarely matches the bill. The trick is to work out each person's exact share and round once at the end. There's more on that in how to split service charge and tips fairly.

Questions people ask

Should a voucher be split equally or by what people ordered?

Either can be fair if you agree first. In proportion is the fairest, because everyone gets the same percentage off. Equally is simpler and gives lighter eaters a little more of the saving.

Who gets the saving on a 2 for 1 meal?

Everyone who had a main in the deal, ideally in proportion to the price of their main. Otherwise the person who ordered the cheaper dish eats for free, which is down to luck.

Is service charge worked out before or after a discount?

It depends on the restaurant, which can set it either way on most till systems. Look at the receipt to see which total the percentage was applied to.

If I pay with a gift card, does everyone get a share?

No. A gift card is money, not a discount. Using it is the same as paying part of your share with your own card.

Do I have to share my student discount?

No. A personal discount is yours. If it's only valid on your own order, it can only come off your share anyway.
