How to split a bill for a large group (10 to 30 people)

A calm method for splitting a big group bill: set menus, deposits, drinks, service charge and one card. Worked £ example for 18 people, plus messages to copy.

Kyle, founder of OnlyBills17 October 2026 · 6 min read

The calm way to split a bill for a big group is to decide the method before you book: a set menu or fixed price for food, drinks paid by what each person had, the service charge spread in proportion, and one person paying the whole bill while everyone pays them back that night. Most of the stress with 10 to 30 people comes from making these decisions at the table, at 11pm, with a £1,000 bill.

Settling up takes long enough with a normal-sized group. A 2021 comparethemarket.com survey of 2,000 diners found people had waited an average of 6 minutes 45 seconds to settle a bill, and 29% had had to apologise for how long it was taking (comparethemarket, 2021). With 20 people at one table, it can take a lot longer.

Before you book

Most of the work happens here. Get these right and the bill sorts itself out.

Choose somewhere that does groups

Ask when you book:

  • Is there a set menu or a group menu for this size? Many restaurants ask big groups to choose one.
  • Is there a deposit? Often per head, taken when you book and taken off the final bill.
  • Is there a service charge? For large groups it's commonly added automatically. Ask what percentage.
  • Can you split the bill, and how? Some restaurants won't split between more than a few cards for big tables. Better to know now. (More on this in can you ask a restaurant to split the bill?)
  • Is there a minimum spend? Common for private rooms.

Tell people the rough cost in the invite

People can only say yes or no fairly if they know what they're agreeing to. Put the numbers in the first message.

Booked the Italian place for Saturday 7.30pm, 18 of us 🎉 It's a set menu at £35 a head, drinks extra, 12.5% service added. They want a £10 deposit each, so please send me £10 by Wednesday: [link]. Roughly £55 to £70 each with a couple of drinks.

Collect deposits before you pay them

If the restaurant wants a deposit, collect it from everyone before you pay it on your card. Only count people as booked once they've paid. It's the simplest way to stop no-shows costing the organiser money.

Set menus make big groups much easier

A set menu or "feasting menu" means everyone's food costs the same. That leaves only drinks to work out per person, which is a much smaller problem.

If someone can't eat from the set menu, the restaurant can usually swap a dish. Ask at booking.

Ordering on the night

  • Order drinks in a way you can track. Bottles for the table are fine if you note who's sharing which. Individual drinks are easier if people order their own.
  • Watch the "one more bottle" effect. When people know the bill will be split equally, groups tend to order more. A small 2004 restaurant study found diners spent about 51 shekels each when splitting equally against about 37 when paying for their own, about 36% more (Gneezy, Haruvy and Yafe, 2004). It was six-person groups of students in Israel, but the effect is familiar. More on why groups order more when splitting.
  • Ask for one bill. It's faster for staff, and you'll split it properly afterwards.

When the bill arrives

Step 1: check it

Big bills are where mistakes hide. Look for:

  • Items you didn't have (an extra bottle, a dessert nobody ordered).
  • The deposit. Has it been taken off?
  • The service charge. Is it the percentage you were told?

Step 2: one person pays

With a large group, one card is quicker and kinder than 20 cards at the table. Someone with a good credit card limit pays, and everyone pays them back before they leave or that evening.

If nobody wants to take on a £1,000 bill, ask the restaurant at booking if you can split it into a few chunks, for example one card per table of six.

Step 3: work out each person's share

The fair method for a big group:

  1. Food: the set menu price each (or each person's own dishes).
  2. Drinks: what each person had. Shared bottles split between the people who drank them (see splitting the bill when some people don't drink).
  3. Service charge: spread in proportion to each person's food and drinks.
  4. Deposit: taken off each person's share.

Worked example: 18 people, set menu

18 people on a £35 set menu, drinks extra, with 12.5% service. Everyone paid a £10 deposit.

Bill Amount
Food: 18 × £35 £630.00
Drinks £324.00
Subtotal £954.00
Service charge 12.5% £119.25
Total £1,073.25
Less deposits paid (18 × £10) £180.00
Left to pay on the night £893.25

If you split the whole thing equally, everyone owes about £59.63 in total (nine people pay £59.63 and nine pay £59.62, so it comes to exactly £1,073.25), or about £49.63 after their deposit. But the drinks weren't equal. Here's what three people pay when you split by what they had:

Food Drinks With service Less deposit Pays on the night
Lily (soft drinks) £35.00 £8.00 £48.38 £10.00 £38.38
Jamal (two glasses of wine) £35.00 £15.00 £56.25 £10.00 £46.25
Rob (cocktails and a bottle share) £35.00 £38.00 £82.13 £10.00 £72.13

Split equally, Lily would pay £11.25 more than her own share, and Rob about £22.50 less. Across 18 people, that adds up to a lot of quiet resentment.

If there's a penny out at the end from rounding, the person who paid absorbs it. Don't hold up 18 people over 2p.

Questions people ask

How do you split a bill for a large group?

Agree the method before booking: set menu for food, drinks by what each person had, service charge in proportion, deposits off each share. One person pays and everyone pays them back that night.

Will a restaurant split a bill for 20 people?

Some will, but many limit how many cards they'll take for a big table, especially at busy times. Ask when you book. One card and settling up afterwards is usually quicker.

Is service charge automatic for large groups?

Many UK restaurants add a service charge automatically for larger tables. The group size it starts at varies by restaurant. It should be shown on the menu or the bill, and a discretionary service charge is still optional. Ask when you book so it's in the invite. Our guide to whether service charge is mandatory explains the difference.

How do you collect money from a large group?

Ask for deposits by bank transfer or a payment link before you book, set a deadline, and only count people as booked once they've paid. On the night, have everyone pay their share before they leave.
