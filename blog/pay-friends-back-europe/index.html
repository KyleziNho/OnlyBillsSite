How friends pay each other back in Europe, country by country

Spain uses Bizum, Sweden Swish, the Netherlands Tikkie. What locals use in each European country, and what works if you're visiting without a local bank.

Most European countries have their own way for friends to pay each other: Bizum in Spain, MB WAY in Portugal, Tikkie in the Netherlands, Swish in Sweden, Wero in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg. They nearly all need a local bank account, so if you're visiting, a euro bank transfer to an IBAN, a Revolut link or PayPal is usually what works.

Here's what people use in each country, what a visitor can use instead, and how to pay a friend from another country.

The short version

The pattern: the local app is the easiest way to pay a local, but only if you have a local bank account. Everyone else falls back to IBAN, Revolut or PayPal.

Spain: Bizum

Bizum is built into Spanish banking apps. You send money to a friend's mobile number and it lands in their account straight away. Both people need a Spanish bank that offers Bizum. Limits are set by each bank, so check your app if you're paying for a big group dinner.

Portugal: MB WAY

MB WAY works the same way in Portugal: send to a phone number from the MB WAY app or your bank. It needs a Portuguese bank card or account.

Netherlands: Tikkie and iDEAL, becoming Wero

The Dutch split bills with Tikkie, ABN AMRO's payment request app. Someone sends a link, you tap it and pay through iDEAL in your own banking app. iDEAL is being moved over to Wero in stages, with the Dutch banks aiming to finish by the end of 2027. Paying a Tikkie usually needs a bank that supports iDEAL. Full details in Tikkie, Wero and iDEAL explained.

Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg: Wero

Wero is the European Payments Initiative's app for paying someone by phone number or email. It's live for person to person payments in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg, where it replaced Payconiq in September 2026. EPI says it has about 58 million users. In Germany, many people still use PayPal or a plain bank transfer between friends, so ask which your friend prefers.

Italy

There's no single dominant app. Friends commonly pay by bank transfer, PayPal, or apps such as Satispay. An instant euro transfer to an IBAN works from any euro account.

The Nordics: Swish, Vipps and MobilePay

Sweden uses Swish, Norway uses Vipps, and Denmark and Finland use MobilePay. Vipps and MobilePay are now one company but still separate apps in each country. Swish needs a Swedish bank account and Mobile BankID, which needs a Swedish personal identity number, so most visitors can't use it. More in splitting a bill with Swish, Vipps or MobilePay.

Switzerland, Poland and Greece

  • Switzerland: TWINT, linked to a Swiss bank account. Payments in Swiss francs.
  • Poland: BLIK, which many Polish banking apps use to send money to a phone number. Payments in złoty.
  • Greece: IRIS, a phone number payment service inside Greek banking apps.

Everywhere else, and between countries

If your friend banks in another country, or nobody shares a local app, three options work almost everywhere.

IBAN transfer. Every euro account has an IBAN. Within the euro area, instant transfers arrive within 10 seconds and can't cost more than a standard euro transfer. Since October 2025, your bank also checks the name against the account before you pay. See how to pay a friend back by IBAN.

Revolut. Popular with students and expats across Europe. Friends without Revolut can pay a Revolut payment link by card or Apple Pay.

PayPal. Widely used, especially in Germany. Your friend may need a PayPal account, and sending in a different currency involves PayPal's conversion.

If you're visiting: what works without a local bank

  1. Ask for an IBAN. Your friend almost certainly has one. Paying a euro IBAN from a UK bank is possible but can carry fees and a weak exchange rate. A Revolut or Wise account usually costs less.
  2. Pay their Revolut link by card or Apple Pay.
  3. Use PayPal if you both have it.
  4. Cash still works for small amounts.

Don't expect a phone number app to accept you. Bizum, MB WAY, Swish, Vipps, MobilePay, TWINT and BLIK all need a local bank account.

Are the national apps going to work across borders?

Partly, and slowly. In February 2026, Bizum, Bancomat, SIBS (MB WAY), Vipps MobilePay and EPI (Wero) agreed to connect their apps, starting with payments between people in different countries. No go-live date had been published when we checked, so don't count on it for this trip.

Questions people ask

What is Bizum?

Bizum is Spain's phone number payment system, built into Spanish banking apps. You send money to someone's mobile number and it arrives straight away. Both people need a Spanish bank account with Bizum switched on.

Can a tourist use Bizum?

Not without a Spanish bank account. If you're visiting, pay a Spanish friend by bank transfer to their IBAN, through Revolut or PayPal, or in cash.

What's the European equivalent of Venmo?

There isn't one app. Each country has its own (Bizum, MB WAY, Swish, Vipps, MobilePay, TWINT, BLIK), and Wero works in Belgium, France, Germany and Luxembourg. Across borders, people use IBAN transfers, Revolut or PayPal.

How fast is a SEPA transfer now?

Euro instant transfers arrive within 10 seconds, at any time of day. Since October 2025, banks in the euro area must be able to send them and can't charge more than for a standard transfer.

Does Revolut work for friends without Revolut?

Yes for payment links: they can be paid by card or Apple Pay without a Revolut account. A transfer to someone's Revolut IBAN also works from any euro bank account.
