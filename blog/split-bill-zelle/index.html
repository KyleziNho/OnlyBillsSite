Can you split a bill with Zelle?

Zelle has no split button of its own, but some banks add one. How to split with Chase or U.S. Bank, request vs send, and why there's no Zelle link.

Sometimes. Zelle has no split button of its own, but some banks add one to their Zelle screens: Chase lets you split a charge and include your own portion, and U.S. Bank lets you split a bill with up to 10 people. If your bank doesn't, you can still send each friend a separate request, or simply ask everyone to send you their share.

Zelle lives inside more than 2,400 bank and credit union apps, so the buttons and wording depend on your bank. Here's how the main split options work, why sending is often easier than requesting, and what Zelle can't do. We checked each bank's help page on 14 November 2026.

How splitting works with Zelle, by bank

Bank Split option How many people Notes
Chase Split on a charge, or Request/Split with Zelle Not stated Tap "Include my portion" and Chase works out your share
U.S. Bank Split bill, in Send & request money with Zelle Up to 10 Starts even; edit any share and the rest adjust
Other banks Varies Varies Look for "Request" or "Split" on your bank's Zelle screen

Chase and U.S. Bank are examples, not the only ones. If you don't see a split option, use one request per person (below).

Splitting with Chase

  1. In the Chase Mobile app, open the checking account or credit card with the charge.
  2. Swipe left on the charge and tap Split.
  3. Choose contacts or add new ones, then tap Next.
  4. Tap Include my portion and Chase calculates your share.
  5. Tap Request money and confirm.

To request a specific amount instead, go to Pay and Transfer, then Request/Split with Zelle®.

Splitting with U.S. Bank

  1. Go to Transfer & pay, then Send & request money with Zelle®, and choose the split bill option.
  2. Pick up to 10 people. Each needs a Zelle-enrolled phone number or email.
  3. Enter the whole bill, including your share. The amounts start even, and you can edit anyone's share.
  4. Choose the account to receive the money, review and submit.

U.S. Bank notes that the total you type isn't taken from your account. It's only used to work out everyone else's share.

Request or send: which is easier for friends?

Bank split tools create requests. Zelle says requests can only go to people who are already enrolled, and each friend then has to find and approve the request in their own bank app.

Often it's simpler to skip the request. Tell each friend their amount and your Zelle phone number or email, and they send it. Sending works to anyone enrolled, and if the person you're paying isn't enrolled yet, they get a message to enroll. Unclaimed payments expire after 14 days and the money goes back.

Either way, work out the uneven amounts first. If people ordered different things, use the tax and tip formula so nobody tips on someone else's steak.

There's no Zelle payment link

Venmo, Cash App and PayPal all have links that open a profile or a payment page. Zelle doesn't. There's no web address you can text a friend that opens their bank app with your details and the amount filled in.

The standalone Zelle app also stopped sending and receiving money in April 2025. Zelle now lives only inside bank and credit union apps, so "download Zelle" isn't an option for a friend whose bank doesn't offer it.

In practice, paying you by Zelle means your friend:

  1. Opens their own bank app and finds Zelle.
  2. Types or pastes your phone number or email.
  3. Types the amount and sends.

Some bank apps can show or scan a Zelle QR code, which saves typing, but only inside bank apps that support it.

Safety: who you should Zelle

Zelle is built for paying people you know. Its own guidance is to use it only with people you trust, and it offers no purchase protection. Once money reaches an enrolled person, it usually can't be cancelled.

For splitting dinner with friends, that's fine. A few habits help:

  • Check the name. Many bank apps show the enrolled name before you confirm. Make sure it's your friend.
  • Copy, don't retype. Pasting the number avoids sending to a stranger with one digit different.
  • Be wary of "requests" from people you don't know. A request from a stranger, or a message saying you must send money to receive money, is a common scam pattern. Ignore it.

Questions people ask

Can you split a bill on Zelle?

Zelle itself has no split button, but some banks build one into their Zelle screens. Chase has "Request/Split with Zelle" and U.S. Bank lets you split with up to 10 people. If your bank doesn't, send a separate request to each person, or ask each friend to send you their share.

Can I send a Zelle request to someone who isn't enrolled?

Zelle says requests can only go to people who are already enrolled. Some banks let you send a request by email, and the friend has to enroll before they can pay it.

Is there a Zelle link I can send to friends?

No. Zelle doesn't have a payment link that opens a friend's bank app with your details filled in. Friends need your enrolled phone number or email, which they type or paste into Zelle in their own bank app.

Is Zelle free?

Zelle doesn't charge people to send or receive money. Your bank sets its own fees, and Zelle says almost all linked checking and savings accounts charge nothing. Check with your bank if you're unsure.

Is it safe to Zelle a friend?

Yes, for people you know and trust. Once the money reaches an enrolled person it usually can't be cancelled, and Zelle offers no purchase protection, so double-check the number or email before you send.
