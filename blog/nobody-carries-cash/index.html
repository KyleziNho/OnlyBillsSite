Nobody carries cash any more. So who pays?

Cash was 8% of UK payments in 2025, down from 45% in 2015. What that did to splitting the bill, and what replaced the pile of notes on the table.

Kyle, founder of OnlyBills20 October 2026 · 6 min read

Cash made up just 8% of UK payments in 2025, according to UK Finance's latest UK Payment Markets report. Ten years earlier it was 45%. That shift changed how groups split a restaurant bill: the pile of notes in the middle of the table has been replaced by one person paying on their card and everyone else paying them back by bank transfer or payment link.

Here's what the numbers say, and what it means for the next time the bill arrives.

How fast did cash disappear?

UK Finance publishes the main yearly count of how people in the UK pay. Its 2026 report, covering 2025, counted 49.7 billion payments. Cash accounted for 3.9 billion of them, down from 4.3 billion the year before.

Year Cash share of all UK payments
2015 45%
2020 17%
2024 9%
2025 8%
2035 (forecast) 4%

Since 2024, cash hasn't even been the second most used way to pay. UK Finance says it fell behind Direct Debits, Faster Payments and credit cards, and is now the fifth most used payment method.

Cash isn't gone, though. 49.3 million people used a cash machine in 2025, and UK Finance expects cash to keep declining "but not disappear", because for some people it matters for budgeting, accessibility or personal preference.

What replaced it?

Mostly the debit card, and mostly by tapping it.

  • Debit cards were used for 26.6 billion payments in 2025, 54% of all UK payments.
  • Contactless (cards and phones) made up 39% of all payments, up from 3% in 2015.
  • Mobile wallets: 65% of UK adults were registered for at least one mobile payment service like Apple Pay or Google Pay in 2025, up from 57% in 2024.
  • Faster Payments and other remote banking reached 6.2 billion payments, up 10% on the year, making it the second most used method after debit cards. 75% of UK adults used mobile banking.

That last number is the one that matters for splitting the bill.

6.2bn

Faster Payments and other remote banking payments in 2025, up 10%, partly from people splitting bills and paying friends back

UK Finance, 2026

UK Finance's press release credits part of the long-term growth to "people using Faster Payments to split bills and send money to friends and family", replacing payments that used to be made in cash. The money that used to go in a pile on the table now goes from banking app to banking app.

How splitting the bill used to work

With cash, the method was rough but quick. The bill came, someone read out the total, everyone threw in notes, and whoever was sitting nearest counted it. If it was short, someone added a fiver. If it was over, the extra became the tip. Nobody worked out who had the extra glass of wine. It was approximate, it was done in a couple of minutes, and nobody owed anybody anything when they walked out.

It had problems: someone always only had a £20, someone else had no cash at all, and the person who counted it often quietly covered the shortfall. But the debt was settled at the table.

How splitting the bill works now

Without cash, a group has four options, and each has a cost.

How the group pays What happens The catch
Everyone pays separately at the till The server runs the card machine for each person Slow for big groups, and someone still has to work out each amount. Not every restaurant will do it
One person pays, split it equally One card, then everyone sends the same amount Fast, but light eaters and non-drinkers overpay
One person pays, everyone works out their own share One card, then each person adds up their items Accurate, slow, and the shares rarely add up to the bill
One person pays, an app splits it Scan the receipt, people claim their items, the app works out each share Someone has to have the app, and friends still have to pay

Whichever you pick, the big change from the cash days is that one person ends up being owed money when they leave. That's new, and it's where the awkwardness lives.

The IOU problem

When the debt is settled at the table, nobody has to chase anyone. When it isn't, someone does.

Starling Bank and Opinium, 2023

A 2021 comparethemarket.com survey of 2,000 diners found 43% feel awkward working out how to pay when eating out, and the average respondent had waited 6 minutes 45 seconds to settle a bill. Diners were split almost evenly on method: 34% thought you should split the bill straight down the middle, 36% that it should be split by who had what.

(Both are surveys run by brands with an interest in the topic: a bank promoting its payment links, and a comparison site. Treat the numbers as rough guides.)

What makes a cashless split work

The cash method had one big advantage: the debt was settled before anyone left. The best modern methods try to get that back.

Settle before you leave the table. Send the numbers and a payment link while everyone is still there. It's much easier than chasing three days later.

Send an exact number, not "roughly £30". People pay faster when they can see what the number is for. "£23.40 (your pasta, half the wine and service)" beats "can you send me some money for dinner?".

Make it work with any bank. Not everyone banks with Monzo. A payment link that takes a card, or your sort code and account number, works for everyone.

Make the shares add up. If four people each round up to the nearest pound, the payer gets a little extra. If they each round down, the payer is a few quid short. The amounts should add up to the bill exactly. The free split the bill calculator does this by item, service charge included.

Questions people ask

What percentage of UK payments are cash?

8% in 2025, according to UK Finance's UK Payment Markets 2026 report. That's 3.9 billion cash payments out of 49.7 billion in total. In 2015 it was 45%.

Will the UK become cashless?

UK Finance doesn't expect it to. It forecasts cash will fall to around 4% of payments by 2035, about 2 billion payments, but "not disappear".

How do people split a bill without cash?

Usually one person pays by card and everyone else pays them back by bank transfer or a payment link from Monzo, Revolut or Starling (step by step in how to split a bill with Monzo, Revolut and Starling). Some restaurants will split the card payment at the table, and apps can work out each person's share from the receipt.

Can you ask a restaurant to split the bill?

Yes, many will take separate card payments, though it's slower for big groups and not every restaurant will do it. See can you ask a restaurant to split the bill?.
