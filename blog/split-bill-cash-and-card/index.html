How to split a bill when some pay cash and some pay card

Some friends have cash, some only cards. How to settle one bill so nobody loses out: who takes the cash, the change, card minimums and a worked £ example.

Work out everyone's share first, then decide how each person pays. The simplest fair method is for one person to pay the bill by card and the cash payers to hand their cash to that person, so they're paid back on the spot. If the cash goes to the till instead, sort out the change before anyone's card goes in, and tell the server one clear amount for each card.

Mixing cash and card is less common than it was. Cash was 8% of UK payments in 2025, down from 9% the year before (UK Finance, 2026). But most groups still have one friend with a twenty in their wallet, and that's where the pennies go missing. Here's how to stop that.

(For how cash disappeared and what replaced it, see nobody carries cash any more. This page is about the practical bit: one bill, mixed payments.)

Why does mixing cash and card go wrong?

Because people pay in notes, and shares come in pennies.

If your share is £24.85 and you have a £20 and a £5, you put down £25. Do that across a few people and the pile is a few pounds over. Then someone takes "change" from the pile, someone else puts in a tenner and takes a fiver, and by the time the last person taps their card, nobody knows what's been covered. Usually the person who pays last, or the person who paid by card for everyone, ends up short.

The fix is to keep two things apart: how much each person owes, and how each person pays.

Step 1: work out each person's share

Do this exactly as you would if everyone were paying by card. Split by item, evenly, or food evenly and drinks separately, with the service charge in proportion. (If you're not sure which, see how to split a bill fairly.)

Five friends have dinner. The bill is £137.40 including service. Their shares:

Person Share Paying by
Amira £31.20 Card
Ben £24.85 Cash
Chloe £28.60 Card
Dan £22.15 Cash
Eve £30.60 Card
Total £137.40

Ben has a £20 and a £5. Dan has a £20 and a £5. Between them they have £50 in notes, but they only owe £47.00.

Step 2: pick one of two ways to pay

Way 1: cash goes on the bill at the till

Hand the server the cash with a clear instruction, then put fixed amounts on the cards.

Here's £50 in cash. Can we have £3 change, please? Then £31.20 on this card, £28.60 on this one, and the rest on the last one.

The till takes £47.00 in cash. The cards cover £31.20 + £28.60 + £30.60 = £90.40. Total £137.40. Ben takes 15p of the change and Dan takes £2.85.

"The rest on the last card" is the useful part. It means the final card absorbs any penny you've miscounted, rather than the server having to work it out. (There's more on asking for split payments in can you ask a restaurant to split the bill?)

Good for: small groups, quieter times, when the cash payers have something close to the right amount.

Way 2: one person pays by card, and the cash goes to them

Amira pays the whole £137.40 on her card. Ben and Dan give her their cash, and she gives back the change. Chloe and Eve pay her by bank transfer or a payment link.

Amount
Amira pays the restaurant £137.40
Ben gives Amira £25, gets back 15p £24.85
Dan gives Amira £25, gets back £2.85 £22.15
Chloe sends Amira £28.60
Eve sends Amira £30.60
Amira gets back £106.20

£137.40 minus her own £31.20 is £106.20, so she's exactly square.

Good for: big groups, busy restaurants, or when the server doesn't want five payments.

If Amira hasn't got change, the simplest fix is for her to keep the £50 and send Ben 15p and Dan £2.85 by transfer. Or Ben and Dan can say the change is a tip, as long as everyone knows.

Who should take the cash?

Whoever is owed money. In Way 2 that's the person who paid by card. It means the cash payers have settled before they leave, which is the whole point of cash.

A few things to check first:

  • Do they want it? Some people genuinely don't use cash. If the card payer would rather not carry notes home, the cash payers could put their cash on the bill at the till instead (Way 1), or pay by bank transfer like everyone else.
  • Can they pay it in? Many UK banks let you pay cash into your account at a Post Office counter. The Post Office keeps a list of which banks are covered (Post Office).
  • Is the pile counted? Before anyone takes change, count the cash against the shares. It takes a minute and saves the argument.

Can someone pay their share in cash and the rest go on one card?

Yes. This is a mix of the two ways, and it's common. The cash payers put their exact share on the table, and one person pays the rest on card.

From the example: £47.00 cash goes to the server, and Amira pays the remaining £90.40 on her card. Chloe and Eve then owe Amira £28.60 and £30.60. That keeps the number of card payments to one, and the cash payers are done before they leave.

What about card minimums?

Some smaller pubs and cafés have a minimum spend for card payments. If several people want to pay small amounts by card, put them together on one card and have the others pay that person back.

A minimum is different from a card fee. A business can't charge you extra for paying by a consumer debit or credit card, according to Business Companion, the Trading Standards guidance service (Business Companion).

What about the tip if some pay cash?

If the bill already has a service charge, nobody needs to add more (see should you tip on top of a service charge?).

If you're tipping and some people pay cash, the cash payers can leave their part as cash. A cash tip a worker receives and keeps, with no involvement from their employer, is outside the UK's tipping law and its code of practice (GOV.UK code of practice, paragraph 13). In plain terms, the server keeps it. Card tips and service charges go to staff under the law, shared through the venue's tipping policy. More on that in who gets the tip?

Just make sure everyone agrees on the tip amount first. Otherwise the cash payers round up to the nearest note and the card payers add 10%, and the tip ends up bigger than anyone intended.

Messages you can copy

In the group chat beforehand:

I'll put dinner on my card. If you're paying cash, give it to me on the night. Everyone else, I'll send your number after.

At the table:

Ben, yours is £24.85. Dan, £22.15. Cash to me and I'll sort change. Chloe and Eve, I'll send you a link.

After, for anyone paying by transfer:

Dinner came to £137.40. Yours is £28.60 (your share plus service). Here's my link: [link]. Thanks!

Questions people ask

Should the person who pays by card take the cash?

Usually, yes. They're the one who's out of pocket, so cash going straight to them settles it at the table. If they'd rather not hold cash, the cash can go on the bill at the till instead.

How do you split a bill when someone only has cash?

Work out their exact share first. They give their cash to whoever paid by card, or put it on the bill at the till, and any change is sorted before the cards go in.

Can a restaurant take part cash and part card?

Many will. Give them the cash first with a clear amount, then tell them what to put on each card, with "the rest" on the last one.

Can a pub charge a fee for paying by card?

Not on a consumer debit or credit card. Some pubs set a minimum spend for card payments instead, which isn't the same thing as a fee.
