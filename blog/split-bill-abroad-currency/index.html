Splitting a bill abroad: which currency should friends pay you back in?

Friends should pay you back in your own currency, at a rate you all agree at the table. Worked examples, card fees and which exchange rate is fair.

Friends should pay you back in your own currency, at a rate you all agree before anyone pays. The fairest rate is either a public reference rate on the day or the rate your card actually charged you, and once it's agreed it shouldn't change when the market moves.

Below is a worked example showing how much the choice of rate matters, the card machine trap to avoid, and a message you can send to the group.

Why the exchange rate matters

Say four of you have dinner in Bangkok and the bill is ฿2,400. You pay on your card. Everyone had about the same, so each share is ฿600. How much is that in pounds? It depends on which rate you use.

Rate used £1 buys Each friend's share Whole bill
Public reference rate ฿43.80 £13.70 £54.79
Rate on your card statement (with a fee) ฿43.10 £13.92 £55.68
Paying in pounds at the machine ฿41.00 £14.63 £58.54

Example rates for illustration. Real rates change daily.

The gap between the first two rows is under a pound on the whole bill. The third row costs the table about £3.75 more than the reference rate, for nothing.

The maths is the same in any currency. An American who paid a €24 lunch in Paris at €1 = $1.0823 asks for $25.98 in total.

Always pay in the local currency at the machine

When a card machine abroad asks whether you want to pay in pounds or baht, choose baht. Choosing pounds is called dynamic currency conversion: the shop's payment provider picks the exchange rate, and Monzo, for example, advises paying in the local currency because your card's own rate is usually better.

The same applies at cash machines. If the screen offers to "guarantee" your rate in pounds, decline it.

Card fees: what did the payer actually pay?

Before you pick a rate, check what it cost the person who paid. Some UK debit cards charge a fee on every foreign transaction. Barclays, for example, charges a 2.99% non-sterling transaction fee on debit card spending abroad. Monzo and Starling say they don't charge a fee on card payments abroad and use Mastercard's rate.

If the payer used a card with a fee, a reference rate leaves them slightly out of pocket. On one dinner it's small. Across a week of paying for the group, it adds up. More on card fees in how to split costs on a group holiday.

Three fair ways to settle

1. A public reference rate on the day. Look up the rate for the date of the meal and use it for everyone. It's neutral and easy to check. The payer absorbs any card fee, so this suits a payer with a fee free card.

2. The payer's real rate. Divide the pound amount on the payer's statement by the bill total. ฿2,400 that cost £55.68 means £1 = ฿43.10. Nobody loses money for paying. Card payments can show as pending for a day or two before the final amount appears, so use the settled figure if you can wait, or agree to use the pending one.

3. Settle in the local currency. If friends have baht left over or a multi-currency account, they can pay you in baht, and nobody converts anything. This works best on the trip itself, before people head home.

Pick one and tell the table which. The rate matters less than everyone knowing it up front.

Freeze the rate so nobody argues later

Exchange rates move every day. If you wait a fortnight to settle, someone will notice that the pound has moved and ask to use the new rate. Avoid it by writing the rate down when you split the bill: "£1 = ฿43.80, rate from 13 Nov". Whoever pays later pays the same amount as whoever paid that night.

If the group is splitting a whole trip in an expense app, the same rule applies. Splitwise Pro converts currencies, and Tricount and Settle Up both let you log expenses in more than one currency. Check which rate the app uses and when it applies it.

Tipping and service abroad

Service charges and tipping norms vary a lot by country, and they change what the fair split is. Italy's coperto is charged per person, while Brazil's 10% service is optional. See tipping and service charge by country.

Questions people ask

Should I pay in pounds or the local currency abroad?

The local currency. If you choose pounds at the card machine, the shop's provider sets the exchange rate, and it's usually worse than your card's own rate.

Which exchange rate is fair when splitting a bill abroad?

Either a public reference rate on the day, or the rate the payer's card actually charged them. Both are fair. What matters is agreeing one at the table and not changing it later.

Can friends pay me back in my own currency?

Yes. Convert each person's share at the agreed rate and ask for that amount in your currency. Friends from other countries just pay that amount, and their own bank converts it.

What if my card gave me a worse rate than the reference rate?

Use your card's rate instead, from your banking app or statement. It's fair that you don't lose money for paying on everyone's behalf. Say so before anyone pays.

Who pays the card's foreign transaction fee?

If the payer's card charges a fee, most groups share it by using the payer's real rate, which includes the fee. If someone in the group has a fee free card, let them pay next time.
