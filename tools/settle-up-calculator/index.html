Who owes who calculator

Several people paid for different things? Enter what each person paid and get the fewest payments to settle up.

Who paid what

Put £0 for anyone who didn’t pay for anything. Costs are shared equally between everyone listed.

How settling up works

  1. Add up everything that was spent. Four friends spent £240 between them on a weekend away.
  2. Work out each person's fair share. £240 ÷ 4 = £60 each.
  3. Compare what each person paid with their share. Sam paid £120, so he's owed £60. Maya paid £75, so she's owed £15. Priya paid £45, so she owes £15. Jon paid nothing, so he owes £60.
  4. Settle with as few payments as possible. Jon pays Sam £60 and Priya pays Maya £15. Two transfers and everyone is square.
Person Paid Fair share Balance
Sam £120 £60 owed £60
Maya £75 £60 owed £15
Priya £45 £60 owes £15
Jon £0 £60 owes £60

When the total doesn't divide exactly, a penny or two goes to some people so the shares still add up to the total.

Restaurant bills are different

Settling up works for a trip where costs are shared evenly. A restaurant bill is usually fairer split by what each person ordered, with any service charge in proportion. For that, use the split the bill calculator, or let friends tick their own items on their phone with OnlyBills. There's more on group trips in splitting costs on holiday.

Questions people ask

How do you work out who owes who?

Add up the total spent, divide by the number of people for each person's share, then compare what each person paid with their share. People who paid more than their share are owed money; people who paid less owe it.

What's the fewest number of payments to settle up?

Usually one fewer than the number of people at most. The calculator pairs the person who owes the most with the person owed the most, again and again, which keeps the number of transfers low.

Can someone pay a smaller share?

This calculator shares costs equally. For a bill where people had different things, split it by item instead with the split the bill calculator.
