How we work out your number

Your estimated overpay is:

meals a year × spend per head × how much less you order × how often it's split equally

If you eat out with friends 3 times a month at £30 a head, order about 15% less than the table, and the bill is split equally 3 times out of 4, that's 36 × £30 × 15% × 75% = about £122 a year. Not drinking adds to the gap, because drinks are where a table's spending varies most.

The percentages are our assumptions, not research figures, which is why we call it your estimated overpay. If you usually order more than everyone else, splitting equally probably saves you money, and your friends are the ones paying for it.

Questions people ask

Do you lose money splitting the bill equally?

If you usually order less than the people you eat with, yes. In a 2016 OpenTable survey, 38% of Brits felt they lose money when the bill is split equally, by about £8.73 a meal on their own estimate.

Why do people order more when the bill is split?

Because each person only pays a fraction of anything extra they order. In a study by Gneezy, Haruvy and Yafe (2004), diners who split the bill ordered noticeably more than diners who paid individually.

How can I avoid overpaying without being awkward?

Suggest splitting by item before the bill comes, or use an app where everyone ticks their own items so nobody has to argue over the receipt. See our bill splitting etiquette guide.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's an estimate. The gap percentages are our assumptions and your real number depends on what your friends order. Use it as a rough guide, not a precise figure.
