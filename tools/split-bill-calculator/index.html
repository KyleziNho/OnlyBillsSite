How this calculator splits a bill

  1. Each item goes to the people who had it. One person, one price. Tap three people on a bottle of wine and each pays a third.
  2. Service, tips and discounts follow what you ordered. A 12.5% service charge on a £60 bill is £7.50. If you had £30 of it, you pay half the service, £3.75. The person who had a £9 salad pays service on £9.
  3. The pennies come out right. £10 split three ways is £3.34, £3.33 and £3.33, not three lots of £3.33 that leave a penny on the table. It uses the same money engine as the OnlyBills app.

A worked example

Four friends at an Indian restaurant. The bill is £71.20 of food and drink plus 12.5% service (£8.90), so £80.10 in total.

Person Had Food and drink Share of service Pays
Sam Chicken ruby, lassi £18.40 £2.30 £20.70
Priya Paneer tikka, a third of the naan and rice £16.60 £2.08 £18.68
Jon Lamb biryani, two beers, a third of the naan and rice £25.80 £3.22 £29.02
Maya Salad, a third of the naan and rice £10.40 £1.30 £11.70
Total £71.20 £8.90 £80.10

Split equally, everyone would pay £20.03. Maya would hand over £8.33 more than she ate. Jon would pay £8.99 less. That gap is why 38% of people say they lose out when a bill is split evenly.

When splitting equally is fine

If everyone ordered roughly the same, splitting equally is quick and nobody minds. As a rule of thumb, if the most and least anyone spent are within about £5 of each other, just split it. If someone didn't drink, or one person had the tasting menu, split by item. There's more on this in is splitting the bill equally fair?

Questions people ask

How do you split a bill with service charge?

Add up what each person had, then give each person the same percentage on top. With 12.5% service, someone who had £20 of food pays £22.50. That way service follows what you ordered.

How do you split a shared starter or bottle of wine?

Divide it between only the people who shared it. A £24 bottle shared by three people is £8 each, and the people who didn't drink pay nothing towards it.

Why don't the shares divide exactly?

Some amounts can't be split into equal pennies. £10 three ways is £3.33 and a third. The calculator gives the spare penny to one person so the shares add up to the bill exactly.

Is service charge compulsory in the UK?

No. A discretionary service charge is optional, even when it's already printed on the bill. You can ask for it to be removed if the service was poor. Read more in is service charge mandatory in the UK?

Can my friends enter their own items?

Not in this calculator, but they can in the OnlyBills app. You scan the receipt, share a QR code, and everyone ticks what they had on their own phone. No app download needed for them.
